## §3 — WHAT WE MOST WANT LOOKED AT, RANKED

**1. Book V as a whole, and V.11 in particular, for the thing V.11 just found out about itself.**
Ruling 164: the book had been arguing a third position in a two-position debate (perennialist vs.
constructivist) and had named neither position's owner in forty-two chapters. V.11 names them and
makes a narrow cut on Katz. **The concession to Katz is the most expensive sentence in Book V and it
is made from general knowledge with no primary text in the repository.** If the cut is wrong, or if
the concession is either too large or not large enough, that is the highest-value finding available
in this packet.

**2. Whether the honest-ledger half of V.11 is honest.** Four places where an older road is *ahead*
of us. A reader who thinks any of the four is flattery, or who knows a fifth we missed, should say
so — this is the chapter's own claim to being two-sided rather than a compliment.

**3. The register: does any chapter assert a claim at a strength `07` does not license?** R-8's
claim-to-chapter cross-check has never been run by anybody. `C<n>` appears zero times in the prose
of Books I–IV; the C-LICENSE convention only starts at V.1.

**4. The seam between Book IV and Book V.** IV.10 pre-spent Book V beats at containment 1.00 and
some of that is repaired and some is rowed (R-25). Does the join read?

**5. Book V's institutional half.** `04`'s sharpest standing finding is that the roster is
*"occult-heavy and institution-light, inverted against the reader's actual history."* Book V is now
closed. Was it fixed, or does the finding stand against the finished book?

## §4 — THE NULL SPACE, STATED, BECAUSE IT IS WHY YOU ARE BEING ASKED

The last pre-registration scored itself and reached this conclusion, which has not been repaired
and possibly cannot be:

> **Every one of my five predictions was about the machinery** — lexicon compliance, string-matcher
> coverage, citation exposure, chapter length. **Both of Fable's findings were about the argument.**
> I have instrumented the book at the level of bookkeeping and left the philosophy ungauged, because
> bookkeeping is the part I can write a gauge for.

Tonight added a fourth member to a family that says the same thing structurally: R-47 (nothing reads
a beat against a later ruling), R-49 (nothing read a brief for a missing field — now built), R-51
(nothing reads a brief field for *content*), **R-53 (nothing reads a name for *engagement* — a name
in a list counts as present and owes as much as a zero).** The cheap structural check keeps getting
built. The check on whether the thing is any *good* does not exist, is not cheap, and is what an
outside reader is for.

**So: findings about the argument outrank findings about the apparatus, and a finding in a category
not on any list in this file is worth more than everything on them.**
