## Method, and one instrument defect caught mid-run

"Panpsychism" would have been missed and read back as a zero. Re-run case-insensitive; both passes
agreed here, but the agreement was luck and is recorded as such.

**Positive control, so the zeros carry weight.** The same extraction over the ancestors the book

## Finding 1 (new here): the near-miss is worse than the absence, and it defeats the instrument

The book's zero-count instrument fires on *name absent*. Four of the most consequential living-adjacent
names are **present**, in other roles:

- **Dennett — 6 hits, every one in V.4**, engaged at full strength as one of the Four Horsemen, with
  the endnote apparatus running hard against the chapter's own interest ([^1] convicts the "one
  decade" framing as thirty-three months; [^5] catches the subtitle used unattributed). The book
  gets Dennett *right* in the one role it casts him in — opponent of religion. It never casts him in
  the role where he is the book's most dangerous living opponent: the man whose position is that the
  awareness clause is a user-illusion.
- **Searle 28** — 21 of them in IV.6, i.e. the Chinese Room, i.e. machine consciousness, not the
  metaphysics of consciousness.
- **Tononi/IIT 23** — used *constructively*, as a grading instrument in II.4, not as a rival theory
  with a published objection to being used that way.
- **Nagel 14** — the what-is-it-like, taken as a tool.

So a name-presence census over this manuscript reports **Dennett: engaged.** The instrument that
caught seven unnamed ancestors would return a clean bill on the one opponent whose absence matters
most, because he is in the building under a different charge. **Selector: name present. Question:
position engaged. They are not the same set, and the gap between them is exactly where the book's
foundation is undefended.**

## Finding 2 (new here): III.5 defends the most contested claim in a room of the dead

The no-NPC rule is the book's most contested claim and it knows it. Its defence against the
absurdity objection is staffed by **Giordano Bruno (d. 1600, 2 hits), the Ācārāṅga Sūtra (c. 5th c.
BCE, 10 hits), and Albert Schweitzer (d. 1965, 10 hits)** — all in III.5, and nothing else. Zero
living panpsychists arguing for it; zero living opponents arguing against it. The convergence
offered is real and it is entirely historical. This is the same shape the book itself convicts in
V.5 — a convergence assembled from a set the assembler selected.

all the review claimed. `Ghost Opus 5 Truth-and-Consequences-Critical-Assessment.md:91` reached it

By the book's own convergence rule — worth most where the routes are independent — two readers of

**Correction, against the book's interest:** Ghost's "Goff appears once" is wrong. That hit is
**Goffman**. The true count is zero, and the prior review's count erred in the book's favour.

the other four charges in its §VIII are untested here.

**Not tested and worth a later pass:** the review's own §VIII does not contain the D204 fresh read's
