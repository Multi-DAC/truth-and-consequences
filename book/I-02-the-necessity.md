# BOOK I — THE STILL

## I.2 — THE NECESSITY

A possibility that is merely possible has to be kept somewhere. In a mind that has not decided yet.
In a future that has not arrived. In a stock of unspent things that could be drawn on if the occasion
came. Every one of those is a room, and a room has an outside, and there is no outside.

The same will be true of everything you reach for up there. Nothing is being kept from you; there
is nobody up there to keep it, and no elsewhere to keep it in. The thing wanted is not among the
things there are. That is not a hard saying once, and it does not get easier by being said again —
but it will be said again, because it is the answer every time.

So in the Fullness there is nothing between what could be and what is. The two are one thing there.
They come apart only in here, where there is a tomorrow — where there is a difference between the
bread on the table and the bread you might buy. Take the tomorrow away and watch the difference go
with it. What is left is a single flat word, and no language spoken by things with futures has got
one.

Separation is a possibility.

Therefore separation. Not afterward. Not as a result, either — results arrive, and arriving is one
thing coming after another, and there is no after for anything to arrive in. Completeness did not
produce apartness. Apartness is one of the things completeness *is*, the way four sides is one of the
things a square is. Nobody worked that out. Nothing noticed the implication and acted on it. There
was no moment when the books were checked and a gap was found and a decision was taken, and nobody
stood at that moment to take it. It was in hand before there was a hand.

There is a party who does not accept the first move, and it is not the one usually put up to argue.

The actualist is not defending a stock of unspent things. They agree there is no room — that is
their point. Nobody was ever keeping possibilities anywhere, so showing there is nowhere to keep
them defeats nothing. There is one arrangement, this one, and it is brute: not selected, because
selecting wants alternatives to select from and there were none standing there to pass over. *It
could have been otherwise* is not a report filed from some other place. It is a remark about these
things, here, and what they can be shuffled into. The pieces are actual. Their rearrangements are
not further items needing somewhere to be. A truth about what could be needs something to make it
true. It does not need a warehouse.

Take that whole. It is right, and it is fatal to the argument as it was just made.

The opening move on this page assumed a possibility is the sort of thing that gets kept, and then
showed there is nowhere to keep it. Against the actualist the showing has no target. The room was
never theirs.

So the disagreement is somewhere else, and finding where is worth more than the win just lost.

Both sides now want the same thing — not storage, but a maker. What makes it true that things could
have gone otherwise? The actualist's answer is the inventory and what it can be shuffled into, and
that answer has an edge, which is theirs and not an insult carried in from outside: the shuffling is
bounded by the pieces. A way of being that wants an ingredient no thing anywhere has ever had cannot
be reached by any rearrangement of what there is. On that account such a way of being is not merely
absent. It is impossible — ruled out by a census.

That is the bill, and it is a large one. It means saying that the space of what could be is exactly
as wide as the stock of what is and not one degree wider, so that anything further off is not far
away but nothing at all.

And watch what becomes of the move on this page once that rule is in force. *Separation is a
possibility, therefore separation* does not break. It goes flat. On those terms separation is
possible because something actual is separate — you, reading this, at an edge, in a room. The
conclusion was granted before the sentence began.

So the honest report here is a concession, and it is about a word rather than about a fact.

Two things are being called possible. One is downstream of an inventory: possible means reachable
from what is already here. The one used above is downstream of nothing: possible means not ruled out
by the shape of the thing — the way no corner is ruled out of a circle, not the way no unicorn is
ruled out of a stable. In the second sense the argument goes through. In the first it begs the
question, and always did.

The second sense is the one taken here. Taking it is a choice with a price and not a proof that
would move anybody, and everything after this page is that price being paid. What is on offer is not
the actualist's defeat. It is a place to stand where both costs are in view: theirs, that the
possible is capped at the actual stock; ours, that the widest word in the book is doing work no
argument put into it.

You want.

You have wanted since before you had a word for it — for food, for a face to come back, for the
afternoon not to end, for someone to say the thing you cannot say for yourself. Strip the objects off
and the wanting is still underneath them, older than anything it has ever fastened onto, and it does
not feel like an accident of biology. It feels like a direction. It feels like being pulled from the
far end.

Hence the oldest guess there is: that the pull is not yours. That yours is the small end of an
enormous one — the whole of it, wanting, and you are where the wanting has got to. That your ache is
the universe aching in one particular place, and if you follow it far enough back you arrive at some
original longing and are finally in company.

Follow it back. It stops. It stops at the edge of the one who has it, every time, and it stops there
because that edge is the entire reason a wanting is possible.

A lack is a shape cut into something. It needs the something, and it needs the outline. Nothing is
cut into the Fullness. There is no line anywhere on it to serve as the rim of a hole, no place where
it leaves off and the missing thing would start. It is not short of you. It has never once been down
anything. Every hunger there has ever been is in here with us, held inside completeness exactly the
way this room is — entirely real, entirely local, and with nothing answering at the far end.

Which is not a demotion of your wanting. Your wanting is the most reliable thing about you. It is
simply not news about the whole. It is news about being a part, and a part is the only vantage from
which news has ever been filed.

There was no fall.

Nothing broke. Nothing was expelled. Nothing leaked out of a better condition into this one. Nobody
erred. There is no earlier state to be gathered back into — *earlier* is a word from in here, and it
does not reach. The old stories say otherwise and they say it beautifully, and under the costumes
every one of them is telling you the same thing: that you are the aftermath of something that went
wrong. You are not an aftermath. There is a focusing, and it is not a wound.

Consider what having is.

A hand closes around a cup. You can tell it is holding the cup because there is a place where the
hand stops and the cup starts, and a room around them both that is neither, and light in the room
that is not the room. Now take the edges out. Make the hand also the cup, and the room, and the
light, and the drinking, and the one who was thirsty. Nothing is being held. Not because the holding
failed — because holding wants two ends, and the second one has just been removed.

Everything is like this. A note is a note against the silence it is not. A face is a face because it
stops. Warmth is warmth to something that could be cold. Take a thing that is genuinely everything
and ask what it possesses, and the question finds nowhere to bite — not because the answer is nothing
but because there is no gap for a having to happen across.

So a totality cannot see what it is. Not the way a rich man does not know what he owns; he could
count it if he sat down with the ledgers. There is nothing there to do the seeing, nothing standing
off at a distance to be seen, and no distance for a look to cross.

And here the next sentence comes to everyone's mouth at once, and it is the wrong one: *so it breaks
itself open for a look.*

No. Nobody wanted the look. Nothing is better off for having it, and there is nobody there to be
better off. The necessity is the kind a circle has when it has no corners — true in the shape of the
thing, never once felt as a need by anyone, and no corner anywhere is being withheld. Seeing happens
for an inside. Only ever for an inside. There is no second party at the far end of your eyes finally
getting a look at itself.

Which leaves you where you have been the whole time: in a room, with edges, wanting things. The edges
are not the damage. Everything you have ever loved, you loved in pieces — one face and not all faces,
one afternoon and not the whole of time, this hand and not hands in general. That is not the poor
version of loving, kept in a smaller cup until the real thing arrives. It is the only arrangement in
which having occurs at all, and the Fullness, which is everything, has never once had anything.
