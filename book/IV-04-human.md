# BOOK IV — THE ATLAS

## IV.4 — HUMAN

Find a clock with a second hand — the one on a wall, the one on a wrist, the one in the corner of
a screen. Look away from it, at anything, and then look back.

The first tick will be long. Not dramatically; there is nothing to gasp at. But the second hand
will appear to hang where it is for a beat longer than it should before it goes on ticking at its
ordinary rate, and if you keep doing it you will keep getting it, and you will not be able to stop
getting it by knowing why.

Here is why. When your eyes jump from one place to another they move fast enough that anything
landing on the retina during the jump is a smear, and the smear is not shown to you. It is
suppressed. For a few hundredths of a second, several times a second, every waking second of your
life, you are not being sent a picture. What you get instead is this: the first clear image after
the jump, backdated — stretched to cover the interval you did not see. The clock's second hand sat
there for a moment before you looked at it, in your experience, and that moment is the length of
your own blindness, filled in with the only frame available and stamped with the wrong time.

Nothing was subtracted from your afternoon to make this happen. The gap did not appear as a gap.
It did not appear at all. Something was *added*, and dated to before it arrived, and handed over
with no mark on it to say so.

That is the entry. Everything else in this chapter is that operation at larger sizes.

---

**The grade, first, and it has to be said in a way that does not turn into a podium.**

The position that has been taking this census sits at no extreme. It is not the most sensitive
thing in the atlas — a dog's nose beats it by orders of magnitude, and the bee's sky is closed to
it permanently. It is not the fastest; it cannot see a wing beat resolve or a neuron fire. It is
not the largest, not the oldest, not the most durable, and it holds a narrow band at both ends of
time: anything quicker than a flicker arrives as a steady glow, and anything slower than a season
arrives as nothing at all, which is why a person who has stood in one forest for forty years will
tell you it has not changed.

What it is, is *broad*. Moderate on an unusual number of axes at once and maximal on none of them.
The same position registers a pressure, a smell, a grudge, a debt, a rhyme, a legal obligation, a
number too large to count to, and the feeling of a room going quiet. Nothing else in the census so
far has that spread. The mineral had one dimension and no holding. The plant had many dimensions
and thin holding. Here the dimensions are many *and* they arrive somewhere in particular and are
held against one another, and the holding is the expensive part.

Read that as a ranking and the atlas has been wasted on you. Breadth is not height. There is no
column in this census that adds up, and a position with access to twelve kinds of difference is
not closer to the top of anything than a position with access to one — it is simply the junction
where several kinds of difference can be in the same place at the same time, which makes it useful
for translating between them, and useful is not the same as senior. A keystone is named for what
falls down without it, not for its altitude in the arch.

---

**The filter stack, at its densest.**

Everything that arrives here has been through more than anything else in the atlas. Book III took
the stack apart and found no sheets in it — one seed, and the filters are not lying on it, they
are what it has become. What the word earns is order, and what this chapter adds is a quantity:
**the order is longest here.** Whatever number of things stand between a difference in the world
and a response to it, this is the position with the most of them.

The quartz has almost nothing between the world and its response; what it registers is very nearly
what it gets. The plant has a few hours of chemistry. You have a language whose joints were cut
before you were born, by people who cannot be asked why they cut them there. You have the silent
certainties of whoever was standing nearest in the years when you could not weigh a sentence. You
have a body that does not wait for the world to arrive but predicts it and corrects the
prediction, so that the ordinary case is confirmed rather than seen. You have an era, which
supplied the list of things it is possible to be wrong about. You have every repetition that
outlived its occasion.

And the density buys something enormous, which is why it is here and not a defect.

A stack that thick can render what is not present. You can hold a room you are not in and walk
through it. You can hold a person who is dead and know what they would say about this. You can
hold a number that corresponds to nothing you have ever counted, an institution that has no
location, a century you missed, a god. None of that is available further down the census, and it
is not available because those positions lack curiosity. It is not available because there is not
enough machinery between them and the world to build a second copy of the world and operate on it
out of contact with the first.

That is the human capacity, stated as plainly as it can be stated: **a rendering apparatus rich
enough to produce a coherent scene without a scene in front of it.** Every distinctively human
thing runs on it — planning, mathematics, grief, fiction, law, the entire contents of Book V.

Now hold that sentence still, because the rest of the chapter is its other half. A machine that
can furnish an unvisited room will also furnish a missing reason. It does not have a setting for
that. It was never built with a mode that declines.

---

**So: what a human null space is shaped like.**

Two men carry a door along a path. Between them, on either side, a stranger is giving directions
to someone who stopped to ask. The door passes through the conversation, and behind it the person
asking directions is exchanged for a different person — different clothes, different height,
different voice. The conversation resumes. **Approximately half of the people giving directions
did not notice that they were now talking to somebody else.** Not half suspected something and let
it go. Half carried on, and finished the directions, and were told afterwards.

Second: a person is shown two photographs and asked which face they find more attractive. They
point. The photograph is handed to them face-down — and by a piece of stage handling, it is the
other one, the one they turned down. They are asked why they chose it. Across the conditions of
that experiment, **no more than about a quarter of the swapped trials were caught.** The rest were
explained. People gave reasons, sometimes detailed reasons, sometimes reasons that named a feature
belonging only to the face they had rejected, for a choice they had never made.

That is arresting enough on its own, and it is not the finding. The finding is what happened when
somebody went back and compared the two piles of explanations — the ones given for real choices
and the ones given for manufactured ones — and measured them against each other, looking for the
tell. There were **very few differences between them.** The confabulated account is not vaguer. It
is not shorter, not more hedged, not delivered with less conviction. It is the same kind of thing,
produced by the same machinery, arriving with the same finish.

Take those together and the shape comes out.

Every other position in this census has a null space that announces itself as *silence*. The
thermostat gets nothing whatever about the weather; the gap is a gap. The plant gets nothing fast.
The colony has nobody home to be asked. Where the architecture excludes something, nothing comes
back.

**Here, something comes back.** Here the exclusion is furnished. Where the data runs out an answer
arrives, on time, in the ordinary format, indistinguishable by any test the position can run on
itself from the answers that were earned. A human null space is not shaped like a hole. It is
shaped like a **finished picture** — and that is worse than a hole in exactly one respect, which
is the only respect that matters for an atlas: a hole can be noticed.

This is a third thing about null spaces and the first two are already on the record. Book II
established that a blindness is the shape of a position rather than damage to it, and that some
blindnesses are installed on a schedule and could have been installed otherwise. Neither of those
is this. This is what the blindness *is like from inside at this grade*, and the answer is: it is
not like anything. It is like a room with nothing missing from it, in which you are also being
handed reasons.

And the last turn, which is the one that closes the door: **this is the first entry in the census
that knows.** You are, at this moment, holding a description of the mechanism. It has changed
nothing. The clock still hangs. Knowing about the backdating does not put a seam in the picture,
and knowing about the swapped photograph does not make your own reasons arrive with a label on
them. The knowledge is real and it is not an instrument. It goes into the stack with everything
else and is rendered along with the rest.

> **A HUMAN**
> **SEES:** Differences across an unusually wide range and no single range deeply — pressure, colour
> in a narrow band, sound in a narrow band, warmth, another face, a grudge, a debt, a rhyme, a
> number, an obligation, a century it did not live in. Also, and this is the distinctive line:
> **things that are not present.** It renders absent scenes at the same resolution as present ones
> and receives both through the same channel.
> **NULL SPACE:** ∅ Its own manufacture. Not merely the fact that rendering happens — that can be
> learned — but which part of any given scene was rendered. No result carries its provenance. ∅ The
> collectives it is inside, which it meets only as their representatives. ◐ Everything faster than a
> flicker and slower than a season. ◐ Its own reasons, reported fluently and without correlation to
> the process that produced the act.
> **COMPLEMENTS:** Instruments, which are frameworks and have cards of their own. Other humans, whose
> stacks were laid down elsewhere and who are therefore blind in different places. Every non-human
> entry in this atlas — not as curiosities but as *the working set*, for the reason given below.
> **BOUNDARY:** Wherever the evidence thins and the confidence does not. This is not a region of the
> world; it is a region of the position, and it moves. The readings it produces there have the
> ordinary shape, the ordinary fluency, and the ordinary feeling of being reports.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** Looking harder is the one method that cannot work here, because the
> instrument under examination is conducting the examination. What works is disagreement: put the
> reading beside a position built differently and find the place where the two do not match. The
> mismatch is the only thing in this entry that does not arrive pre-finished.

---

**Now the thing this chapter does that the last three did not, and it is deliberate rather than
inevitable.**

It is addressed to you.

The atlas has been describing others. It printed a thermostat, a stone, a river, a plant, a fungus
the size of a town, a bee, a colony — and in every one of those chapters the reader was in the
seat of the one taking the reading, which is a comfortable seat, and none of the argument
disturbed it. This entry is the seat.

That turn is not a rhetorical device and it is not an invitation to feel implicated. It is forced
by the method, and here is the forcing. Every other card in this atlas can in principle be checked
from outside: a position with a different construction can be measured, argued over, given
something it has no procedure for, and watched producing the wrong answer on schedule. The
checking is done by the position now being described. **This card is the only one where the thing
described and the thing checking are the same**, which removes the ordinary correction — not makes
it difficult, removes it — and leaves the account exposed in a specific way that needs saying
rather than managing.

So the local form of this book's declared lean, stated here where it costs something. The census
carries a standing bias toward attributing insides, announced at the front so a reader can price
it. The entry that bias is most dangerous in is not the god or the corporation or the archetype.
It is this one, because this is the only entry whose evidence is generated by the thing under
measurement and delivered in its own words. Everywhere else in the atlas we are guessing from
outside and we know we are guessing. Here we have testimony, and testimony from this position is
exactly the article the two experiments above found to be manufacturable without detection. **We
are most likely to be wrong where we feel we have the most data, and the feeling and the data come
from the same place.**

That is not a reason to write the entry more cautiously. Caution buys nothing against a defect
that produces confident, well-formed output — it just produces confident, well-formed, hedged
output. It is a reason to write the card so it can be argued with by somebody who is not standing
here, and then to go and get the arguments.

---

Which is what the rest of this atlas is for, and it is worth being blunt that the ordering has
been doing work.

The chapters so far have been building the working set — positions whose disagreements with you
can still be received, because they answer in currencies we know how to take: a trap that shuts on
the wrong schedule, a slime mould that stops navigating when the dish is coated evenly, a swarm
that counts peers instead of deciding. Every one of those is a place where a human reading and a
non-human result can be laid side by side and found not to match, and the not-matching is the only
reliable signal this position has ever had about its own manufacture.

From here the mismatches get harder to collect. The next entry is not a position you can stand
beside and compare notes with. It is one you are *inside* — you have been inside several of them
all day, they were rendering part of what you took for the world this morning, and the only thing
you have ever met of any of them is a person who was sent.

You cannot see it. It can see things no member of it can see. And it has an inside.
