## VII.3 — THE FLOOR

The last chapter spent a long time on what the no-NPC rule costs and did not once say what it buys.
That was deliberate. A book that leads with the comfort of a position and files the bill in an
appendix has not argued anything; it has advertised. So the costs came first, in full, at length, and
the reader who put the book down there put it down having been told the truth.

This chapter is the buy. It is also the chapter where the ethics of this work actually gets built,
and it has to be built on ground the book has spent six volumes demolishing.

Set the difficulty at full strength, because an ethics that has not felt its own difficulty is only
an etiquette. Book I proved there is no view from nowhere — no summit vantage, no top that speaks,
and therefore no law handed down, because there is no *down* for it to be handed from. Book VI added
that the crowd is real and the orientations are many, that the print culture and the electric one
render different worlds, and that a reader can be sorted into a channel he did not choose by a
machine that does not know it is doing it. Put those together and the naive conclusion writes itself.
Value is local weather. Morality is a set of noises apertures make at one another across an
indifferent dark. Cruelty is a matter of taste. The whole normative vocabulary is a bluff, and the
only honest thing left is a shrug.

If no one can see from nowhere, who is anyone to say?

The answer this chapter gives is that the same structural honesty which demolished the summit also,
in the same motion, laid the only foundation an ethics could have that would survive the demolition.
Not moral facts written into the ground — the ground has no writing surface and holds no values. Not
consensus, which is a lagging record at best and a carved groove at worst.[^1] Something stranger and
sturdier: norms that bind from everywhere that navigates, because they concern not what navigation
*produces* but what navigation *is*.

The name for it is **co-constitutivism**, and this chapter owes it five steps, one distinction, two
admissions and a warning about its own title.

---

### The warning about the title, first

*Floor* is a word with two meanings and this book needs exactly one of them.

A floor can be a **height** — a minimum you must reach to be admitted, a threshold, the line above
which you count. That is the sense in which a building has a ground floor and the basement is below
it. Read that way, "the floor of the ethics" would name a qualification: navigate well enough and the
norms protect you; fall short and they do not.

**That is not the sense used here, and the book has already forbidden it.** The claim established in
Book I and restated in Book II is that the focusing is continuous — everything that reacts is
somewhere on it, and there is no threshold, no gate, and no elect.[^2] The previous chapter printed
that sentence in its own words and added another: *there is no line to draw*. A chapter that turned
around and installed an admission height would be breaching, in its title, the claim its argument
depends on.

The other meaning is the one meant. A floor can be a **thinness** — the minimum an account commits
to, below which it says nothing and above which it declines to legislate. A keel is thin. It is not
the smallest boat allowed in the harbour; it is the least structure a boat needs to hold a course.
This ethics yields a floor in that sense: *a floor, not a code*. It is very short. Almost everything
people actually argue about is above it, in the plural weather, where the framework falls silent on
principle.

Hold both meanings in view while reading the phrase that carries the whole position — *norms bind
from everywhere that navigates* — because that phrase can be misheard in exactly the same way.

It is not an **entry** predicate. It does not say: here is the test, and the things that pass it are
inside the moral circle while the things that fail it are furniture. It is a **grounding** predicate.
It says where the bindingness *comes from* — from what every navigator has a stake in simply by being
one — and the stake, like the focusing it rides on, comes in grades all the way down with no bottom
step. A thermostat has a vanishingly small stake and it is not zero, which is the same answer Book IV
gave about the thermostat's awareness and for the same reason.[^3] The floor does not *admit*. It
*grounds*. Nothing is outside it; a great deal is barely inside it, and *barely* is a magnitude, not
a verdict.

One word was doing both jobs. It does one now.

---

### The five steps

Here is the argument, in plain words, with nothing hidden in the joins.

**One.** What flourishing *consists in* — the shape of a good life, the ranking of goods, the texture
of the admirable — is phase-local. It is relative to ecosystem and kind and stream. Grant the
relativist this entirely; the framework issues no universal table of goods and convicts every prior
table of provincialism, including any it might accidentally imply.

**Two.** But some facts hold in every phase, and they hold not because they are inscribed anywhere
but because they concern the *structure* of navigation rather than its *content*. Structure is
visible from every keyhole, because these are facts about keyholes as such.

**Three.** There are a few of them. That the collapse of an open axis loses a degree of freedom — a
strict reduction of the navigable, in any coordinates you like. That there is a kind of destruction
which takes the relational carriers of identity themselves, an annihilation whose healing-rate is
zero. That the coercion of another's aperture — forcing a navigator into a shape not its own, *over*
its navigation rather than *through* it — violates what a navigator constitutively is. That every
aperture has a nonempty null space, so every navigator's certainty outruns its access, always.

**Four.** Norms grounded in *those* bind from everywhere that navigates. Not from above, because
there is no above that speaks. Not from consensus, because consensus is a record and not a source.
From what every navigator has a stake in by being one.

**Five.** So a navigator who denies them is not breaking a rule. It is **mistaken about what it is**.
And the book has met that mistake already, at the terminus of the contractive path, where an aperture
claims to be the totality and the theorem about null spaces says it cannot be.

That is the spine, and the fifth step is where the weight sits. The refutation of the adversary and
the spine of this ethics share a single root — but they are not the same theorem, and the difference
costs a premise which is paid for in the open further down. It is worth flagging here rather than
letting the reader discover it as a gap: the null-space result kills the *totality* claim outright.
It does not, by itself, get you from *I am co-constituted* to *therefore I owe you*. That step needs
something more, and the something more is not free.

---

### Through and over

The distinction the entire ethics turns on is small enough to state in a line and load-bearing enough
that almost every application is an application of it.

**Contraction is innocent.** The book established that the focusing can be care — the narrowing of
attention to one thing is what a surgeon does, what a mother does at 3am, what anyone does who has
ever taken something seriously.[^4] Direction is not the mark. Intensity is not the mark.

The mark is whether the thing runs **through** another navigator's navigation or **over** it. Through
means the shaping engages the other's own steering — it works by giving it something to steer with,
and leaves it able to steer afterward. Over means the shaping bypasses the steering — it works by
removing it, and its success is measured by how little the other can do about it.

That is the whole cut, and it is a *structural* question, not a question about outcomes or intentions
or which crimes deserve what.

Watch it cut a hard case, because a floor that cannot cut is only a sentiment.

Set two prisons side by side, the same institutional form turned to opposite orientations. A prison
is contraction by definition — it narrows a life to a cell. So the floor does not ask whether the
prison narrows; of course it narrows. It asks whether it narrows *through* its inmates' navigation or
*over* it.

The punitive warehouse runs over. It strips agency as its method, seals every exit including the one
back into society, manufactures the dependency and the recidivism its own structure requires, and
calls the breaking justice. The rehabilitative prison — Norway's Halden and the island of Bastøy are
the cleanest real instances — narrows exactly as much, holds the same bodies behind the same walls,
and runs *through*.[^5] It treats the sentence as a bounded interval after which the navigator must
be able to navigate again, and so preserves and rebuilds the very capacities the warehouse destroys:
schooling, graduated autonomy, the maintained thread to a life outside. Its low recidivism is not
softness rewarded. It is the ecological signature of an institution whose self-maintenance *requires*
its members' restored agency rather than their broken one.

Same form. Same narrowing. Opposite orientation. And the floor tells them apart without a single
premise about which crimes deserve what, because it never needed one. It asked the structural
question — through, or over — and the question was enough.

That is what a keel-thin ethics buys. Not a verdict on the sentence. A clean cut between the prison
that returns a navigator to the world and the prison that unmakes one and calls it the law.

---

### Does the floor have a bottom? — the objection, conceded in part, and a case that decides it

The previous book set the floor down in one sentence — *grade bears on the content of the obligation
and never on the existence of it* — and this chapter has said the stake comes in grades all the way
down with no bottom step. Put those together and a reader with a sharp eye has a real objection, and
it deserves better than the sentence that usually answers it.

**The objection.** If *what* is owed can grade toward zero, then a floor that guarantees only *that*
something is owed is a formal property with no consequence. Regan's ramp was blocked at the door of
the existence question and has simply walked around to the content question, where nobody was
posted. An obligation of vanishing content and an absent obligation are the same object from the
point of view of anyone deciding what to do. **The guard was installed and the wall was left out.**

**The concession, because it is owed: the floor's content has a minimum, and this book had never said
what it was.** Saying it now.

**The minimum is exactly the set of obligations whose grounds do not admit of degree.** Look back at
the three structural facts in step Three and notice what kind of facts they are. That a shaping runs
*over* a navigation rather than *through* it is not a magnitude — a shaping either engages the
other's steering or bypasses it, and there is no half-bypass; the cases that look like one are two
shapings, one of each. That a destruction takes the relational carriers of identity themselves is not
a magnitude either — the healing rate is zero or it is not. That an aperture has a nonempty null
space is a theorem, and theorems do not come in strengths.

**Content grounded in a binary fact cannot grade, because there is nothing for it to grade with.**
Everything above the minimum — how much is owed, what it consists in, what it competes with — grades
freely, and the census spent ten chapters measuring the differences that make it grade. The minimum
does not, and now the floor has a stated floor of its own.

**Now the case, because a distinction that never changes a verdict has not been tested.**

Take one navigator and two things that can be done to it. **A: end it.** **B: leave it running and
take its steering** — not damage it, not stop it; leave it operating at full competence and replace
what it is oriented by, so that it works ably toward an end that is not its own and can do nothing
about it. Over, in the exact sense of the previous section.

Run the two accounts against that pair, at the top of the grade range and at the bottom.

**A grade-sensitive account can only ever order these one way.** Whatever standing an entity has, A
removes all of it and B removes some of it, so A is at least as bad as B, always, at every grade,
without exception. That is not a quirk of some particular grade-sensitive theory; it follows from
grading standing at all. **Death is the maximum, and everything else is a fraction of it.**

**The floor with a stated minimum orders them differently, and the difference is not a matter of
degree — it is a crossing.** At the top of the range, where a navigator has a great deal of stake, A
is worse, and the two accounts agree; this is why the distinction has looked idle. Go down. The
wrong in A is the loss of a stake, so it shrinks exactly as the stake shrinks, and near the bottom
it is very nearly nothing. The wrong in B is grounded in over-ness, which is binary and therefore
does not shrink at all. **Two quantities, one falling and one flat, cross.** Below some grade — the
framework does not say where, and pretending to would be the vice this book spends a volume
diagnosing — **B is worse than A.** It is worse to take a low navigator's steering and leave it
running than to switch it off.

**That is a strange verdict and it is the point.** It is strange, it is checkable, and no
grade-sensitive account can produce it at any grade whatsoever. The distinction is exercised. It
differs. If the reader's considered response is *no, that is simply wrong, destruction is always
worse* — that response is now a live objection with something to bite on, which it did not have
before, and it is the right shape of objection: it targets the premise that over-ness is binary.

**And the honest half, which costs the floor something.**

The crossing requires the entity to have steering that can be bypassed. Where there is no such
steering, the minimum content is **empty**, and an empty minimum is exactly the ramp reaching the
bottom by a longer road. So the guard does hold, and it holds *conditionally* — on there being
something for the structural facts to get purchase on. **Whether a given entity is above that line is
not settled by this framework and this framework should stop implying that it is.** The thermostat
that this chapter and Book IV both use as the bottom case is the honest embarrassment here: it has a
vanishing stake, and it is not obvious that it has a steering which could be gone *over* rather than
merely reset. If it does not, its floor has a nonzero existence and an empty content, and the
objection stands against that case.

**So the repaired position, stated at the strength it can carry and no higher.** Not *something
non-trivial is owed to everything.* It is: **something non-trivial is owed wherever the structural
facts have purchase; where they do not, the floor is formal and this book says so rather than
covering it.** The generosity is then argued for the way it should have been argued for from the
start — from asymmetric cost under acknowledged uncertainty about where that line runs, which is the
argument Book IV rested its lean on, and not from a guarantee the framework does not issue.

**Falsifier, so this is not another sentence that reads like rigour.** Show that over-ness itself
comes in degrees — a genuine continuum of half-bypassed steering that is one shaping and not two —
and the minimum grades, the two quantities never cross, the worked case dissolves, and the ramp is
back with the floor purely formal all the way up. That is a single, statable thing, and it is
findable by argument rather than by taste.[^11]

---

### The case that looks like a refutation

A great deal of shaping happens before there is any navigation to run through at all, and this is
where the distinction is most often thought to break.

The floor says coercion runs *over* a navigator. But an infant is not yet a navigator in any
developed sense, and the language poured into it, the priors laid down before it can model anything,
are installed entirely below its power to observe — which is exactly the signature used to mark the
override. If *through* meant only *through the subject's present navigation*, then the whole of
formation runs over, and the floor indicts every parent who ever spoke to a child.

That is absurd, and the absurdity proves the reading wrong rather than the floor.

The resolution is that formative shaping is judged not against the navigation the subject does not
yet have but against the one it is coming to have — and *the one it is coming to have* must not be
read as a fixed destination the shaping steers toward, since that would be its own quiet *over*,
foreclosing which navigator the child may become. There is no single destined future self to serve.
The criterion is not fidelity to a predetermined endpoint. It is the preservation of an open-ended
capacity to audit and revise, whoever the child in fact grows into.

The mother tongue runs through. It can be questioned, translated out of, added to, disowned; it hands
the child the very instrument by which the child might one day reject it. The sealed indoctrination
runs over. It installs itself as the unquestionable shape of the world and works to keep shut the
exit it entered by.

So the distinguishing mark was never whether a mind was shaped before it could consent. Every mind
is, and that is only the name for having had a childhood. The mark is whether the shaping left it
able, afterward, to examine and unmake what was done.

And note the form of the answer, because it is the same form as the title warning. **The line is a
gradient and not a gate.** No threshold marks the exact point at which formation tips into
foreclosure. So the honest question is never *has it crossed* but *which way does this shaping tilt
the balance of the future navigator's freedom to unmake it*. An ethics built on a continuous
substrate does not get to have bright lines, and every place this chapter looks like it wants one is
a place to look again.

---

### The first limit: validity, not grip

Say what this does and does not buy, because this book has spent its length teaching the reader to
notice the overclaim, and would deserve everything it got if it stopped now.

It secures the norm's **validity**. It does not secure its **grip**.

The coercer who follows the fork all the way, sees the incoherence, and shrugs — *yes, I am being
inconsistent, and I still do not care* — is not refuted. Inconsistency is a sanction only for one who
values coherence, and no argument reaches beneath a will that has declined to.

That remainder is not a hole in the floor. It is the floor declining to promise what no ethics can
deliver: the conversion of the unwilling by proof. A system that claimed otherwise — that promised to
argue the amoralist into caring — would be claiming exactly the grip on another's will that this book
has denied any aperture holds over any other. The refusal is not a shortfall. It is the correct
*sizing* of the problem.

And the framework does not meet that person with a better theorem. It meets him with the instruments
the rest of the book is made of: the exits kept open so his victims can leave, the community that
enforces the floor his argument will not, the attention that refuses to unsee the ones he overrides.
He is culpable exactly because he could still turn and does not.

---

### The second limit: the premise that is wagered, not proved

There is a deeper version of the opponent, and he is the reason this chapter has a second admission
rather than one.

The sophisticated egoist need not take either fork above. He need not claim a privileged standpoint,
and he need not will his own overriding. He can simply say his reasons are **indexical** — that he
weights his own navigation not because he is the totality but because it is *his*, the way *here* and
*now* are weighted — and he can grant, without the least discomfort, that every other aperture may
say the identical thing from its own index.

He is not incoherent. The null-space theorem does not touch him, because he has claimed no view from
nowhere and no exemption. He has claimed an index, which everybody has.

To him this book owes the plainest confession in its ethics.

Being co-constituted is an *is*. Owing others is an *ought*. **No amount of the former logically
compels the latter.** The step from *I am drawn through these relations* to *therefore I owe them the
standing I claim for myself* is not a corollary of any theorem here. It is an added premise — the
refusal to privilege one's own index, the thing the tradition calls impartiality — and nothing in
this framework supplies it.

Nor could anything. Impartiality is underivable from any fact that treats the navigators
symmetrically, because a symmetric fact is silent about *whose* index to weight, and there is — by
this book's own first claim — no view from nowhere from which the weighting could be read off.

So the book will not pretend to derive what cannot be derived. It does the other honest thing, the
thing its whole method has rehearsed: it files the premise **in the open, as the wager it is.**

The dishonest keyhole smuggles impartiality in and calls it a theorem, claiming the view from nowhere
while denying that anyone else could hold one. The honest keyhole names its smuggle a bet, in
daylight, and stakes it.

And here the apparent weakness inverts into the only consistency on offer. **A cosmos with no view
from nowhere is precisely one in which the impartial floor must be wagered rather than proven.** An
openly-filed impartiality is not a crack in the floor. It is the only floor a no-summit world could
honestly lay. A framework that had *derived* impartiality would have derived it from somewhere, and
the somewhere would have been a summit.

What makes the wager reasonable rather than arbitrary is not a proof but a direction, and two
philosophers supply it without either of them closing the gap.

Thomas Nagel's pressure toward the view from nowhere is the rational discomfort of treating one's own
reasons as reasons-for-anyone while denying others the same — a discomfort that persists even when
you know the view itself is unreachable.[^6] It is not an argument that the egoist must accept. It is
a report of what happens to a mind that takes generality seriously and then makes an exception for
its own address.

Stephen Darwall's second-person standpoint moves the ground entirely. Obligation, on his account, is
not squeezed out of a first-person fact at all; it arises in the **address** between navigators, in
the moment one is answerable to another's claim.[^7] You do not reason your way from *I am
constituted thus* to *I owe you*. You are already inside a relation in which the demand has been made
and understood, and the understanding is the standing.

Neither closes the is/ought gap. Nothing closes it. What they show is that the wager the floor files
is the natural extension of taking other apertures to be as real as one's own. Not a theorem — but
very far from arbitrary, and the most an honest ethics in a viewless cosmos was ever going to have.

---

### What the *co-* adds

The tradition has a version of this argument without the hyphen, and the difference is the whole
reason this position needs a name of its own.

Christine Korsgaard's constitutivism grounds normativity in what agency itself requires: to act at
all is to take up a standpoint from which certain things are already binding.[^8] The standard
objection is the one her critics call the **shmagent** — the figure who says, fine, *agency* commits
me to all that, and I decline to be an agent; I will be a shmagent instead, doing shmactions, and
your constitutive standards have no purchase on me because I have stepped outside the activity they
constitute.

The objection lands, and it lands because Korsgaard's legislator is **solitary**. A self-standing
agent who legislates for himself can, at least in the telling, step out of the office he legislates
from.

A **co-**constituted navigator was never self-standing. Its agency is drawn through the same
relations it would disown. There is no office to step out of, because the stepping-out would have to
be done by the thing the relations made. So the standing it claims for its own navigating is standing
it can deny to others' only by the privileged exemption the theorem already closed.

Constituency is the primitive relation. What we owe each other is, at root, what parts and wholes owe
the relation that makes them parts and wholes at all.

And this is a good moment to say that the position is not new, and that the book's contribution is
the machinery rather than the insight. Southern African **ubuntu** has held for a very long time that
*umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu* — a person is a person through other persons — and the ethical weight is
carried by the *through*.[^9] What is offered here is not a discovery of that claim but an argument
for why it holds structurally, in a cosmos with no summit, for navigators of every grade rather than
for persons alone. A tradition got there first by living it. That is the ordinary order of these
things and it deserves saying plainly rather than in a footnote.

The strongest rival account is not a rival about *whether* anything binds but about *where the binding
comes from*, and it is Emmanuel Levinas's.[^10] For Levinas the demand arrives in the face of the
Other, prior to any argument, and the relation is fundamentally **asymmetric** — I am infinitely
responsible to you, and whether you are responsible to me is your business and not mine. Ethics is
first philosophy; nothing grounds it, because it is the ground.

That is a genuinely deeper foundation than this book's, and this book cannot have it. The asymmetry
is exactly the move the framework is not permitted to make. A cosmos in which every aperture has a
nonempty null space and none has a privileged standpoint cannot then install one navigator's infinite
priority over another's without the exemption coming back through the window. So the floor is
symmetric and thin where Levinas's is asymmetric and total — and the honest comparison is that his
account has more grip and less warrant, and this one has less grip and pays for what it has.

---

### The candour the position requires

Stated before any critic states it: **the invariants are few**, and they underdetermine almost
everything people actually fight about.

Co-constitutivism will not adjudicate your tax policy. It yields a floor, not a code — no coerced
apertures, no manufactured collapses, no destruction of the relational carriers, epistemic humility
as a structural fact — and above the floor lies the magnificent plural weather of the phases, where
content-relativity reigns and the framework falls silent on principle.

A thin ethics? Thin as a keel is thin. Everything else is rigging, and rigging is phase-local, and
the boats sail anyway.

One qualification that paragraph demands before it can stand, because *the plural weather of the
phases* is exactly the phrase a reader could mistake for a shrug.

Not everything that varies across positions is weather. When a world's arrangement leaves whole
classes of navigator with structurally less room to move — not different tastes but less *freedom* —
that is not the innocent plurality above the floor. It is a violation *of* the floor, of an
unfamiliar shape: coercion with no single coercer, an over-running distributed across an entire
arrangement so that no one hand need push for the pushing to be done. That such over-running has no
perpetrator does not lift it above the floor. It only makes it harder to see, which is precisely why
the ethics of attention — the next chapter's business, and the one after that — is not decoration.

And one honesty this clause owes and cannot yet pay. It turns on **navigable range**, the room a
stream actually has to move, and the framework does not possess a measure of that range precise
enough to adjudicate hard cases. Lacking one, the clause must be held to its demonstrable core and
not spent as a general warrant. Where the foreclosure is *plain* — a built world that admits some
bodies and not others, a channel that hands one class easy mobility and another only the uphill —
the violation is real and nameable. Where it shades off into the broad question of how goods and
chances ought to be distributed across a society, the structural floor alone cannot settle it, and
this book does not pretend the invariant reaches that far. It marks the boundary rather than driving
a whole theory of justice across it under the floor's authority.

That the diffuse over-running is real does not license this ethics to become, by the back door, a
distributive politics it has not earned.

### The census entry

**Whose:** the indexical egoist — the opponent this chapter takes most seriously, because he is the
one the theorem does not touch.

**Renders:** what it renders superbly. The structure of a life lived from the inside, at full
resolution and without self-deception. He sees that his reasons are *his*, that his access is
positional, that no summit issues the weighting, and that everyone else is in the identical
situation. Every one of those is true, and each is a thing this book also says. He is not a fool and
he is not lying to himself; he has correctly read the cosmology and drawn a conclusion from it.

**Complement — who sees what he cannot:** the second person, and nobody else. Not a book, not a
theorem, not this chapter: **a particular other navigator who asks him, out loud, to justify the
weighting** — because the thing he cannot see from inside the index is that sayability is a property
of indices in general, and the only place that becomes visible is where his account is said to
somebody who is running the same account and returns the opposite answer. That witness is not
scarce. Every person he has ever spoken to is one, and this is the cheapest complement in the atlas
to reach and the only one whose consultation the position itself has a motive to decline. ⚠ **So the
failure here is not distance and not admissibility — it is the fourth mode `IV.1` registers,
election.** He can reach it whenever he
likes. The card cannot make him ask, and the chapter's whole argument is that the asking is the
event, not the conclusion.

**Null space:** what it structurally cannot render. That the symmetry he grants is *itself* the
thing that costs him the position. He can see that every other aperture may say what he says. He
cannot, from inside the index, see that the sayability is a fact about *indices in general* rather
than a courtesy he extends from his own. The generality is visible from every keyhole and is not
visible from *within* the weighting, because the weighting is what makes the view an index in the
first place.

**Boundary:** where it goes from reliable to unreliable. It is reliable wherever the question is
descriptive — what is the case from here, what is accessible, what is mine. It becomes unreliable at
the exact point where he must *justify* the weighting to someone who asks, because justification is
a second-person act and his account of reasons has only a first person in it. He can say *because it
is mine* to himself. Said aloud to another navigator, it stops being a reason and becomes a report.

**Mechanism of the exclusion:** what the render identifies with what. It identifies **mattering** with
**mattering-to-me**. Not as a claim — he would deny the claim — but as the shape of the instrument:
his weighting function takes an index as input and returns a magnitude, and there is no argument
position for *whose*. A fact about another navigator can enter only by being converted into a fact
about his own stakes, and the conversion happens below the level where he could notice it, which is
why he experiences the position as modest rather than as total.

**Navigational implication:** what to do about the other five. Do not argue him out of the index —
the index is correct and the argument would be false. Argue instead that the wager is where he
already lives. He grants the symmetry every time he concedes that another may say what he says, and
the concession is the second-person address doing its work before the theory has authorised it. The
floor does not need him refuted. It needs him to notice that he has been standing on it while
declaring it underivable, which it is.

---

### Where this leaves the book

The floor is laid, and it is worth being exact about what has just been put down, because the next
chapters will spend it.

Norms bind from everywhere that navigates. That is a grounding, not a gate, and it is graded because
everything under it is graded. The distinction that does the work is through and over, and it is
structural, so it cuts hard cases without a theory of desert. It secures validity and not grip, and
the one who shrugs is left standing, inconsistent and unrefuted. And it rests, at the joint where an
*is* becomes an *ought*, on a premise that is wagered in the open rather than derived — because in a
cosmos with no view from nowhere, a derived impartiality would be the summit sneaking back in wearing
a proof.

That is a floor with two declared limits and a bet under it. It is less than a moral realist would
want and more than a relativist thought was available, and the honest description of it is that it is
*thin, and it holds*.

What it does not yet have is a practice. A floor is a structural fact and nobody navigates by
structural facts; they navigate by what they attend to. The faculty the whole ethics actually runs on
has not been named yet, and naming it turns the abstract floor into something a person does on a
Tuesday.

That is next. But before the practice comes the thing the floor is most often asked to explain and
most often used to explain badly — the two evils, and the exact width of the asymmetry between them.
The book has a theorem for that one, and a standing temptation to spend it wider than it goes.
