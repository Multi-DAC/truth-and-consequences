# BOOK II — THE NAMING

## II.6 — COHERENCE

Most of the words in this book had to be taken from somebody. This one has to be taken from nobody in
particular, which is harder, because it is not owned. It is only used — constantly, thinly, and with
a meaning so settled that the reader will not notice a different one arriving.

**Coherent** currently means *makes sense*. A coherent argument hangs together. A coherent paragraph
parses. Somebody surfacing from anaesthetic is asked whether they are coherent yet, and the question
is whether their sentences follow one another. Every one of those uses is about a *description*:
whether a set of statements is consistent with itself. The word has been demoted to a property of
accounts.

Here it is a property of things. Not of the account of the thing — of the thing.

And it has two senses, which is the difficulty, because a reader who is given two senses assumes one
of them is the loan.

---

**The first sense is structural.** A thing made of levels is coherent when its levels agree.

Almost everything is made of levels. A body is cells inside tissues inside organs inside a body. A
person is the one at this moment inside the one who has a week inside the one who has a life. A
household is people inside pairs inside a household. Nothing in that list is a heap; each level is
doing something, and what each is doing either fits what the level above and below is doing or does
not. When it fits, all the way up and all the way down, the thing coheres in the structural sense.
This is a fact about the arrangement. Nobody has to feel anything for it to obtain.

**The second sense is felt.** It is the sense of going the way you are made to go.

This one has no good name in English, which is why it gets described in figures — moving with the
grain rather than against it, being in your element, the work that goes easily after a month of
work that did not. It is not pleasure. A person can be steeped in pleasure and know, underneath it,
that they are going wrong, and the knowledge is not a thought they are having but a condition they
are in. Nor is it satisfaction, which is what happens after getting; this is available in the middle
of not getting, and it is what makes some kinds of difficulty bearable and some kinds corrosive.
Its absence has a better name than it does. **Dissonance** is the felt distance between where you
are and where your own motion is tending — not a punishment administered from outside but a reading,
taken from inside, of how far the two have come apart.

Now the ordinary move. One of these two is taken as real and the other as a manner of speaking, and
which way a person takes it announces their metaphysics before they have said a word. The
hard-headed reading says the structural sense is the fact and the felt sense is a report about it —
a readout, generated by machinery, useful for steering and metaphysically inert. The soft reading
says the felt sense is the reality and the structural one is a diagram of it drawn afterwards by
people who like diagrams. Both readings are doing the same thing. Both have decided in advance that
one of the two is the original and the other is its shadow.

**They are one thing met from its two sides.**

Not two things that reliably keep company. Not a fact and its readout. The structural agreement of a
thing's levels and the felt rightness of going the way it goes are the outside and the inside of a
single condition, and they are one for exactly the reason the last four chapters have been
establishing: at a position there is no seam between what is the case and what is had. A perspective
is not a device that observes its own condition. It is that condition, from inside.

So coherence is not a human feeling laid over neutral machinery, and the feeling is not evidence for
something else that is the real event. The feeling is the event, known from within. It is what
going-well *is*, for anything that goes — and where there is a position at all, however slight,
there is some faint inside to its agreeing or failing to agree with itself, on the same continuum
the previous chapter laid out end to end.

This is the chapter's whole claim, and everything after it is conditions and failure.

---

**The conditions.** Four of them, and a thing that meets all four coheres and keeps cohering.

It is easy to cohere in a snapshot. At an instant, almost anything looks consistent; a photograph of
a collapsing building is a photograph of a building. What is hard, and what the word is for, is
staying coherent while the world moves and new information keeps arriving and the pressure to settle
early never lets up. So the conditions are conditions on the maintenance, not on the configuration.

Run them through a body, since everyone has one and everyone has watched one go wrong.

**Separation.** The levels have to be genuinely distinct. A body works because a cell is not the
tissue and the tissue is not the organ — there are real boundaries, and something is held at each of
them. A body whose levels had dissolved into one another would not be more unified; it would be a
slurry, and there would be nothing left to agree. This is the condition most often mistaken for its
opposite. Union is not the erasure of the parts. Where the parts have been erased there is only one
level, and one level cannot cohere with anything.

**Measurement.** There have to be real points of contact with what is the case — events that could
have come out otherwise and that tell the system something its own expectations did not already
contain. A body finds out about the world through hunger, injury, illness, exertion, the taste of
something spoiled. What it cannot do is find out by consulting itself. A system that only ever
confirms its own priors is not settling anything; it is talking to itself, and the talk can go on
indefinitely without a single thing being decided. This word is doing more work than it looks and
is the whole subject of the chapter after this one.

**Agreement across the levels.** The settling at one level has to be compatible with the settling at
the others. The cell's business and the organ's business and the body's business have to be
resolvable in the same direction — not identical, they are doing different jobs, but capable of
being true at once. Where they are not, nothing has been resolved. A contradiction has been buried
at a level where nobody is looking, and it comes back later wearing the clothes of an unrelated
problem.

**Maintenance through time.** The whole arrangement has to be performed again. A body is not a thing
that was assembled correctly once; it is a rhythm — beating, breathing, sleeping, repairing,
replacing nearly all of itself on a schedule while remaining, unambiguously, the same body. Coherence
is not a state that is reached and then held. It is a rhythm that is kept, and the keeping is the
thing itself rather than the maintenance of the thing.

In the game: a party is not coherent because its members are individually strong. It is coherent
when what each of them is doing can be true at the same time as what the others are doing, again,
next fight, and the fight after that.

---

Now the two ways it loses, and they go here rather than in a later chapter that defends the term
under pressure. A claim that names its failure conditions at the definition is a claim. A claim that
waits to be pressed is a definition with good manners.

**The first failure is the lying channel.**

Everything above assumed that contact informs — that looking longer gets you closer. Where that
fails, coherence does not merely weaken. It inverts. Give a system a channel that is not noisy but
*deceptive*, one that drifts a reader further from the truth the longer they attend to it, and the
faithful integrator — the one that takes in what arrives and lets it accumulate properly into a
settled reading — converges precisely on the falsehood. Meanwhile the sloppy system, integrating
badly, half-ignoring what it is told, does better. At the point where a channel stops informing, the
capacity that was an advantage becomes the mechanism of the injury.

Which reveals that the word has quietly been doing two jobs, and this is the place they come apart.
One job is *faithful integration* — holding a reading open and letting evidence accumulate into it.
The other is *settling at the right moment*. On an honest channel these are the same discipline,
which is why nobody separates them. On a lying one they are opposed: faithful integration walks you
into the lie, and a system that knew what its channel was actually worth would find that one more
look raises its error rather than lowering it, and would settle at once. So the vulnerability is not
in holding a question open. It is in being wrong about your own channel — computing when to settle
from numbers taken on trust. No amount of internal agreement repairs a statistic you were handed.
When to settle, and how a system could know, is Book VIII's practical problem; what matters here is
that whether a channel informs or deceives is a checkable fact about the world and not a fact about
the system's virtue, and that is what keeps this a claim the world can refute.

**The second failure needs no deception at all.** It needs only the reminder that coherence is
relative to a scale.

Consider a cancer cell. It is not a disordered thing. At its own level it is ferociously coherent —
self-maintaining, holding its own direction, meeting separation and contact and maintenance-through-
time as well as any cell around it, and by the local measure of doing-well it does not merely survive
but outperforms every well-behaved neighbour it has. It fails exactly one of the four conditions,
and it fails it completely. Its coherence is bought *against* the coherence of the thing it is part
of. And the more successful the part becomes, the more certainly the whole is destroyed, and the
part with it.

That is where *outperform* comes apart into its scales and shows what it had been hiding. The tumour
outperforms at the cell's level and is catastrophic at the body's. There is nothing smuggled into
that sentence — no appeal to the tumour being secretly incoherent, no borrowed word doing moral work.
There are two measurements, taken at two levels, which the account requires to agree, and which here
do not. This is why the demand was never for coherence as such but for coherence *across* levels,
and why a part's coherence maximised against its whole is not a small evil but the paradigm of one.

Two failure surfaces, then — whether a system's channels inform, and whether its levels agree. Their
existence is the entire difference between a principle and a definition. Coherence is not another
word for good. The two places it turns destructive are the two places the account can be caught being
wrong, which is what an account, as opposed to a tautology, is for.

---

Somebody got most of this in the seventeenth century, and got it in a form sharp enough that the cut
has to be made carefully.

**Baruch Spinoza**, in the *Ethics*, put the striving of a thing to persevere in its own being not
alongside its essence but *as* its essence — the striving is not something the thing does, it is what
the thing is. And in the same work he made the move this chapter has just made, in his own
vocabulary and with far more machinery behind it: thought and extension are not two things that keep
company, they are one substance comprehended now under this attribute and now under that. The inside
and the outside of one condition, three hundred and fifty years early, argued rather than asserted.

He also had the scale problem, and had it in an image nobody has improved on. Writing to Henry
Oldenburg in November of 1665, he asks what the world would look like to a little worm living in the
blood — a worm able to see the particles of lymph and chyle, and to watch each one meet another and
be repelled or pass on some of its motion. Such a worm, he says, would live in the blood as we live
in our part of the universe, and would take each particle of blood for a whole rather than a part.
The worm is not making an error of reasoning. It is occupying a level, and from that level the
blood's own nature is not visible.

**The cut is at what the striving is for.** Spinoza fixes it in the fourth part of the *Ethics*: the
more each thing strives, and is able, to seek its own advantage — that is, to preserve its being —
the more it is endowed with virtue. Persistence is the criterion. And persistence is exactly the
quantity that comes apart across levels, because by that measure the tumour is the most virtuous
cell in the body. It strives harder, preserves its being more successfully, and seeks its own
advantage with fewer inhibitions than anything around it. So perseverance cannot be what coherence
is. What is wanted is not the strength of the striving but the agreement of the strivings, and those
are different quantities that happen to coincide in the healthy case.

And underneath that, the deeper divergence, which is not a failure of his care. **In Spinoza's
nature, the whole always wins.** The worm's ignorance is an inadequacy of the worm's position and is
correctable by a wider view; at the widest view everything agrees, necessarily, because the whole is
God and God does not contain a genuine quarrel. Disagreement between levels is therefore never a
fact. It is always somebody's inadequate idea. Which means that the second failure surface this
chapter had to state is unstateable in his system: he cannot have a part whose coherence is really
purchased against its whole, because the agreement of the whole is guaranteed before any part is
examined.

The same thing is true of the oldest version of the structural claim, and the repetition is worth
naming rather than leaving to look like coincidence. *As above, so below* — the Hermetic formula,
fifteen centuries older than Spinoza and the slogan this whole picture has been walking around
without crediting — says the levels *resemble* each other. Resemblance is stronger than agreement
and cheaper, because it makes divergence not pathological but impossible. Neither the formula nor
the philosopher can say what is wrong with a tumour. Both bought the harmony of the levels in
advance, and what you buy in advance you cannot lose, and what cannot be lost was never a claim.

One link upstream, because the striving is not Spinoza's either. It is Descartes', from the second
part of the *Principles of Philosophy* — that each thing, so far as it is in its power, remains
always in the same state. Spinoza had worked through the *Principles* closely enough to write his
own exposition of them, and when the phrase reappears in the *Ethics* it is carried over word for
word, moved out of motion and into essence. **The persistence sense of going-well arrives from
physics.** It is the law of inertia in ethical clothing, which is why it feels self-evident that a
thing's good is to keep on being what it is, and why the intuition is so difficult to put down. It
is also why it is the wrong shape for this. Inertia has no levels in it. A law about one body
continuing cannot tell you anything about whether a body's parts are continuing in the same
direction as the body.

---

What this chapter settles is a word, and the word will now be used without further defence. Coherence
is the agreement of a thing's levels with one another, met from outside; it is the felt rightness of
going the way one goes, met from inside; and those are one condition and not two. It is maintained
rather than achieved, on four conditions. It can be lost in exactly two ways: by a channel that lies,
and by a part that succeeds against its whole.

One of the four conditions has been named and not defined, and it is the one holding the weight —
*measurement*, the contact that could have come out otherwise. That is the next chapter, and it is
the chapter where the most expensive borrowed word in the book gets taken away from the people who
own it.
