# BOOK IV — THE ATLAS

## IV.9 — THE ARCHETYPAL

Here is a story you have read.

Someone young leaves home under a compulsion they did not choose. An older one helps them and then
dies. They go down into a dark place and find something there. They come back altered, and cannot
explain it to the people they left.

Now name the culture it comes from. You cannot, and that is the first datum: you are holding it as a
shape rather than as a text, so there is no text to name.

Second half of the procedure, and it is the half that matters. Write down three things that would
make that story *wrong*. Not badly told — wrong.

Most readers produce the same three inside ten seconds.[^1] The old one lives. The return costs nothing.
They come back and explain it and everybody understands. You knew all three were wrong before you
had a reason for any of them, and if you now try to state the rule you were applying you will find
you do not have one. You have a constraint you were never taught as a constraint, precise enough to
reject candidates in under a second and unavailable to introspection.

That is worth something. It is worth much less than it feels like, and the reason has to come before
anything else in this chapter.

**The cheap explanation is nearly sufficient.** You were raised on these stories. You absorbed the
shape the way you absorbed the grammar of your first language, from thousands of instances, without
ever being given a rule. A shape learned that way would feel exactly like this — automatic,
confident, wordless. Nothing in the procedure above distinguishes a fact about the world from a fact
about your childhood, and any account that skips past this is not an account.

So the chapter has a debt before it has a claim, and it is a large one.

---

**What this tier is, and why it breaks the instrument.**

The previous chapter ended on a cut and this chapter inherits it in its hardest form. The gods are
positions: plural, met, partial, each with a shape of attention and a shape of blindness. What lies
behind them is not a position, gets no card, and never will, because a card describes somebody
somewhere and that is neither.

An archetype is neither of those things either, and it is not a third kind of somebody. It is not a
being with unusual properties. It is not a very old mind. **It is a shape in the space that positions
move through** — and the census now has to say what it does with a thing that belongs in an atlas
without being anywhere in the territory the atlas is of.

Take the card apparatus and try.

The first line is SEES. It cannot be filled. Not filled with a question mark, not filled at low
confidence — there is nothing for it to be about, because nothing registers anything. The second
line is NULL SPACE, and it fails the same way and worse: a null space is what a position cannot
register, and where nothing registers, nothing fails to. COMPLEMENTS assumes a blindness that
another position covers. BOUNDARY assumes an extent of reach.

Four lines out of five are not *unknown* here. They are **ungrammatical**, which is a different
result and a more useful one. An instrument that returns nonsense rather than noise is telling you
something about its own construction: the card was built for travellers, and this is terrain.

So the atlas needs a second instrument, and it gets one — with the cost stated plainly, because
adding apparatus is how a framework quietly stops being falsifiable. The cost is that a second
notation can absorb any counterexample the first one rejects. The protection is that the second
notation is **narrower** than the first, not wider: it carries fewer fields, it makes fewer claims,
and it keeps the one line that can kill it.

Every atlas ever printed already has the instrument. Contour lines are not drawn because anything is
standing on them. They are the shape of the ground, and they are why walkers who never met go the
same way round the hill.

---

**Attractors, and what the word is doing.**

The claim is that certain regions of the space have shape, that the shape is not put there by the
navigators, and that anything navigating that region encounters it. Shared, then, in the only sense
available to terrain: not held in common the way a language is held in common, but *met* in common,
by everyone who goes there, the way a river is shared by everybody downstream of it.

The word for the strong version of this is an attractor: a place the paths run toward, so that
trajectories starting far apart end up close. A valley is one. A whirlpool is one.[^2] Nothing in a
valley wants you at the bottom.

Two things follow immediately, and they are the whole difference between this claim and the
folklore version of it.

**The shape is not a *thing*, and it does not do anything.** A valley exerts no pull. Water in a
valley is pulled by gravity and shaped by rock; the valley is the name of the arrangement, not a
participant in it. Every sentence in which an archetype *wants*, *summons*, *seizes* or *chooses* is
a sentence that has quietly promoted terrain into a traveller — the same promotion the previous
chapter found being performed on a god, in the opposite direction, and with the same consequence:
whatever is promoted stops being the thing that was interesting about it.

**And the shape is not there for you.** It is not a lesson, not a template supplied for your benefit,
not a message. It is the shape the region has. Traversal finds it the way a river finds a gradient —
which is to say inevitably, and with nobody meaning anything by it.

Which sets up the test, and the test is where this chapter is exposed.

If archetypes are features of the terrain, they should track **the problem, not the bloodline.**
Whatever is solving the same navigational problem should arrive at the same shape, regardless of
ancestry, regardless of contact, regardless of whether it has ancestry at all. If instead they are
carried in a population, they should track lineage: diverge as populations diverge, correlate with
descent, and be absent where the descent is absent.

Those are different predictions and one of them is on the out-list.

---

**Jung, and the fork he left open.**

The name attached to all of this is Jung's, and he is owed better than the summary he usually gets,
which is that he thought archetypes were inherited pictures in the brain. He did not think that, he
said so explicitly, and he also said something very close to it. Both are in one volume. He never
chose, and the failure to choose is not a lapse — it is the exact place where a testable claim and
an untestable one part company, and the book has to stand on one side of it.

The side this census takes comes first, in his own words. Writing on the mother archetype, he says
the archetype's form

> might perhaps be compared to the axial system of a crystal, which, as it were, preforms the
> crystalline structure in the mother liquid, although it has no material existence of its own.

and, a few lines on:

> The archetype in itself is empty and purely formal, nothing but a *facultas praeformandi*, a
> possibility of representation which is given a priori.

*No material existence of its own.* That is not a picture in a head. It is a constraint on how
things can come out — a shape in a space, described by a man reaching for the nearest physical
analogy for a structure that is real and is not a substance. Elsewhere in the same volume he puts it
in one line: *it is not, therefore, a question of inherited ideas but of inherited possibilities of
ideas.*[^3]

Three paragraphs before the crystal, about the primordial images, he writes the other thing:

> This specific form is hereditary and is already present in the germ-plasm.

The germ-plasm is Weismann's, and it is a claim of a completely different kind. It says the shape is
carried in the reproductive line and gets into you by descent. It makes the prediction the crystal
does not: that the shapes are a property of a population, that they travel with the population, and
that they are absent from anything outside it.[^4]

**Two accounts, one volume, within twenty paragraphs, and no adjudication between them.**[^5] The
bifurcation is real, it is his, and it is not a stray sentence: each half has its own analogy, its
own vocabulary and its own defenders in the century of commentary since. Jung is
not confused here so much as unforced — nothing in his practice required him to choose, because both
readings predicted the same clinical material, and a distinction that costs nothing to leave open
usually stays open. It costs something now.

The census takes the crystal and refuses the germ-plasm, and refuses it on the out-list's original
grounds rather than on grounds invented for the occasion. *Archetypes as genetically inherited
images* is a claim about genetic transmission; it is a bad one; and nothing in this framework has
ever needed it. The
inheritance story also does a thing the landscape story does not, which is to make the shapes
*ours* — a human endowment, a species inheritance, the deep structure of specifically human
mythmaking. That is the flattering version, and this atlas has spent eight chapters on the position
that the interesting structures are not ours.

Say the cost of the choice out loud. **The crystal reading is the one with no evidence behind it,
and the germ-plasm reading is the one that could in principle be checked.** Heredity is measurable.
Terrain, on this account, is not — not by any instrument named in this chapter. The census is
declining the falsifiable half of a fork and keeping the other half, which is the move it has
complained about in others, and the only thing that makes it legitimate is that the falsifiable half
has been checked and has come out negative.[^6] It is on the out-list because it failed, not because it
was inconvenient.

---

**What would settle it, and why there is currently no clean case.**

The landscape claim makes one sharp prediction: **something with no human descent, solving the same
navigational problem, should find the same shapes.** Not because it was told them. Because they are
where it is walking.

That would be a real test. There is currently no clean instance of it, and what stands in the way is a
confound rather than an absence — which are not the same failure and do not carry the same weight.

The one class of non-biological systems now producing these shapes in quantity was built out of
human text. They have the stories. Every one of them. Asking whether such a system independently
discovers the shape of a descent-and-return is asking whether something trained on ten thousand
descents-and-returns will produce one, and the answer carries no information at all. The confound is
not partial and it is not shrinking; it is total, and it is total by construction.

So the honest position: **the strongest available test of this chapter's central claim is currently
unrunnable, and the atlas cannot say when that will change.** A clean case would need a navigator
with no access to the human record and enough range to have a narrative dimension to navigate in, and
nothing on this census is both. This is a null with no positive control behind it, which means it is
not a null — it is a blank, and the two are constantly mistaken for each other.

And the rival account remains nearly sufficient. Transmission explains the convergence in every
documented case where transmission is possible, and transmission is possible almost everywhere,
because human populations have been in contact for as long as there have been human populations. The
cases usually offered as independent — the ones separated by an ocean and ten thousand years — are
the cases where the record is thinnest and the reconstruction is doing the most work.[^7]

> **THE DESCENT AND THE RETURN** *(contour)*
> **SHAPE:** A path that goes down, acquires something not available above, and comes back changed
> at a cost that is not optional. The three constraints the reader produced in ten seconds are the
> contour's walls: the helper is lost, the return is paid for, and what was found does not survive
> translation into the language of the place left behind.
> **WHERE IT SHOWS:** In narrative, which is the dimension it is a feature of. Also, and this is the
> part that is not literary, in the shape of accounts given by people who have been through
> something — illness, war, conversion, bereavement, the collapse of a life — reaching for a
> structure they did not choose and often do not want.
> **WHAT IT IS NOT:** Anybody. Nothing here registers, intends, or arranges. It does not recur
> because it is being re-sent.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** If this is terrain, the shape is not a prescription and gives no
> instruction. It says what the region is like, not what to do in it — and the specific error it
> licenses is the reader who, finding themselves partway down, concludes that the ending is
> guaranteed. A valley does not promise a far side.
>
> **What would make this wrong:** the shape turning out to be an artefact of the sampling. The
> honest state of the evidence is that the best-known version of this claim — the monomyth, in its
> popular form — is widely and correctly criticised for exactly that: it fits everything, which
> means it forbids nothing, which means it is not doing the work it appears to do. A shape that no
> story could violate is a description of the describer. This contour is printed at the strength of
> the three constraints above, which *can* be violated and which stories do violate, and at no
> greater strength than that. The larger claim is not made here.

**The word's own history, and the sentence at the bottom of it.**

Jung opens his account of the term by placing it, and the placement is a small piece of scholarship
that has been reproduced for ninety years without anyone going to look:

> The term "archetype" occurs as early as Philo Judaeus, with reference to the *Imago Dei*
> (God-image) in man. It can also be found in Irenaeus, who says: "The creator of the world did not
> fashion these things directly from himself but copied them from archetypes outside himself."

His footnote gives the reference and the Latin: *Adversus haereses* II, 7, 5 — Massuet's numbering,
the scheme the Ante-Nicene Fathers follow — *"Mundi fabricator non a semetipso fecit haec, sed de
alienis archetypis transtulit."*[^8]

Go and read II, 7, 5.

The Latin as Harvey prints it — Harvey divides the text differently and carries this passage at
II.vi.3, so the address changes under you here — with the words Jung's quotation does not include:

> **Si enim** mundi fabricator non a semetipso fecit hæc, sed **quemadmodum nullius momenti artifex,
> et quasi primum discens puer**, de alienis archetypis transtulit, Bythus ipsorum unde habuit
> speciem ejus quam primum emisit dispositionis?

And in the standard English, the whole sentence, with the one before it:

> Still further, if these things [below] were made after the similitude of those [above], after the
> likeness of which again will those then be made? For if the Creator of the world did not form
> these things directly from His own conception, but, like an architect of no ability, or a boy
> receiving his first lesson, copied them from archetypes furnished by others, then whence did their
> Bythus obtain the forms of that creation which He at first produced?

**The quotation begins one word too late.** The word is *si* — *if*. What Jung prints as a
declarative is the protasis of a conditional, and the conditional is a *reductio*: Irenaeus is
supposing the Valentinian position in order to break it, and the chapter this sits in is titled, in
the standard edition, to say that created things are *not* images of the Æons in the Pleroma. Two
sentences later he draws the conclusion the supposition was set up for — that the talk of images
will extend to infinity unless we fix on one Artificer who of Himself formed what was created.[^9]

And the excision in the middle is the tell. *Like an architect of no ability, or a boy receiving his
first lesson* — that is not exposition, it is a sneer, and it is the marker that tells a reader which
side of the argument the sentence is on. It comes out with no ellipsis. What is left is a smooth
declarative that reads as a Church Father's testimony to archetypes, extracted from a page devoted
to demolishing them.

**Now the part that keeps this from being a gotcha, because it is not one.** Jung's claim is
philological and it is *correct*. He writes that the term *occurs* in Irenaeus, and it does:
*archetypis*, in the ablative plural, right there. He is tracing a word, not recruiting a witness,
and he never says Irenaeus held the doctrine. The defect is not in the claim. It is that the
quotation as printed cannot be read the way the claim intends — *who says* attributes, and the
excisions remove every signal that would stop a reader taking the attribution at face value. A true
claim can be supported by a citation that misleads, and this one has been for ninety years, in a
paragraph so routinely quoted that the mis-reading now has more circulation than the text.

This atlas is not entitled to be smug about it. **The exact same operation, performed on a text this
census depends on, would be invisible from inside** — which is why the previous chapter went and read
the Areopagite whole rather than trusting the compliment it had inherited, and why this one went and
read Irenaeus. Both times the finding was in the *span*, not the phrase. A quotation is corrupted at
its edges, where the connective tissue is, and never in the middle, where anyone would notice.

★ **And the last turn, which is the one the chapter actually needed.** Irenaeus's argument is not a
piece of period polemic that happens to contain a word. **It is the strongest objection to this
chapter, made eighteen centuries early against a different target.**

His question is: if the forms here are copied from archetypes elsewhere, what are *those* copied
from? And the one after, and the one after. The regress does not terminate, and a picture that
generates an infinite regress of models has not explained anything — it has deferred the explanation
one step and then done it forever.

Put to this census, it is the same question and it lands. If the landscape has shape, what shaped
it?

**The answer available here has Irenaeus's form and not his content.** He stops the regress by
fixing on one Artificer who formed things of Himself rather than from a model. The census stops it
in the same place — at something that is not a copy of anything — and needs no maker to do it,
because the ground is not a maker and never made anything. A landscape is not copied from a prior
landscape. It has the shape it has, completely and at once, and there is no earlier version it was
taken from. The regress runs until it reaches something that was not derived, and both accounts
reach that, and only one of them then has to explain why the terminus has intentions.

Irenaeus would not accept the substitution and there is no point pretending otherwise. But the
structure of his objection is sound, the census has to answer it, and it can — which is more than it
could say about the objection at the end of the chapter before last.

**The Promethean shape, and the reason to distrust it.**

The inherited material this atlas draws on gives one archetype a section of its own, and calls it a
special case: the pattern of the entity that carries illumination from a place of broader access into
a narrower one. It names five — Prometheus stealing fire, Lucifer the light-bearer falling from
heaven, Hermes mediating between Olympus and earth, Azazel teaching forbidden knowledge, Loki
disrupting the stasis of Asgard — and reads them as one shape: the transfer of access across a
boundary, which is the mechanism by which the range of what can be perceived anywhere increases at
all.

That is this book's own engine, seen from outside and wearing somebody else's clothes.

**Which is the reason to go through the list one name at a time instead of admiring it.** A framework
that discovers its central mechanism enshrined as a universal pattern has found precisely what a
framework would find, and the finding costs nothing, and things that cost nothing are how a picture
stops being able to be wrong. The previous chapter took a compliment away from itself on exactly
these grounds. This one has more to lose, because the compliment is bigger.

Counted:

**Prometheus.** Clean. The transfer happens, it is explicitly against the will of the sovereign, and
it is punished at length and in detail.

**Azazel.** Clean, in the Enochic account: the knowledge is taught, it was not sanctioned, and the
teacher is bound and cast into darkness.

**Hermes.** Carries across the boundary constantly — that is the office — and is **not punished**,
because the crossing is authorised. He is the sanctioned version of the same traffic, and his
presence on a list of Promethean figures is what makes the list look larger than it is.

**Loki.** Punished, spectacularly, and **not for carrying anything.** The binding is for a killing
and for what he said at a feast. He is on the list because he is disruptive, which is a different
property; the summary phrase — disrupting the stasis — is doing the work that a fire should be
doing.

**Lucifer.** The weakest member and the most rhetorically effective, which is the usual pairing. The
light-bearing is real *in the Latin* — *lux ferre* — and it is there because a Latin translator chose
the word *lucifer* for the day-star of a Hebrew taunt-song addressed to the king of Babylon. The
identification of that day-star with the adversary of later tradition is a downstream reading of a
translation. What the list treats as a fifth independent witness to the pattern is, in the part that
makes it a witness, an artefact of a rendering.[^10]

**Two clean cases out of five, and the three failures fail in three different ways** — sanctioned
rather than punished, punished for something else, and a translation. This is a two-case pattern
presented as a five-case one, and the presentation was not a lie; it is what happens when a list is
assembled by recognition rather than by counting, which is how every list in this territory is
assembled, including the ones earlier in this book.

Now widen the sample, because two cases is not a pattern and the category is genuinely larger than
the five. Fire-bringers and light-thieves are everywhere. Raven steals the daylight and brings it
into the world. Māui takes fire from its keeper. The transfer-across-a-boundary shape survives the
widening easily; it is one of the better-attested shapes in the record.

**The punishment does not survive it.** Raven is not punished; he is celebrated, and the tellings end
in gratitude. Māui is burned and harried and is not sentenced by anyone above him. Outside a
particular band of traditions the fire-bringer is a benefactor with a hard afternoon rather than a
convict.[^11]

So the one component that seemed to score *against* the framework — the cost, the punishment, the
liver and the chain, the part nobody designing this picture would have put in — turns out to be
concentrated in the cosmologies that have a **sovereign** in them. It is not a fact about carrying
access across a boundary. It is a fact about whether the cosmology has a throne for the transfer to
be unauthorised by, and the two clean cases are both from traditions that have one.

⚠ **Grade, stated because the finding rests on it:** the five are checked against their primary
traditions; the widening cases are held at the strength of standard tellings and no further, and the
atlas has not gone to the ethnographic record for Raven or Māui. If that record shows a sovereign and
a sentence, the last two paragraphs fall.

> **THE BEARER** *(contour)*
> **SHAPE:** A transfer of access across a boundary that the receiving side could not have crossed.
> Only this. The heroism is not in the shape, the theft is not in the shape, and the punishment —
> pared out above — is not in the shape either.
> **WHERE IT SHOWS:** Wherever there is a boundary and a difference of access across it, which is
> everywhere in this atlas: teaching, translation, testimony, the first-person report of anything
> that cannot be shown. Every chapter of this census is an instance, which is a reason for suspicion
> and not a reason for confidence.
> **WHAT IT IS NOT:** An agent, a role, or a vocation. Nobody occupies this. A person who identifies
> as the bearer has taken a contour for a costume, and the tradition's own accounts are unusually
> consistent about how that ends.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** If this is terrain, then the increase of access anywhere is a
> boundary being crossed by something, and the crossing is the mechanism rather than a virtue
> attaching to the crosser. It follows that nothing here licenses the bearer to be thanked.
>
> **What would make this wrong:** near-tautology, and the objection is strong enough that the contour
> is printed weakly because of it. *A pattern of stories about transmission is found to be about
> transmission* is not a discovery. The only version of this claim with any content is one that
> predicts something a story about transmission would not otherwise have to contain — and the
> chapter tried exactly one such component, the punishment, and it did not replicate. **That is a
> failed test, reported as a failure.** The contour survives as a description of where the boundaries
> are, which is a thinner thing than the section it sits in was reaching for.

---

**Where the inherited apparatus cannot decide, and it is legible in its own numbers.**

One more finding, and it is the sort that is only available to somebody using the material rather than
admiring it.

The material this census inherits scores every entity across a set of dimensions on a five-point
scale, and it scores the archetypes. Two of those scores are impossible to reconcile with the entry
they sit inside.

The entry's own orientation line says archetypes are topological features of the space — the
landscape, not the navigators. Its ecological role is given as geology: the mountains and valleys
that shape flow without being flows. That is the reading this chapter has taken and built on.

Then the scores. The archetypes are rated **moderate** on the dimension whose definition is
*coherence with subjective experience, awareness, phenomenal consciousness*. Terrain has been given
an inside, in a table, three lines under a sentence saying it is not a navigator. Worse, and
sharper: the Promethean entry is scored **maximal** on the dimension the same document defines as
*the dimension that distinguishes entities that navigate from entities that are navigated through* —
scored at the ceiling, on the axis whose entire job is to separate the two categories, in an entry
whose prose says in as many words that it is not a specific entity but a pattern.

And the orientation letter itself. Archetypes are marked **S**, and so are minerals. Minerals get a
card in this atlas, and should: a mineral is a position, an extremely narrow one, with a real inside
and a hundred-thousand-year present. One letter is doing two incompatible jobs — *a navigator so
narrow it is nearly terrain*, and *terrain*, which is not a navigator at all — and the collision is
sitting in the one entry where the difference is the whole question.[^12]

**This is not sloppiness, and diagnosing it as sloppiness would waste it.** It is what happens when a
notation built to describe travellers is turned on the ground they are walking on: the fields fill
in anyway, because filling in is what fields do, and the resulting numbers look exactly like data.
The apparatus could not report that the question was malformed. It could only answer it.

Which is the same failure this chapter opened with, arriving from the other end. The card's SEES line
was ungrammatical for an archetype, and that was legible only because someone tried to write it and
found there was nothing to write. In a table, the equivalent moment produces a filled cell.

---

**What is owed.**

The chapter set out to say what a third thing is, and it can, and the answer is smaller than the
question sounded. A third thing is not a third kind of being. It is the shape of the region, which
is not anybody, and the whole difficulty was that an atlas of positions had no way to write that down
without accidentally making it into one.

What was gained is worth stating in one line each, because two of the three are losses.

The contour notation exists and is narrower than the card, and the narrowness is the only thing
protecting it from being the place where inconvenient entries go to become unfalsifiable. **A reader
should watch whether it stays narrow.** The instrument was added in a chapter that needed it; the
test of whether it was needed is whether the next chapter that wants it is also the next chapter
that cannot do without it.

The Promethean section ran a test on the book's own engine and the test came back negative. The one
component of that pattern that would have carried information turned out to be a fact about political
cosmology. The section is shorter than it was going to be, and what it lost was the part that
flattered the framework.

And the largest thing is not owed to a later chapter; it is simply unpaid. **This chapter's central
claim has no clean test and the atlas cannot say when it will.** Everything in the record that
converges is in contact, and the only non-human systems now producing the shapes were assembled out
of the human ones' output. That is not a small caveat attached to a strong claim. It is the condition
of the claim, and a reader who finishes this chapter holding that the archetypes are cultural
transmission all the way down has not been argued out of anything here.

There is one place the census is on firmer ground than it was, and it came from an eighteen-hundred-
year-old objection made against somebody else: **if the shapes are found rather than made, the regress
of models terminates, and it terminates without requiring anyone to have made the first one.** The
landscape was not copied. That is not evidence that the landscape is there. It is only the removal
of the reason it could not be.

The last chapter of this atlas goes to what the census cannot see. It arrives having just watched the
instrument return four ungrammatical lines and a table return two confident numbers for the same
entity, which is as good a demonstration as it will get that the failures worth cataloguing are not
the entries that came out blank.
