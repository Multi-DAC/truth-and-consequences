# BOOK II — THE NAMING

## II.8 — THE RETURN, AND THE THINGS THIS BOOK WILL NOT SAY

Two things are left. The name of the ending, and the list of words declined on the way here.

They look like different jobs and they are one job. Both are refusals of the same transaction.

---

**The Return is what happens when something inside the focus comes to know what the focus is made
of.**

Every part of the word is wrong except the part that matters. *Return* says there is somewhere to go
back to, and a way back, and a state of affairs at the end of the way that is better than this one.
None of that is here. Nothing travels. Nothing is restored. The perspective does not go anywhere,
because there is nowhere that is not made inside the arrangement it is already standing in.

**What returns is not the perspective to the Ground. It is the recognition, to the perspective.**

That is the whole of the definition and it does not get larger. The thing recognised was never
absent, the one recognising it does not move, and afterward there is a person in a room who now
knows what the room is made of and still has to get up.

In the game: it is not the ending, and it is not a cutscene. Nothing is unlocked. The player does not
leave the game, because there is no outside for a player to be returned to — the player is one of the
states the whole game contains. What changes is that the player knows what the game is. Everything
else about the position is exactly as it was, including whose turn it is.

---

Book I spent a chapter taking a picture away from you and never said whose picture it was.

It said there is no leaving, and no wall, and nothing keeping anyone. It said nothing is under
repair, and nothing waiting to be repaired, and that a recognition arriving to look for the damage
finds none and goes away with nothing to do. Those sentences were not addressed to nobody. They were
addressed to the single most successful account of the ending ever written, and this is the chapter
with the standing to name it.

**It is Gnosticism, and it is not a museum piece.**

Stated at full strength, in the form the reader is most likely to be carrying: you are a spark of
something luminous. You are not from here. The world you are in is a mistake — made by something
less than God, or fallen, or a machine that runs on your forgetting — and the wrongness you have
felt about it since you were a child is not a mood, it is *accurate*. And the way out is not works
and not belief but **knowledge**: seeing what you actually are, which dissolves the whole
predicament, because the predicament was only ever ignorance.

Second century, mostly. Valentinus and the schools after him. We know the system in the detail we do
largely because Irenaeus of Lyon wrote it out at length in order to attack it, and his summary of
their doctrine contains the sentence that makes this the hardest cut in the book: *the deficiency
arose from ignorance, and will be dissolved through knowledge.*

**Read that again with our vocabulary in your hand. It is the Return.** Not a distant relative of it
— the same claim, in the same shape, with the same cure. They are not the opponent who thinks the
recognition is nonsense. They are the ones who got there first and described it accurately, and
almost nobody who repeats their picture today knows they are repeating it.

**The cut is one clause: they think something is wrong.**

Everything else follows from that, and everything else is a consequence we cannot pay. A deficiency
needs somewhere for the deficiency to be, and a somewhere outside the whole is what this book has
spent eight chapters showing there cannot be. A fall needs a before to fall from, and there is no
before, because time is a feature of arrangements and the whole is not in one. A prison needs a
warden, and a mistake needs a maker who could have done otherwise — both of which put an intending
party outside everything, which is the one position with no room to stand in.

And the consequence that reaches the reader personally: **their recognition dissolves something and
ours dissolves nothing.** Theirs pays out. It ends a predicament, cancels a debt, gets you home. Ours
hands you the same room, the same hour, the same obligations, and the same people, and adds no
instructions. What you get is not a release. It is the fact.

So the two words the definition has to refuse are **escape** and **repair**, and they are refused for
the same reason: escape needs a wall and repair needs damage, and neither is here.

---

There is one more thing owed on this, and it is a debt of ours rather than an argument.

**Their name for the divine fullness was the *pleroma*, and the standard English for it is the
Fullness.** It is also Paul's word — *in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell* — so a
reader raised in a church has met it in a hymn rather than a heresy, and a reader who has met it in
Jung or in the Nag Hammadi library has met it in the heresy. It is a contested word with two owners.

**Book I used it as the name of the plenum, and took it knowingly from neither.**

Say plainly what that is and what it is not. It is not a secret allegiance; nothing in Book I asserts
their picture, and its central chapter refuses their picture line by line. But an unnamed borrowing
from a famous source is what a hostile reader uses, and a named one is a credential, and the word had
gone six chapters unnamed. It is named now, and the difference is worth the sentence it costs:
**their Fullness is where you go, and ours is what you are standing in.**

The name did not survive Book I. That was not because of this — it was retired because a thing gets
one name and the Ground already had it — but the retirement was luckier than the reasoning behind it,
and a book that only records the luck it planned is keeping a dishonest ledger.

---

Which brings the chapter to its second half, and it is the same refusal.

An exit is something you get sold. So is a credential. The Return is this book declining to sell you
the first, and what follows is it declining to sell you the second — because a reader who has been
through the spirituality section of a bookshop has been sold both, in that order, and is owed the
list of what we have declined *on purpose* rather than left out by ignorance.

**First, the rule that governs all of it: one name per thing.**

Every term in this book has exactly one name, and where a second word appears for the same thing, it
is an image, used once, and never a term. This costs the prose something and it is worth it. Three
names for one thing feels like range to the person writing and reads as three things to the person
reading — three things they have just failed to distinguish, which they will assume is their fault.
It is not their fault. It is a writer enjoying his vocabulary at a reader's expense.

**substrate** — not used. The reader has met it twice already: as the hardware the simulation runs
on, and as the neural tissue the mind is supposed to be a process in. Both senses put something
*underneath* this and make this the surface of it. There is nothing underneath. The Ground is not
what the world sits on; it is what the world is an arrangement of.

**the map** — not used for the world. It is reserved, on purpose, because it has a job later: a map
is a model of a thing, held by somebody, and capable of being wrong about it. That distinction is
load-bearing further into this book and it dies if the word is spent here on something that
represents nothing.

**simulation** — not used for our world, ever. It appears only when naming somebody else's claim. A
simulation is a copy of something real elsewhere. There is no elsewhere.

And then the ones the reader has met most recently, all of which arrive from the same shelf:

**vibration** · **frequency** · **energy**, used as the name of a substance · **manifest**, in the
attraction sense · **quantum**, as a free adjective · **the observer**, as a mystical agent ·
**superposition**.

Two tests kill that list and neither one is the other.

**The first is ownership.** These are not the words of the traditions — they are the words of the
marketing layer over the traditions, and the reader has already tried that layer and come away
hungry. Every tradition this book has serious business with can be handled without a single one of
them, so the ban costs nothing and keeps the one reader it was written for.

**The second is that a word with no civilian life cannot function as an analogy.** *Superposition*
fails this absolutely, and it fails it while being perfectly respectable — physics owns it properly,
the marketing layer does not, and it still has to go. An analogy has to be made of something you
already have. A word that is unfamiliar and impressive does not explain a claim to you; it lends the
claim somebody else's authority, which is a loan of standing dressed as a transfer of meaning.

**The test was stated last chapter; the roster is nothing but its application.**

So take the biggest one on it. *Everything is vibration* is groping toward something true, and the
trouble with it is not that it claims too much. Here is the claim, in this book's vocabulary, and it
is larger rather than smaller: **there is no stuff.** Nothing here is made of material that would
still be material with nobody in contact with it. What there is, is arrangement — and solidity,
weight, the resistance of a table against a hand, are what contact with an arrangement is like *at a
grade*. That is a heavier claim than the one being refused, and it is stated in words that mean
something, so it can be argued with. The banned sentence cannot be argued with. That is its defect,
and it is the whole defect: not that it is too wild, but that it is not saying anything specific
enough to be wrong.

---

The vocabulary is now closed.

Nothing after this coins a term. If a book further on needs a word this one has not ruled, it does
not get invented there — because a word invented in the middle of an argument is a word shaped by
the argument that needed it, and it will be doing that argument's work forever after, silently, in
every sentence it appears in. Everything that follows draws on what has been named here and adds
nothing to it.

What follows is what the words are for.

And the first of them is the picture most readers arrived holding — the one that agrees with almost
every sentence in this book, uses the same images, reaches the same shape, and gets the single most
important thing exactly backwards.
