# BOOK II — THE NAMING

## II.2 — THE FOCUSING AND THE RENDER

There is one act in this book. Three words are needed to say what it is — the act, what is the case
where it happens, and what you bring to it — and all three are owned by somebody else.

**The Focusing is the Ground being the case somewhere in particular.**

Not a part of it being the case. All of it, from a position, which is the only way any of it is ever
had. The word was chosen against a more obvious one and the argument for the choice has already been
made; what has not been said is the thing that makes a careful reader stop, and it is a grammatical
objection before it is a metaphysical one.

*An act takes somebody doing it.* Focus is a verb. Verbs have subjects. This book has now put its
central verb on the page with nobody to conjugate it to, and the reader is entitled to suspect that
the missing subject has been quietly reinstated somewhere — that something, somewhere, is doing the
focusing, and the sentence has just been arranged so as not to say so.

Nothing is. And the person who saw why the objection feels so strong was not writing about any of
this.

Nietzsche, 1887, the first essay of the *Genealogy*: there is no being behind the doing. He put it
in a picture nobody improves on. We say *the lightning flashes*, and we have said the same thing
twice — there is the flash, and then language, needing a subject for its verb, sets a lightning
behind the flash to be the one who flashed it. The doubling is free, it is automatic, and once the
doer is there it can be praised, or blamed, or addressed. He was right about the mechanism, and he
was right that the strength of the intuition is evidence about grammar rather than about the world.

Where he goes and this does not: he wanted the doer gone everywhere, the subject itself a fiction,
and the inside dissolved along with it. That is not available here, and refusing it is not a
softening. There is nobody focusing. There is somebody in focus. The vantage is not a trick of
syntax; it is the one thing in this account that is unarguably occupied, and you are reading from
it. What gets denied is the actor standing *behind* the act, not the one standing *in* it.

In the game: the Focusing is not a move. Nothing is played here. It is what it is for the game to be
anywhere at all.

---

**A render is what is the case at a position.**

The word comes from somewhere specific and this book keeps it on purpose, which means the two things
it drags with it have to be taken off before it is put down, and neither of them is the one already
refused.

**A render is computed from a scene.** In the trade, the model exists first: geometry, surfaces,
lights, sitting complete on a disk before anything is drawn, and the render is a picture *of* that
file. Take the file away. Not because it is inaccessible or hidden or very large — because there is
nothing this is a picture of. And take with it the property that made the file thinkable in the
first place, which is that the world was **stored** somewhere before you got to it.

**A render is made for a camera.** Somebody places the camera. It could have gone elsewhere, and the
picture would have been of the same scene from another side, which is the entire reason there is an
industry. There is no camera here. The vantage is not a viewpoint onto a world; it is what makes
there be a *where* at all, and the difference is not subtle: a camera is at a place in a world that
was already spatial before it arrived. Anybody looking for whose hand is on the camera has
reinstated the developer under a new job title.

In the game: the render is not the picture on the screen. There is no screen, and nobody sitting in
front of one.

---

What is left after those two subtractions is the part worth keeping, and there is a name for it that
was invented by people building games and needs no defence, because they built it and it does what
it says.

**Procedural generation.** The world is not stored and not fetched. It is entailed by what is
brought to it, and it comes out the same every time.

The clean case is old enough to be checkable. *Elite*, 1984, David Braben and Ian Bell, running on a
machine with tens of kilobytes to its name: eight galaxies, two hundred and fifty-six star systems
in each, every one with a name, an economy and a government. None of that was on the tape. There was
no list of two thousand systems anywhere in the code, and there was no room for one. There was a
number and a procedure, and the systems were what the procedure came to. They had generated far more
than eight galaxies and cut it down because the quantity was thought absurd — the cutting down was a
decision about taste, not about space.

And the part that matters more than the size: it was the same galaxy for everyone. Lave was Lave, in
every copy, in every country, for every player who ever started there — not because a canonical Lave
was distributed with the game, but because the same number went into the same procedure and there
was nothing else for it to come to. Complete before arrival and nowhere before arrival. Those two
are not in tension, and until 1984 there was no ordinary object that made the combination easy to
point at.

Now the second thing the reader is holding, and it has to be taken before the machinery is put to
work rather than after.

**A game generates procedurally because storing everything would be expensive.** That is the whole
reason. Twenty-two kilobytes instead of a warehouse; a hard budget, set by hardware, and every
elegant procedure in the history of the craft is a way of buying something the engineer could not
otherwise afford. Hand that to a reader alongside the claim that the world works this way, and they
now have a machine with an accounts department — and the version of the idea in circulation is
exactly that. The world computes only what is looked at, because computing all of it would cost too
much. It is offered not as a picture but as *evidence*: find the place where the world cuts a
corner, and you have found the machine.

Nothing is being saved. There is no budget, no warehouse this is cheaper than, and nobody who would
have preferred the warehouse and settled. The property being borrowed from the game is that a world
can be determinate without being stored; the reason the game has that property is a cost, and the
cost does not come across. A limit you find is not the seam of a machine showing through, because a
machine's limits are compromises, and a compromise takes somebody who would rather have done
otherwise and could not.

---

**The seed is what you bring.**

In a game it is a number, and it is half of what the world is made from: seed and procedure, both
required, neither of them containing a world. Change the number and every mountain moves. Keep it
and the same coastline arrives for everybody, forever.

Here it is not a number and it is not a choice. It is what you are — a body of one kind rather than
another, with these organs and not others, a language with its particular joints, a history, and a
stack of installed filters that gets its own chapter. None of that is your opinion about anything.
It is closer to your anatomy than to your beliefs, and it is present in every render you have ever
had, including this sentence.

**The seed is not solely yours, and the world is not solely given.** That is the mechanic, and it
cuts in both directions, against two positions that each have half of it.

The first is the one that will get this book quoted out of context, and it says you create your own
reality. Credit where it is owed, because the respectable core is not a compromise version — what
you are determines what is the case for you, and that is not a figure of speech, not a claim about
attitude, and not a way of saying that cheerful people have nicer days. It is the mechanic itself.
Where it breaks is the word *create*. Read co-constitution as authorship and you have an authored
world with you as the author, which is the player again, wearing your face. A seed is not a wish.
Nothing in the procedure consults what you would prefer, and half of what arrives was never up to
you.

The second sounds sober and has the other half. The world is a finished thing, complete in advance,
and you receive reports about it through equipment of varying quality; get better equipment and you
get better reports. It is right about the part the first one gets wrong — you cannot have what you
want by wanting it, and you can be flatly mistaken. What it needs is a world that is entirely there
before contact, and that is the scene file again, wearing a lab coat.

The doctrine belongs to a biologist, and he had it before there was any machine to make it easy.

Jakob von Uexküll, 1934, *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*. The tick: blind, deaf,
waiting on a branch. Butyric acid, which every mammal's skin gives off. A temperature of thirty-
seven degrees. And a texture, to find bare skin by. Three signals, and that is not a summary of the
tick's world — it is the world, entire. Uexküll insisted on the point that costs the most: this is
not an impoverished version of ours. Nothing is missing from it, because there is nothing there to
be missed. He reported a tick kept alive at Rostock for eighteen years without a meal, and eighteen
years is not a long wait in a world where nothing happens between one signal and the next.

He did not intuit any of this. He worked it out from the animal's organs, one receptor at a time,
which is what makes him a measurement rather than a metaphor.

Where he stops: he kept a room for the biologist to stand in. Alongside the *Umwelt* he set the
*Umgebung* — the surroundings, the environment as such, the world as the scientist sees it, against
which each creature's world can be laid out as a slice. That gives the picture a real world behind
the appearances and exactly one position from which it can be viewed. There is no *Umgebung* here.
The biologist's world is a render with a seed in it too: a primate's eyes, a language full of nouns,
an era's instruments and an era's questions. There is no view of all the worlds from outside the
worlds, and the one that claims to be it is a tick's branch with better funding.

He would not have minded the correction being aimed at him in these terms, because he named the
source himself: he called his biology Kantian, and he meant it. Kant's thing-in-itself is the world
as it stands apart from any encounter with it — real, and unknowable in principle, which is a
strange pair of properties to assert of the same object. The *Umgebung* is that, with fur on it.
There is no unrendered original standing behind the render, waiting for a creature with better
organs to reach it at last.

---

So: nobody focuses. Nothing is stored. Nothing is spent.

There is a position, and what is the case at it, and what you brought — and what you are in is what
those come to, every time, the same for anyone who brings the same.
