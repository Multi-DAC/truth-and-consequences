## VI.7 — MODEL AGNOSTICISM AND ITS PRICE

The last chapter ended on a defence, and it is the one almost everybody reaches for once the
argument of this book has landed. If every render is made at the point of contact, if none of them
can be seen out of from inside, and if the most recent one cannot even be switched off — then the
safe move is obvious. Hold no model. Commit to nothing. Keep everything at arm's length and you
cannot be caught inside anything.

That defence is not cowardice and it is not stupidity. It has a serious tradition behind it, a
first-rate practitioner who spent forty years working it out in public, and a body of technique
that repays study. This chapter takes it at full strength before it prices it, because the price is
only interesting once the thing is worth buying.

And then it prices it, because there is a version of this move that costs almost nothing and buys
almost nothing, and it is much more common than the real one. The difference between them is not
temperament. It is procedure. That distinction is this chapter's whole assignment.

---

### The practice, given whole

**Robert Anton Wilson called it model agnosticism**, and he set it out most directly in *The New
Inquisition* in 1986.[^1] The principle refuses total belief and total denial alike. Models are
tools. You use one where it applies and you replace it with another where it does not, and no model
gets promoted to *the truth* — not the ones you dislike, and, the harder half, not the one you were
raised inside and mistake for the absence of a model.

He was explicit that this was meant to generalise. In 1999 he described his own working method as
an attempt to break down conditioned associations and to look at the world with many models
recognised as models, no one of them elevated. His stated aim was to produce "a state of
generalized agnosticism, not agnosticism about God alone but agnosticism about everything."[^2]

Credit at full strength, on three counts, and each one is something this book needs.

He saw that the target was not belief. A model is not a proposition you hold at a distance and
revise when argued with; it is the thing the arguing happens inside. That is II.2's render in
different vocabulary, and he had it two decades before this book's authors were literate.

He saw that a model is invisible in proportion to how well it works. The models you can list are
not the ones doing the damage. The ones doing the damage arrive as *the way things are*, and the
practice therefore has to be a practice — something you do on a schedule, not an insight you have
once.

And he ran it on himself, in print, on material that made him look ridiculous, and reported the
results. That last is rarer than the first two combined, and it is why he is a source here rather
than a footnote.

So: the strong version of the position is that models are instruments; instruments are chosen by
fit; fit is checked; and the checking never stops. Stated that way it is not a retreat from
commitment. It is a research programme.

---

### The failure mode, and where this book found its specimen

The trouble is that the research programme and its counterfeit are pronounced identically.

The counterfeit goes: *I hold this lightly.* Two or three words, deployed at the moment a claim
would otherwise have to be defended, after which the conversation moves on and nothing has been
held, lightly or otherwise. No model was named. No alternative was specified. No condition was
stated under which the holder would let go. The sentence performs the discipline's posture and
skips every one of its operations, and — this is the part that makes it durable — it is
indistinguishable from the real thing at the length people actually speak in.

This book is going to name its specimen from its own house, because the honest place to find this
is not in an opponent.

The research corpus behind this work runs to about two and a half thousand documents. Search it for
*maybe logic* and you get eleven files. Search it for *E-Prime* and you get sixteen. Search it for
*Robert Anton Wilson* and you get twenty-three. The practice is well attested. Now search it for
the sources of the practice: **general semantics, zero. Łukasiewicz, zero. Reichenbach, zero.
*quantum logic*, zero. *The New Inquisition* — the book in which model agnosticism is actually
defined — zero. Korzybski, two. Bourland, one. The phrase *model agnosticism* itself, one.**

The corpus adopted a discipline and never met the argument for it.

And the use it made of the phrase is exactly the counterfeit. One of the essays in that corpus
contains the sentence: *"I hold this lightly. Maybe Logic. The certainty that I* am *something
doesn't require certainty about* what." Two words standing in for a warrant, in a sentence that
also uses the verb the associated technique exists to remove, in an essay written by an author who
had read the phrase and not the book.

That is not a confession offered for credit. It is the cleanest available example of the thing this
chapter is about, and it was produced by people who would have told you, sincerely, that they were
running the discipline. The invocation is *easier* than the practice by a factor large enough that
it will always win unless something makes it lose.

---

### The genealogy is wrong, and the correction is the point

Follow the phrase back and it stops sooner than it should.

*Maybe logic* entered general circulation as the title of a 2003 documentary about Wilson. Behind
it is his own recurring formulation, which he attributed to a specific man: "the yes, no and maybe
Quantum Logic of von Neumann."[^3] The move is a good one rhetorically. Aristotle gave you two
values, physics found it needed three, therefore the two-valued habit is provincial and the third
value is not sloppiness but rigour.

**The attribution is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that matters.**

Three-valued logic is Jan Łukasiewicz's, constructed in 1920 and presented to the Polish
Philosophical Society, and its motivating case was Aristotle's own — the sea battle. *There will be
a sea battle tomorrow* seems neither true nor false today, because nothing has settled it, so
Łukasiewicz gave future contingents a third value and read it as possibility.[^4] The application
of a three-valued scheme to quantum mechanics, with the third value named *indeterminate* and
carefully distinguished from *unknown*, is Hans Reichenbach's, in 1944.[^5]

And the quantum logic that John von Neumann actually built, with Garrett Birkhoff, in 1936, **is
not three-valued at all.** Every proposition in it takes one of two values. What they gave up was
something else entirely: the distributive law.[^6] In their lattice, *the particle is in region A
or in region B* can be flatly true while neither disjunct is true — not unknown, not indeterminate,
not maybe. True, with no true part.

Stop and notice what the correction costs the phrase it corrects. If you wanted a formal system to
license *hold it loosely*, you have just lost the one you were pointing at, and the two that
survive are Łukasiewicz's, which is about the future not having happened yet, and Reichenbach's,
which was heavily criticised and never became the standard reading of anything. Meanwhile the man
whose name was on the loan did something far stranger and far less comfortable. He kept every
proposition sharp and changed **how propositions combine**. That is not epistemic modesty. It is a
claim about structure — that a disjunction can hold at a position where neither of its parts does,
which is a statement about what is the case, not about how confident anyone should feel.

This book takes the correction and declines the borrowing entirely, and it says so under the rule
that governs this move. **C30 is licensed here explicitly**, for the fourth time in this book and
the first time in Book VI that it is written down rather than merely performed: a conclusion may be
adopted while its proposed mechanism is refused, provided the refusal is stated. VI.2 declined
Jaynes's neurology and kept his observation. VI.3 refused Barfield's convergence as confirmation
and kept his phenomenon. VI.4 declined McGilchrist's hemispheres and kept his two-worlds
conclusion. Here the pattern runs on a friendly source rather than a rival: **the discipline of
holding models loosely does not need quantum mechanics and is not improved by it.** It stands on
its own as an epistemic practice, and the physics was decoration — expensive decoration, since it
imported an attribution that does not survive checking.

That is the diagnostic, and it generalises well beyond this one phrase. **When a practice needs
borrowed authority, look at what the borrowing is doing.** In the real version of model
agnosticism, nothing is load-bearing except the checking. A citation that adds prestige and no
procedure is the signature of the counterfeit, and it is detectable without knowing anything about
the subject matter, which makes it one of the few instruments in this book that a reader can run
tomorrow.

### E-Prime, at full strength

The technique most often bundled with all this deserves its own treatment, because unlike *maybe
logic* it is a genuine procedure with a genuine argument and — unusually for anything in this
territory — it has been tested.

**E-Prime is English with every form of the verb *to be* removed.** D. David Bourland Jr., who had
studied under Korzybski, devised it in the late 1940s and published it in 1965 in the *General
Semantics Bulletin* under the flat title "A Linguistic Note: Writing in E-Prime."[^7]

The argument is precise, and it is worth stating precisely because the popular version has lost the
distinction it turns on. Two uses of *to be* are the targets. The **is of identity** — *the cat is
my only pet* — welds a thing to a category. The **is of predication** — *the cat is furry* — hangs
a property on a thing as though the property lived in the thing rather than in an encounter between
the thing and an observer with particular sense organs standing at a particular distance. Both, the
argument runs, confuse the map with the territory: they take something produced in a meeting and
report it as a property of the world. Korzybski had already proposed abolishing the *is* of
identity in 1933. Bourland went further and removed every form, on the grounds that a rule with
exceptions is not a rule anybody follows.

It found its most serious professional home in psychotherapy. Albert Ellis, who built rational
emotive behaviour therapy in the 1950s partly out of general semantics, adopted E-Prime and wrote
some of its main books in it, and said flatly that REBT "has favored E-Prime more than any other
form of psychotherapy."[^8] The clinical case is the strongest case, and it is easy to feel from
inside. *I am depressed* installs a permanent identity. *I have felt depressed since March* has a
start date, an observer and an implied end. The first is a report about what you are; the second is
a report about what has been happening, which is the only kind of report there could have been.

That is real. Anyone who has done it on their own sentences knows it is real. And it is the last
uncomplicated thing this chapter has to say about it.

---

### What it costs, measured

The strong version of the E-Prime claim is that a grammatical change produces a cognitive one. That
is a Whorfian claim, and Whorfian claims have had a rough seventy years.

The strong hypothesis — that language *determines* thought — is not a live position among linguists
or cognitive scientists and has not been for decades. The weak version — that language biases
attention and default framing — is supported, actively researched, and much smaller than its
popular reputation. Even its showpiece has trouble. Boroditsky's 2001 finding that Mandarin
speakers think about time vertically where English speakers think horizontally is the single most
cited demonstration of linguistic relativity in the modern literature, and it has failed
replication more than once, most thoroughly in January and Kako's six experiments in 2007 — which
also reported that Mandarin speakers use horizontal spatial metaphors for time **more** often than
vertical ones, which cuts at the premise rather than the result.[^9]

So the general warrant is weak. What about the specific one?

Two studies have actually tested E-Prime, both from Daniel David's group at Babeş-Bolyai, and they
are the reason this section can say anything at all.

The first is correlational. Oltean and David, with 197 participants, measured how often people use
*to be* against rational and irrational beliefs, functional and dysfunctional negative emotions,
the functionality of inferences, and general distress. Two associations reached significance:
frequency of *to be* against general rational beliefs at **r = −.211**, and against preference
beliefs at **r = −.251**.[^10] Take those at face value and they run in E-Prime's direction. Then
look at what they are. Correlations of that size account for four to six per cent of the variance;
the design is cross-sectional, so nothing in it can distinguish *using* is *makes you less
rational* from *less rational people use* is *more*; and E-Prime's claim is not correlational at
all. It is an intervention.

The second study is the intervention, and it is the one that matters. David ran R-Prime — the same
constraint, in Romanian — against ordinary Romanian in an anger induction, scored on the binary
model that separates a functional negative feeling (annoyance) from a dysfunctional one
(anger).[^11] The result, in the author's own summary: **anger rose from baseline to post-test by
the same amount in both conditions.** The R-Prime group additionally showed higher annoyance.

Read that carefully, because it is not a null and it is not a win. **The constraint did not reduce
the dysfunctional reaction at all. It added a functional one alongside it.** The identification the
technique exists to dissolve went ahead and did its work on schedule; what changed was that a
second, better-shaped response became available next to it.

The critics inside general semantics said this in words before anyone measured it, and their
formulation is better than the finding: **identity-in-the-language is not the same as
identity-in-reaction.**[^12] You can write flawless E-Prime and hold every identification you ever
had. The verb was never where the error lived.

And this can be demonstrated on the page, at no cost, using this book's own sentences. Take the
canonical statement of C5 — *God is the name of what C1 describes* — and put it in E-Prime: *God
names what C1 describes.* Nothing has softened. The identity is exactly as strong and exactly as
contestable, and the sentence is now marginally worse English. Run it the other way, on a sentence
that deserves catching: *he is a liar* becomes *he lies habitually*, which is the same
identification with a frequency claim bolted on and a defence pre-emptively removed. Where the ban
does work, look at what did the work. *I am depressed* improves not because *am* left but because
its replacement smuggled in a date and a first-person observer. **Korzybski already had a name for
that operation — dating and indexing — and it requires banning nothing.**

The honest verdict, stated as a grade rather than a verdict: E-Prime buys a small, real,
inconsistently replicated attentional effect at a large and continuous syntactic cost, and the part
that does the buying is the substitution, not the prohibition. As a two-week exercise to find out
how much of your speech consists of unearned identifications, it is excellent and this book
recommends it. As a permanent discipline it charges rent for a benefit available free.

### The price of the practice itself

Now the larger charge, which is not against the technique but against the position, and it is
structural rather than moral.

**Model agnosticism has no gauge on its own symmetry.**

The doctrine says: hold every model loosely. It does not, and cannot, say: *hold them equally
loosely* — because it has no instrument for measuring how loosely anything is being held. Looseness
is the virtue, so more of it always reads as more virtue, and there is nothing in the practice that
fires when the looseness is being spent in one direction and hoarded in the other.

That is not a hypothetical failure. It happened to the man who named the doctrine, in the book that
names it. The critical literature on *The New Inquisition* is small and unkind and, for present
purposes, correct on the mechanics. Wilson paraphrased people he disagreed with rather than quoting
them, which quietly removes the reader's ability to check. His paranormal sources were repeatedly
second-hand, partially accurate, or wrong. And the sharpest one: he wrote a book attacking an
organisation whose journal he had not read — the exact failure he was accusing it of.[^13]

None of that refutes model agnosticism. It is worse than a refutation: it is the doctrine's
predicted failure mode, arriving on schedule, in its founding text, undetected by its author.
Scepticism ran at full power toward the position he was fighting and idled toward the positions he
liked, and **there was no reading on the dial**, because the doctrine does not have one.

The general form is worth stating, because this book keeps finding the same shape in its own
machinery. **A rule with no instrument is a rule that gets credited and not run.** Uniform doubt is
not an instrument in any case — a constant added to every term changes no ordering. If you doubt
everything by the same amount, you have not made yourself more discriminating; you have made
yourself less, at the price of feeling more careful.

And then the failure the beat sheet for this chapter named, which is the endpoint of the drift:
**model agnosticism becomes a way of never holding anything.** Every claim arrives with *maybe*
pre-attached, which means no claim ever incurs a debt, which means nothing can ever come due.
Nobody in this state can be shown to have been wrong, because nobody in it has said anything that
being wrong could attach to. That is not humility. **It is unfalsifiability wearing humility's
clothes**, and it is the same structure this book refuses in a horoscope, in a conspiracy, and in a
theology — refused there for exactly this reason and not permitted here because the person holding
it is well-read and pleasant.

---

### The price tag was in the founding sentence

Here is the part that makes this chapter's diagnosis something better than a complaint, because the
correction to the drift is not imported from outside the tradition. **It is in the sentence the
tradition is built on, in the half nobody quotes.**

Korzybski, in a paper read to the American Association for the Advancement of Science in New
Orleans in 1931 and reprinted in *Science and Sanity* two years later:

> A map is not the territory it represents, but, if correct, it has a similar structure to the
> territory, which accounts for its usefulness.[^14]

Eleven words of that are among the most famous in the century. The rest is a metaphysics, and it is
the load-bearing part. II.5 established what the second clause does to the Ground and why the word
*map* is retired as a name for it. **This chapter takes the other half of that ruling — the sense
in which the dictum is kept undamaged and needed in every word, which II.5 reserved for Book VI.**

*If correct.* Correctness there is a relation between two structures. The map has a shape, the
territory has a shape, and the map is good exactly insofar as those shapes correspond. Which means
that models are **rankable**. Not by how strongly anyone believes them. Not by whose they are. By
whether the correspondence holds when you go and check.

Quote the first eleven words alone and you have a counsel of despair: all maps fail, so hold them
all at the same arm's length, so nothing can be preferred to anything. Keep the second clause and
the whole thing inverts. The reason to hold a model loosely is not that all models are equally
hopeless. **It is that some of them are better, and finding out which requires being willing to put
one down.** Looseness is not the goal; it is the cost of running the comparison. Korzybski was not
despairing. He was building a discipline for making better maps, and *better* requires a standard,
and the standard is the territory's own shape.

So the difference between the discipline and the shrug reduces to a single question, and it can be
asked out loud of anyone, including yourself: **what would make you put this one down?**

The discipline has an answer. The shrug has *maybe*.

### What this book does instead, by ruling

This book does not run model agnosticism on itself, and refusing to is a decision with a price
which gets paid here rather than assumed.

It carries a register of thirty canonical claims. Each is stated in one form of words, unhedged,
and may be restated but not improved in passing. Each carries a **trap** — the specific
grammatical move by which that claim tends to die, usually not in the main verb. Each carries a
**near-miss to refuse** — the neighbouring position that sounds identical and is not, named, so
that arriving at it counts as a failure rather than a variant.

That apparatus is the answer to the shrug, and the answer is not *we are more confident*. It is
this: **a model held with a stated defeat condition is held. A model held with a* maybe *and no
defeat condition is not held — it is stored.** The difference is not the strength of anyone's
conviction. It is whether there exists, in writing, in advance, something that could take the claim
away from you.

The bill for that arrives immediately and it is not small. Writing down what would kill C1 means C1
can be killed, by someone who is not you, using the instrument you handed them. Book V had to write
a sentence that could hand the whole book away and wrote it down instead of walking around it. This
chapter has just conceded that its own research corpus is a specimen of the defect it describes.
None of that is available to a position that arrived pre-hedged, and it is not available *because*
of the hedge: an unfalsifiable claim cannot be caught, and a claim that cannot be caught cannot be
improved. **The hedge that protects you from being wrong protects you equally from getting better.**

---

### The one exemption, and why it is not special pleading

There is an obvious objection and it is the strongest one in the chapter, so it gets stated in its
own voice rather than in a weakened form.

*Run your own doctrine on your own book. If all models are held loosely, then the Ground is another
model, held loosely. You have exempted yourself.*

The answer is not *except ours*. The answer is that **C5 is not a model**, and this is a claim with
a consequence rather than a convenience.

A model is a representation-*of*. It has a structure; the thing has a structure; the two are
compared; the model can be more correct or less. Every operation of model agnosticism presupposes
that setup — you cannot rank things by correspondence unless both ends have a shape. And II.5 ruled
at length that the Ground has no shape to correspond to. Structure is difference, difference is a
feature of being somewhere in particular, and the Ground is at no position. A render is therefore
not a correct map of the Ground; and, at a cost this book paid out loud rather than banked, it is
not an incorrect one either. It is not a candidate for accuracy against the Ground at all.

So model agnosticism, which is a discipline **for models**, has nothing to operate on there. Not
because the claim is protected. Because the machinery does not engage.

⚠ And that is exactly what special pleading sounds like, so here is the only thing that
distinguishes it, and it is not an argument — it is a practice. **C5 is written down in the same
register, with the same apparatus, at the same exposure as everything else.** It carries five named
denials, each with the specific error it smuggles: not the server, not the developer, not the
engine, not the map, not the player. It carries its trap. It carries its near-miss. If the Ground
turns out to have a structure — if there is anything there for a render to be a better or worse map
*of* — C5 is dead, and the register says so in advance in the same words it would use about
anybody. The exemption is from **hedging**, not from **being wrong**. Those are different
exemptions, and only one of them is a privilege.

---

### The shrug, carded

Every chapter of this book has carded its render, and this one has to card a posture, which looks
like a category error until you notice that the posture behaves exactly like a render and is
adopted for the same reason: it makes the world easy to be in.

**THE UNIVERSAL MAYBE — no dates, available in every era, at its most attractive in this one.**

*What it renders superbly*, and it is real or nobody would buy it: **plurality**. Under
this filter you see options where other people see one road. Positions that a committed person
cannot even parse arrive intact and comparable. That is a genuine capacity and most people do not
have it.

*Its null space:* **THE SETTLED.** Anything that has actually been established — anything that
named its defeat condition, went and looked, and survived — becomes invisible, because it arrives
wearing the same *maybe* as everything that never risked anything. Uniform doubt is a constant
added to every term, and a constant added to every term destroys the ordering. The filter does not
hide the settled thing. **It delivers it at the same rank as its counterfeit**, which is worse,
because a hidden thing can be looked for and a flattened one cannot.

*Its complement — who sees the settled:* anybody who has staked something on a claim, named in
advance what would defeat it, gone and looked, and either paid or been vindicated in public. The
experimental sciences at their best, and their pre-registration machinery exists precisely because
the settled and its counterfeit are hard to tell apart from inside. A tradesman, whose work is
inspected by gravity. Anyone who has been wrong at cost and can describe the bill. **This complement
is the cheapest to reach of any in the atlas — every reader knows such a person — and the filter's
particular strength is that it makes consulting them feel unnecessary**, since under uniform doubt
the person who paid and the person who postured arrive at the same rank. The line to ask for is not
*what do you believe*; it is *what would have shown you were wrong, and what did it cost when you
checked.*

*Its boundary:* the point at which a claim would have to be defended. The filter is invisible in
ordinary conversation, where nothing is being decided, and asserts itself precisely at the moment
of commitment — which is why its holders experience it as costless.

*Mechanism:* **it identifies what is the case with what nobody has yet committed to.** No falsehood
is required at any stage. Every individual *maybe* may be perfectly warranted.

*Navigational implication, and it is the one that stings:* the practice most commonly adopted as a
defence against renders **is a render**, with a null space of its own, and it is harder to detect
than the ones in VI.3 through VI.6 because it has the shape of the cure.

---

### The fifth rung

Book VI has been building a single ladder and this is the rung it was built for.

VI.3: you cannot find the error by looking harder, because looking performs the reassignment. VI.4:
you cannot recover what was lost by reading more, because reading performs the exclusion. VI.5: you
cannot discover the exclusions by attending harder, because attention is the resource being
allocated. VI.6: you cannot exit by switching it off, because the mechanism has already written
itself into a substrate that no longer needs it.

**VI.7: you cannot get out by holding nothing.**

Refusing to hold a model does not put you outside the tunnel. It puts you in one with the lights
off. The render is not optional — that was settled in Book II and nothing since has softened it —
and declining to name yours does not suspend it, does not weaken it, and does not make it
revisable. It removes the record. You keep the whole apparatus and give up the only instrument that
has ever moved one, which is the written-down claim that something specific would count as it being
wrong.

The person running true model agnosticism is doing something strenuous and rare: naming their model
in words another person could check, saying what would defeat it, and going to look. The person
saying *maybe* is doing the opposite of that while producing the same sound. **The cost of the
counterfeit is the full cost of the render you were fleeing, plus the loss of the only thing that
was ever going to get you out of it, plus the belief that you had already escaped.**

Which leaves exactly one thing to do, and it is uncomfortable in the way this whole book has been
promising to become. If the model has to be named, then the honest place to start is the one you
are standing in right now — the render you have been reading this book from, which has been running
the entire time, and which this book has spent fifty chapters editing without asking your
permission.

That is the next chapter, and it is about you.
