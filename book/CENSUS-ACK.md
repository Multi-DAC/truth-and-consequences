# CENSUS ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS

`tools/citation_forward.py` re-counts every claim this book makes about its own text.
Four of them cannot be settled by a grep, each for a different and real reason. They are
written down here rather than left red, because a gate whose alarm branch is the only
branch is unclearable by construction and teaches its reader to skip it.

⚠ **An acknowledgement is not a repair.** It records that a human read the claim and why
the machine could not. The key is `CHAPTER:subject` and carries no line number on purpose —
a key with a line number is invalidated by the next paragraph inserted above it, and an
acknowledgement that silently stops matching reopens as a mystery rather than as a row.

- `IV.10:?` — the corpus is not this book. *"occurs exactly three times in 884 KB, all three in the Decomposers material"* counts the INHERITED transcript, not the manuscript. Outside this gate's corpus entirely, and the note says which corpus it means.
- `V.6:summit` — **the arithmetic checks out and the sense clause is what a human owes.** Scope is V.1, stated two sentences above the claim rather than in it; measured `summit` ×5 in V.1, which is the number claimed. What no grep can settle is *"every one is the metaphysical destination V.1 refuses — a different sense."* That is a reading of five passages.
- `VII.4:Aquinas` — a claim about USE, not about strings. *"Aquinas is used four times in this book on two different questions."* The name occurs 23 times; he is leaned on as an authority four. Both sentences are true, and only a reader can tell them apart. The note names the two questions, which is the form that makes it checkable by a person.
- `VIII.1:Watts` — the scope is deictic and a human resolved it. *"He is not in I.6; `Watts` occurs zero times there."* **There** is I.6, named in the preceding clause. Measured: `Watts` appears in III.2, VI.6, VIII.1, VIII.6 and C.1 — **not in I.6**. The claim is TRUE; the gate could not follow the pronoun.
