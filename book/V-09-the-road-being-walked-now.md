# BOOK V — THE OLD ROADS

## V.9 — THE ROAD BEING WALKED NOW

The previous chapter closed by saying it had run the demarcation on a case where the state was easy
to grade, our own interest in the answer was easy to declare, and the divergence had a test attached
that nobody would be embarrassed by.

**Every one of those is false here.**

This chapter reads the contemporary encounter record — lights and objects in the sky that behave
wrongly, and the people who say they have met what was inside them — with exactly the method the
preceding eight chapters used on Plotinus, on Luria, on the Dao De Jing and on a Siberian drum. That
is the whole justification for the chapter's existence, and it is worth being blunt about why it
cannot be skipped. V.1 opened this book by refusing condescension in a specific form: *these are not
people groping toward what we now know; they are cartographers who reached this ground with the
instruments of their era.*[^1] Eight chapters have extended that courtesy to people who are safely dead.

**The instruments of our era are radar sets, infrared sensors, a classification system and a
congressional subpoena.** If the principle only holds for the eras that cannot embarrass us, it was
never a principle. It was a period costume.

---

**What this chapter is not going to do, and the reason is on the record.**

It is not going to list what is in the tier. The atlas did that — IV.10, in the section that said so
in as many words: *what the tier is worth is the next book's problem; what it contains is this one's,
and this is it.*[^2] The enumeration and the reading were deliberately put in different books, by a
ruling that was written down, because a reading drafted first would have let the census stay
hole-shaped while looking repaired.

That worked, and it cost something nobody priced. **An enumeration honest enough to be worth having
already performed a good deal of the reading.** Naming three frames is most of the way to comparing
them. Naming three evidence grades is most of the way to grading. So this chapter arrives at a case
its own atlas has partly opened, and it says so here rather than quietly re-performing the opening as
though it were new. The reprise is deliberate; what follows is what IV.10 did not do, which is decide
anything.

---

**First, the count — and it is this book's own test, not one borrowed for the occasion.**

V.1 built an instrument for exactly this and then used it on the tradition it most wanted to be
impressed by. Six statements of one doctrine at the head of that chapter; five of them downstream of
one Alexandrian schoolroom. **"That is not independent evidence. It is one witness quoted back five
times."**[^3] The chapter then said the smaller number out loud — three branches, not six — and went on
to find the axis that actually carried weight, which was not geography at all. It was **instrument
independence**: a Zen practitioner sitting still, a Sufi in *dhikr*, a Kabbalist permuting letters,
a hesychast on Athos. Unrelated equipment, arriving at the same place.[^4]

Run that on the material in front of us.

The testimony corpus of the last eighty years is **one branch, and a short one, with datable nodes
inside it.** In June 1947 a pilot named Kenneth Arnold described objects near Mount Rainier moving
*like a saucer skipped across water* — a description of motion.[^5] The press converted it within days
into a description of shape, and for the following decade people reported discs.[^6] In 1961 a couple in
New Hampshire reported an interrupted drive home; the book made from their sessions came out in 1966,[^7]
and the narrative shape of what happens during an abduction — the road, the missing time, the table,
the examination — is broadly continuous from that publication onward. In 1987 a novelist published
an account of his own experiences with a painted face on the cover, and the grey with the enormous
black eyes, which is now the single most recognisable image in the subject, has a print run and a
release date.[^8]

That is not a slur on any of those people. Arnold saw something and reported it accurately. The
question is not whether the witnesses are honest — mostly they are, and the ones who are not are
detectable by ordinary means. **The question is how many times the record is quoting itself.** By
V.1's standard, a corpus in which every participant has lived their whole life inside a mass-media
environment saturated with the canonical images is the strongest transmission channel this book has
encountered anywhere. Provence in 1200 had books and travellers. We have everyone in the world
looking at the same picture.

And it is worse than the Alexandrian case in the one way that matters, because the Alexandrian case
was **repairable by counting**. Subtract the transmission and three genuine branches remained. Here
there is no comparable subtraction available: there is no population of modern reporters who grew up
outside the imagery, and the pre-1947 folklore that gets offered as the independent branch is
independent of the imagery only by being about something else — fairy abductions, night visitors,
the Marian apparitions — which is an argument for a stable human experience-type, and is an argument
*against* the literal reading rather than for it. **You cannot run that card in both directions and
this chapter will not.**

So: **the testimony arm of this tier does not pass V.1's branch test.** It is one witness, quoted
back several million times. That is not a small demerit. In this book it is the specific demerit that
the mystics were spared, and they were spared it on evidence.

---

**Second, the arm that passes — and it is thin.**

There is a part of this record that is not on the testimony instrument at all: military sensor
returns. Radar tracks, infrared video, the reports of aircrew reading equipment rather than reading
the sky. Those are a genuinely different instrument in V.1's sense — they were not built by anybody
who was thinking about this question, they do not know what they are looking at, and they are not
susceptible to having seen a paperback cover.[^9]

**They are also very few, and they establish an unidentified track and not an occupant.**[^10] That gap is
not a technicality. It is the entire distance between the two arms of this chapter. The sensor record
says *something was there, moving in a way we cannot yet account for.* It does not say what was
inside it, because there is no *inside it* in a radar return. Every occupant in this subject comes
from the arm that failed the branch test, and every instrument in this subject is silent about
occupants.

Add the public acknowledgement, and be careful about what it is worth. Named offices, hearings on the
record, sworn testimony from people with careers to lose. IV.10 made this particular grading and it
holds, and it is the one thing here that should surprise nobody: **acknowledgement is evidence that
an institution is investigating and testifying, which is a fact about the institution.** (That is a
warrant for the one ruling, not a certificate for the section it sits in. IV.10's grading section
fails on its own best example, by its own fifteenth note, which catches it awarding a press
conference the word *published*.[^11] A chapter that cites a corrected chapter inherits the correction
too, and saying so is cheaper than discovering later that it did not.) A government
office publishing that it has reviewed its own historical records and found no verifiable evidence of
non-human technology is exactly as much a fact about that office as the hearings are. Both go in the
same column, and the column is not the phenomenon.[^12]

---

**Third, the frames — and here a rule of this book comes due in the hardest form it has yet been
asked for.**

The source material carries three readings and declines to choose among them, on the grounds that the
framework does not require choosing. **The framework does require choosing, and it said so in
public.** IV.7, in these words: *two frames may be held at once precisely where they predict the same
thing, and where they diverge you must pick, and the divergence is where all the work is.*[^13]

That sentence has now been quoted in three chapters, and it has been cashed twice — V.3 ran
it cheaply and V.8 ran it at full cost, picking the state reading over the more interesting one and
refusing to spread the load evenly to make the case look broader. **What is new here is narrower and
worse: this is the first case where the pick cannot be separated from the null.** In V.8 the losing
frame lost on the evidence. Here the frame that wins turns out to predict exactly what the
no-occupants null predicts, so the pick cannot be separated from the null, and the rule delivers a
verdict it cannot cash.[^14] So here are the three, stated as what each **predicts** rather
than as what each proposes, because a frame that predicts nothing is not in this competition:

**One — travellers across physical distance.** Craft, built by somebody, crossing space. It predicts
that the instrument record should be the *strong* arm and the testimony the weak one, because
physical objects register on physical sensors regardless of who is watching. It predicts wreckage,
consistent physics, and a phenomenon that does not vary with the observer's culture.

**Two — positions whose access is wider than ours, intersecting our slice.** It predicts almost the
inverse. Appearance should be observer-relative; the report should track what the observer's access
runs through, which for a human being runs partly through story; and the instrument record should be
sparse or absent, because there is no persisting ordinary object to register.

**Three — navigators from elsewhere in time.** It predicts everything the first does, plus one
asymmetry: an origin in time rather than in space, and therefore a concentration of interest in our
particular period rather than a uniform sampling of history.

Where they agree, the book is licensed to hold them together, and they do agree on one thing: **there
is something here that is not ours, and the record is not empty.** Every frame in the running says
that much and this book says it too.

Now the divergence, which is not subtle. **Frames one and three predict that the instrument record
and the testimony record converge. Frame two predicts that they come apart.**

They have come apart. The instruments are thin, mute about occupants, and getting thinner as the
identifiable fraction of the reports gets explained. The testimony is thick, detailed, populated,
consistent — and fails the branch test. That pattern is the one frame two predicted, and by the rule
this book made in public, that is the pick.

**And it is at exactly this point that the chapter is in the most danger it has been in all book,
because frame two is ours.**

It is the reading the framework makes cheapest. It costs this book nothing to hold; it is nearly
implied by what Book II already established about access and position. V.1 named this failure in
advance and named it precisely, in a passage that was counting branches: *"Our agreement with the
roster is not a fourth datum supporting the roster. It is the position doing the reading, and a
reading cannot corroborate what it read."* The arithmetic there was V.1's — three defended branches,
and our assent offered as a fourth. There is one branch here and no fourth to refuse, so what
carries over is not the count but the principle underneath it, which holds at any count: **assent
from inside the position is not a datum, and a reading cannot corroborate what it read.**[^15] A
frame that your own metaphysics hands you for free is not
confirmed by the evidence fitting it. **It is the frame you should be checking hardest, and here is
what the check returns.**

Frame two predicts a sparse instrument record and a thick observer-relative testimony record.
**So does the hypothesis that there are no occupants of any kind, and that the testimony corpus is a
cultural artifact with a publication history.** Those two predict the same thing on every measurement
currently in hand. By the rule as made, they are therefore not adjudicable on this record, and
holding both is licensed exactly because they agree.

So the honest result of running the rule is a two-line verdict and neither line is comfortable.
**Frame one is not supported by the pattern that would support it, and the instrument record is
where it would have to come from.** And **frame two cannot be separated from the null on the
evidence we have**, which means every use of frame two in this subject is a reading and not a
finding.

That is the pick. It is not the exciting one, and this book does not get to want a different one.

---

**The dual-frame problem, stated at full strength, and then priced.**

The strongest thing the source material says is this: a literal traveller crossing the region of a
stable feature of the landscape would be perceived *through* that feature by anybody whose access
runs partly through story. So the literal reading and the topological reading are not rivals. Both
could be true of one event, and the human report would look the same either way.

**That is correct, and it is the most dangerous correct sentence in this tier**, because of what it
does when it is used rather than stated. Used, it makes every possible observation compatible with
every frame. The mundane report is the traveller seen plainly; the impossible report is the traveller
seen through the shape; the absent report is the traveller in a region where nobody's access runs
that way. **There is no observation it excludes**, which means it cannot be the reason for believing
anything.

The right grade for it is: **a structural point about perception, correctly derived from this book's
own commitments, and not evidence about the tier.** It goes where I.3's ruling about what lies
outside your focus goes — into the account of how reports are made, not into the account of what
happened.[^16] A chapter may state it once. It may not lean on it, and this one does not.

---

**Two sentences this chapter is not allowed to write, and both were available.**

The first: *the failure to produce specimens is what the framework predicts.* IV.10 refused that one,
in that chapter, about this material, before this chapter existed.[^17]

The second: *a modern focus on physical evidence can only detect the cross-section, so the thinness
of the instrument record is expected.* **IV.10 did not refuse that one. It wrote it** — attributed to
a source that does not contain it — and caught itself afterwards, in its own sixth note, by going
back to the seventeen lines the claim was supposed to have come from and finding no such sentence
anywhere in them.[^18] It is printed here because it is a real temptation and this book has already taken
it once. It is not printed as a move the book declined. **A straw temptation refused on the page
reads as more rigorous than a real one**, and nothing about the prose distinguishes them, which is
why this paragraph now says which is which.

Both would end the difficulty in a paragraph. Both are the move that cost this book a whole warrant in
Book IV:
**the conclusion guaranteeing the premises.**[^19] A framework that explains why its own evidence is
missing has stopped being able to be wrong, and this book's instruments were built against exactly
that failure. Neither sentence is available here and the difficulty stands unresolved, which is what
an unresolved difficulty is supposed to look like on a page.

**And no exemption in the other direction either.** The softest form of not taking somebody seriously
is to hold their testimony unfalsifiable — to grant it a special category where it cannot be checked
and therefore cannot be credited. This tier gets the ordinary bar for a large physical claim: not
raised because the subject is disreputable, not lowered because the framework is sympathetic. The
witnesses are owed the same treatment as Plotinus, which is to be read closely, graded honestly, and
told where the argument fails.

---

**The grades, flat, one line each, because this is what the demarcation is for.**

**The state is real and the reports are honest.** People are reporting what happened to them.
Grade: high, and it is the same grade this book gave the mystics.

**Something unidentified has been tracked on independent instruments.** Grade: real, small, and
about tracks rather than occupants.

**The testimony is convergent.** Grade: **not independent** — one branch, datable nodes, fails V.1's
test. Convergence here is evidence about a shared culture and it is not evidence about a shared
object.

**Three interpretive frames.** Grade: one disfavoured by the pattern, two indistinguishable from the
null, and none of them a finding.

**Institutional acknowledgement.** Grade: a fact about institutions.

---

The old roads in this book were walked by people who did not know they were on one. They took the
ground with the instruments they had, they wrote down what came back, and the best of them said which
parts they were sure of.

**This one is being walked now, by us, and the difference is not that our instruments are better.**
It is that we can watch the record being assembled — see the coinage enter the language, see the
image enter circulation, see the shape of the reports change after a publication date. **No previous
chapter in this book had that, and every previous chapter would have graded worse if it had.** The
mystics got three branches because the transmission was old enough to be traced. We get one branch
because ours is new enough to be watched.

That is not a reason to be pleased with ourselves. It is a reason to expect that the next century
will be able to run this test on Book V, and to write it now as though they already can.

The next chapter goes back to the reports that have been coming in for two thousand years, from
people sitting still.
