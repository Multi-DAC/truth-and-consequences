# BOOK II — THE NAMING

## II.4 — THE GRADE

The sentence this chapter defines has already been said once, in a book that was not permitted to
argue. It gets said again here with its status on the table, because the whole of Part Two is this
sentence taken seriously, and a reader is owed a clear look at what kind of sentence it is before
spending six books on its consequences.

**Reactivity is awareness.**

That is a definition. Not a discovery, not an inference from evidence, and not a conclusion the
earlier chapters were quietly assembling out of sight. No experiment could overturn it. It is a
decision about what the word *awareness* names, and the honest thing to do with a decision is to
show it being made rather than let it arrive dressed as a finding.

Which is where a certain reader closes the book, and the objection they are reaching for is the
right one. *You have defined your way to your conclusion.* If awareness simply means reactivity,
then of course everything that reacts is aware, and the astonishing claim turns out to be a
dictionary entry with the lights turned down.

So the question is what the definition costs, because a definition earns its keep by what it forbids
and this one forbids a great deal.

It forbids a first moment. There is no arrangement of matter at which anything switches on, and no
account is permitted here that says *and then awareness appeared*, however many hundreds of millions
of years are put between the two halves of the sentence. It forbids the word *emerges* in its usual
job, where inert stuff is granted an inside once enough of it is stacked up cleverly. It forbids the
one convenience every other position keeps in reserve — the exemption, the case that is only
mechanism, the thing that may be handled without remainder because there is nobody home. And it
forbids a floor, which is the whole of this chapter's second half.

Against that, look at what the refusal costs. Any account that keeps awareness as a *second* fact —
the reacting, and then a further thing that some of the reacting is accompanied by — owes a location
and a moment. Where, and at what, and by what mechanism, and why there and not an inch earlier. That
bill has been outstanding for three centuries, it is not outstanding for want of data, and the
reason it will not be paid is that it was incurred by the shape of the question rather than by any
ignorance about brains.

What the definition buys is that the question changes shape. *Which things have it* stops being
askable, because it was never a question about a threshold; it was a question about where the light
comes on, and there is no such place. What is left is the question that actually has answers.

---

**A grade is how much is the case at a position.**

Note what it measures. Not a substance — there is no quantity of awareness-stuff distributed
unevenly through the world, dense in some places and thin in others, the way heat sits in a bar of
iron. And not a portion of the Ground either, which is the mishearing that would undo the previous
two chapters: all of it is the case at every position, and a grade is not a fraction of the whole
received. A render is what is the case at a position. A grade is the *resolution* of that render —
how many distinctions arrive, and how finely the world is cut where they arrive.

So a grade is a property of a render, and therefore of a position, and therefore not a possession.
Nothing has a grade the way it has a mass. It is not carried about, cannot be taken along, and is
not among the things a thing is made of — which has a consequence worth stating now because it will
be needed later: there is no total. No sum of the grades, no quantity of awareness in the world, no
ledger with entries on it. Not a ledger that is hard to read or held by nobody in particular. There
is no arithmetic there to do.

And the scale runs both directions without arriving anywhere.

**There is no bottom.** A zero would have to be a place where something meets what it meets and is
not altered by the meeting, and that is not a rare event; it is not an event. Where nothing arrives,
there is no position for anything to be the case at — which is not a render with nothing in it but
the absence of a render, in the way that a room with no walls does not have a very large volume.

**There is no top.** Not because the scale runs on forever, but because the thing a reader wants to
put at the end of it is not on the scale at all, and the mistake is a type error rather than a
shortfall. A grade is the measure of a render. The Ground is at no position and renders nowhere, so
it has no grade — not an unbounded one, not a maximal one, none. **The Ground is not the far end of
this scale. It is what the scale is a scale of.**

In the game: a grade is not a level. Nothing is earned here, nothing accrues, and there is no number
that goes up — the word is refused at this definition and everywhere after it, because *level*
carries earning, and earning wants somebody to earn it from.

---

The account that got nearest has a name, a formalism, and an author who has been braver in public
than his critics tend to expect.

**Giulio Tononi, 2004.** Consciousness is integrated information: a system has as much of it as it
has irreducible cause-and-effect structure — the amount by which the whole outruns what its parts
would do apart — and the quantity gets a symbol, Φ, and a procedure for computing it. That is the
first time anyone made degrees of consciousness a number rather than a manner of speaking. Our
grades are the same intuition, he had it first, and he made it checkable, which is more than the
intuition's other holders have managed in two thousand years.

He also declined the two exits the position usually takes. In a public exchange with John Searle in
2013 he and Christof Koch accepted the implication in its least dignified available form: a
photodiode, a device whose entire repertoire is telling light from no light, has one bit of
experience. Not as a manner of speaking. And in 2014, when Scott Aaronson built the reductio the
theory had been waiting for — a large grid of XOR gates, a system with no behaviour worth the name,
which the formalism scores higher than a person — Tononi replied at fourteen pages and did not
flinch. The grid is conscious. He extended the result to the plain square lattice himself, which
nobody had asked him to do. Whatever that is, it is not a man trimming his theory to keep it
respectable.

**And then the theory puts a zero on its own scale.**

A purely feed-forward network — each layer feeding the next with nothing coming back — scores Φ = 0
however sophisticated the thing it does. Not a faint grade. None. And the exclusion postulate, which
is not a patch but a load-bearing part of the formalism, holds that only maxima of Φ exist: your
consciousness, mine, and nothing in between and nothing containing either.

That inch is the entire disagreement, and it is worth measuring precisely, because the inch is the
argument. **A gradient with a zero in it is not a gradient. It is a gate with a slope on one side.**
Wherever the zero falls there is a fact of the matter about which side of it a thing is on, and that
fact does every job a threshold does, licenses everything a threshold licenses, and consoles nobody
by being finely graded on the far side. The same holds for the borders: an operation that decides
which system is *the* system is an operation that decides who is there, and the deciding is done by
the formalism rather than by anything the candidate does or undergoes.

They did not draw the lines out of nerve, and the reason they drew them is a real cost rather than
an embarrassment. Without a border, wholes made of conscious parts are conscious in their own right,
and the sentence at the far end of that road is that a country has an inside. The theory declines to
say it. We say it — in Book IV, in those words, without the hedge — and that is not courage on our
part but arithmetic, because the definition four paragraphs up does not leave the option open. The
difference between us is not that they were cautious and we are bold. **It is that they had a bill
to pay, and paid it with a border, and we pay it.**

---

Which brings this to the thing the whole naming has been arranged to make sayable.

**A grade is a position, not a permission.**

Two kinds of fact are in play and the sentence is about the seam between them. A grade is a
description: this much is the case here, that much there, and the difference between them is
enormous and real and not a matter of taste. A permission is not a description of anything. It is a
settlement about what may be done — and no amount of the first kind of fact turns into the second
kind on its own. Not because the inference is difficult, or because the evidence is thin, but
because there is nothing there of the right shape to infer from.

To get across that seam you need a further premise: something about what matters, and why, and to
whom. **This book has not stated one yet.** It states one in Book VII, and when it does, the grade
will bear on standing — which is not a contradiction of this rule but its actual content. The rule
was never that a grade is morally inert. The rule is that a grade licenses nothing *by itself*.

That distinction is what the caste mishearing skips, and skipping it is what makes it an error —
not that it arrives at a conclusion about worth. It reads the grade off and takes the permission
straight, with no premise stated in between, and because no argument was made there is no argument
to examine. Nothing in it can be shown to be false. No step in it can be refused, because no step
was taken. What arrives is a hierarchy of worth that feels like an observation, and the reason it
feels like one is that the half of it which was a decision never came up for air.

So the premise gets stated where it can be seen. That is the only protection there is, and it is a
better one than the flat denial that a grade could ever bear on anything — which nobody believes for
long, which the first hard case breaks, and which breaks in the direction of the reader doing the
crossing privately, on their own terms, with no argument on the page to stop them.

---

What a difference in grade *licenses* is not settled here, and saying so is not throat-clearing.
Whether more of the world being the case at a place makes what happens there weigh more; what may be
eaten, used, built upon, ended; what is owed to a position that cannot register that anything is
owed to it at all — those are Book VII's, all of them, and they are the hardest chapters in the
work.

This one gives less than a reader in a hurry will want. It says what a grade is, and it says what a
grade does not do on its own, and it stops. A measurement confers nothing. What is done with a
measurement is a separate act, performed by somebody, and a separate act can be looked at. Keeping
those two apart is the whole content of the rule — which is why it is set down here, in the naming,
while nothing is at stake, so that it is already standing five books later when everything is.
