# BOOK III — THE GAME

## III.6 — THE FILTER STACK

The tunnel was named in the last book, and the naming stopped where a naming stops. *A reality tunnel is a
persistent render filter* — three words, each of them cleaned of what it dragged in, and not one word
yet about where a tunnel comes from, what happens when two of them are in the same room, or whether
anything can be done to either.

That is this chapter, and it is mechanics rather than terms. Book II said what the words mean; this
book runs them. Running this one means answering the three questions a reader is entitled to the
moment the definition is granted. Where does a filter come from. What happens when two people who
have different ones look at the same thing. And the one everybody actually wants, which is whether
it can be changed.

---

**Start with the word in the title, because the other two were disciplined and this one was not.**

*Filter* was cleaned at the definition: nothing unfiltered upstream, no bare vantage underneath.
*Persistent* was given the criterion the usual list never states. **Stack** came in with them, did no
work at the time, and was never examined — and it carries a picture. Separate sheets in an order.
Each one lying on the one below. Each one liftable off with the rest left where they are.

There are no sheets. There is one seed, and the filters are not sitting on it; they are what it has
become. The stack is not an assembly of parts and there is no operation that takes one out.

What the word earns — and it earns this much, which is why it is kept — is **order**. Some of what
you bring was laid down before the rest, and the rest was laid down onto it. The language was cut at
its joints before you had anything to say. The silent certainties of the people standing nearest were
in you before you could weigh a sentence. What was acquired at thirty was acquired by somebody the
first thirty years had already made, and it went in along the grain that was there.

So the order is real and the layers are not, and everything said here about change rests on that
difference. An early stratum is not a lower sheet. It is the thing
every later one is made partly out of. That is why an edit near the bottom is not a larger version of
an edit near the top — it is a different operation, performed with an instrument that is downstream of
what it is working on. Which is a real difficulty, and is stated here as a fact about the mechanics
rather than as a discouragement, because a difficulty with a shape can be worked and a mystery cannot.

---

**Now where a filter comes from, and the account we take whole belongs to somebody.**

The five that get recited — era, language, ritual, trauma, choice — are all things that happened
inside a life. But a seed is not only a biography. The organs are not an autobiography. The joints in
a language were cut by people who are not available to be asked, over a run of time no life covers,
and the tempo at which a room arrives is not something that happened to you either. If a filter is a
repetition that outlived its occasion, then some of the occasions were never yours, and an account of
installation that only reaches back to birth has stopped a long way short.

**Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson and Eleanor Rosch, *The Embodied Mind*, 1991**, have the best
account anyone has written of the part that runs further back, and they give it in a single line. They
had been through cognitivism and through the emergence programme, putting the same three questions to
each, and when their turn came they answered the first one like this:

> Question 1: What is cognition?
> Answer: Enaction: A history of structural coupling that brings forth a world.

A history. Not a mechanism, not an architecture, not a stock of representations — a history, with the
weight on the word. What a system meets, it meets with what its previous meetings made of it, and
what it is, its milieu has had a hand in. The relation runs both directions and they are exact about
which two things are doing it: *organism and medium mutually specify each other.* Nothing was fixed in
advance for the other to adapt to. That is co-constitution, run backwards along the whole length of
the thing.

And the timescales come in one clause of theirs, which is the sentence this chapter needed most.
Writing about colour categories — red, green, yellow, blue, and also *yellow-with-green*, which they
report as an otherwise rare basic category encoded by many Native American languages of the Pacific
Northwest — they call the lot of them *experiential, consensual, and embodied*, and then say what they
rest on: *they depend upon our biological and cultural history of structural coupling.*

Biological *and* cultural. One clause, two clocks, and no seam between them. The distinction the five
recited items keep implying — that the deep filters are the ones you were born with and the installed
ones are the ones you acquired — does not survive contact with that sentence, and it should not.
Installed and inherited are not two kinds of filter. They are two lengths of the same operation, and
only one of them is short enough to have happened where you could watch.

There is more in their account than a slogan, and the part that matters here is what the history does
*not* do. Under coupling, the medium *triggers (but does not specify)* — their parenthesis, in the
middle of a numbered list about evolution. What you meet does not dictate what gets laid down. It sets
off a process whose outcome is decided by what the thing already is. That is the mechanics of
installation stated in five words, and it is why two people can be in one era and come out of it
carrying different filters: an era is a perturbation, not an instruction.

---

Their own paradigm case has no metabolism in it, and this is worth a sentence because it is a fact
about the mechanism rather than about them.

To show what a coupling history does, they built **Bittorio** — a ring of Boolean cells with a
rule, dropped into a random soup of ones and zeros. Nobody wrote it a program for noticing anything.
But odd-length sequences of perturbation changed its configuration and even-length ones left it as it
was, and so, in their words, *given its rule and given its form of structural coupling, this Bittorio
becomes an "odd sequence recognizer."* A distinction that exists for that ring and for nothing else,
never designed, laid down by what it went through.

They then say, plainly and without being asked, that they do not intend to suggest such a simple
closure and coupling is *sufficient for a system to experience a world.* Take the disclaimer at face
value; they were being careful, and being careful is the reason their book is worth this much of a
chapter. But note what it is a disclaimer about. It is about experience. **The machinery of
installation was demonstrated on a ring of Boolean cells, and it worked there.** Whatever is true
about who has an inside — and the previous chapter has already settled that, on grounds of its own —
the *stack* was never the part that required a metabolism.

One sentence of cut, because the cut was made last time and re-making it here would be a second
performance of a single argument. They put the render at the far end of the history, and a far end is
a clock. We keep the history and leave the render where it was put two chapters ago: at the point of
contact, with nothing before it. **The credit is not conditional
on the correction.** They found how a seed becomes the seed it is, and nobody has done it better.

---

**Two players, one Ground, two worlds — and this is where the mechanics start doing work rather than
being admired.**

Nothing further is needed to get there. The procedure runs on the seed. Two seeds are two different
things for it to run on. There is one Ground and it is at no position, so it contributes no version
and settles no ties. Two renders follow, and they follow the way a sum follows — not as an event that
befell an evening, but as what the definition already said.

So divergence is not a discrepancy in want of an explanation. **The explanation is owed by the other
picture**, the one where two people receive the same world and one of them has smudged it, and the
debt on that picture is heavy: it has to say where the unsmudged copy is kept.

Two people stand in front of one radiograph. One of them has spent nine years on films and the other
has not, and there is a shadow in the upper left. The one is not inferring the shadow, and is not
looking harder; the shadow is simply there, in the way the word on this page is there rather than
assembled from strokes. The other is not failing. They are in a room that does not have a shadow in
it. Nine years of a particular repetition is a stratum, laid on everything under it, and it did what
strata do.

Now the word a reader has been supplying since the section opened, which has to be handed back. *Both
correct.* It cannot be paid. Correctness is a relation between two structures — the Ground has
none, having no position to have one from — and this was settled where the map was retired. Neither
render is correct about the Ground. Neither is incorrect about it either, which is the half that costs
something. What is true is the negative and it is enough: **neither of them is the error.**

The people who found this out from the other end put the same refusal in print in 1991, and it is worth
having in their words, because it is the sentence a friendly reader reaches for and it is wrong. What
they insist on, they say, is the codetermination of organism and environment, and it is not to be
confused with

> the more commonplace view that different perceiving organisms simply have different perspectives on
> the world. This view continues to treat the world as pregiven; it simply allows that this pregiven
> world can be viewed from a variety of vantage points. The point we are making, however, is
> fundamentally different.

*Different perspectives on the world* keeps the world and hands out seats around it. It is the
generous-sounding version and it concedes nothing, because the thing every seat is a seat on is still
sitting there, and a best seat is available the moment anyone wants one.

They close that door with data rather than with a preference, and the case is colour. Some visual
systems need two dimensions to describe their colour space, some three, some four, possibly some five;
the histories that produced them are *vastly different*; and the conclusion they draw is the one this
chapter needs. Our perceived world of colour, they write, *should not be considered to be the optimal
"solution" to some evolutionarily posed "problem."* It is *one possible and viable phylogenic pathway
among many others.* And of coupling histories in general: *not optimal; they are, rather, simply
viable.*

There is the rank ordering of worlds, refused — not on metaphysical grounds, and not by us. Viable is
a floor and optimal is a summit, and the difference between those two words is the difference between
a plurality of real worlds and a queue.

---

**Then the slide, which arrives on schedule and takes a form worth respecting.**

If renders differ and none is the error, surely the Ground is up for grabs. Whatever anyone's history
delivers is what there is, and the thing underneath has no say.

It does not follow, and the reason is not that the Ground is too solid to argue with. **There is no
counterparty.** Negotiation happens between positions — parties that want, concede, hold out, arrive
somewhere. Every one of those verbs needs a somewhere to be performed from, and there is no somewhere
that the Ground is at. It is not resisting the renegotiation; it is not the sort of thing a
renegotiation could be with. The difference between
two tunnels lives entirely in the two vantages, and it never touches what neither of them is standing
apart from.

Which leaves the question the last book put on the page rather than leave to be found: if a render
cannot be wrong about the Ground, how is anybody ever wrong about anything?

It is answered here, and not in the direction the question's shape suggests. Relativism's real content is
that nobody can be wrong, and this is close to its opposite. Everybody is wrong, constantly, and about
the only thing there has ever been to be wrong about: **other positions.** What is the case at another
vantage is a determinate fact. It is not a matter of taste, it does not vary with how one feels about
it, and it is establishable — by going and standing somewhere, by asking, by the long unglamorous work
of finding out what a room is like for the person in it. Error does not need a territory. It needs
somewhere else, and there is nothing but somewhere else.

That is a heavier burden than the one relativism carries, not a lighter one. The doctrine that all
tunnels are worlds does not excuse anybody from the work of finding out what is in another one. It
removes the excuse — because there is no master copy to consult instead, and no seat from which the
matter could be settled without going.

---

**And the edit, which is the sentence out of all of this most likely to be quoted by somebody it was
not written for.**

The mechanics say three things and then stop, because the practice is a later book's.

**You do not edit the render.** There is nothing there to reach. A render is what is the case at a
position, not a standing object with a state that persists between glances, and reaching for it is
reaching for the wrong noun. What can be edited is what the procedure runs on.

**The seed takes an edit the way it took the installation** — by repetition, over time, at the pace of
whatever is being laid down. Not by preference, not by insight, not by having understood this chapter.
The criterion that put the five on the list is the same criterion that governs the way off it, and it
has already ruled out the version everybody wants. Wanting is not on that list, it cannot get onto the
list by being stronger, and nothing in this chapter is a route by which it might. What went in that
way comes out that way, or does not come out.

**And an edited filter does not deliver a chosen world**, because the render was never solely yours.
Change what you bring and the meeting changes; the meeting is still a meeting. This is not a
consolation and it is not a limit imposed from outside — it is the same two-sidedness that made a
world available in the first place, seen from the side where it costs something.

Book VIII is where that is done rather than described, and it will take its length.

---

What this chapter has actually removed is the last quiet place priority had left to stand.

Book III has been taking *before* off things — off the whole, off its origin, off the world and the
one meeting it. The version that survives longest is the smallest and the most
personal: the sense that underneath the accumulated seed there is a person who would have seen it
plain, and that the filters are what happened to them.

There is no such person and never was one. The strata are not covering a witness; the strata are the
witness. And that is not a smaller thing to be than the one who would have seen it plain — it is the
only thing there has ever been to be, and it is the only thing that can be worked on, which is
precisely why the work is available at all.
