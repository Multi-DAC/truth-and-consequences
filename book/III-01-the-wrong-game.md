# BOOK III — THE GAME

## III.1 — THE WRONG GAME

A frame is not an ornament. Everything named in the last book is about to be put to work, and the
thing it gets put to work inside is a game — which makes the first job of this book the removal of
the game the reader already has.

Part of that was done early, at the ground, before the word *game* had been used at all. *Not the
server*: elsewhere, hosting, switchable, with somebody in the room who could reach over and stop it.
There is no elsewhere, so there is no room, so there is nobody in it. That is spent, and it will not
be argued twice.

But a picture is not a single object, and taking one part of it away does not take the rest. Remove
the hardware and the reader does not set the whole thing down. They keep the part that was never
about machinery to begin with.

They keep the maker.

---

**He was denied by description and left without a name.**

Four things were refused at the ground. Three of them were refused with their objects in plain view
— a server, an engine, a map, each a thing you can point at and each somebody's live position.
The fourth pointed at nobody at all. *Prior, external, intending. Three properties, none of them
available.*

Three properties and no name, in a book whose entire business is naming.

He has a name. He has had one since the second century, and the people who gave it to him wrote the
most complete account of him that anyone has produced before or since — so complete that the men who
set out to destroy it had to reproduce it at length in order to attack it, which is the only reason
any of it survived to be read.

---

**The demiurge.**

The system is not a mood and not a folk memory; it is machinery, and the machinery is the point.

In the Valentinian account, the last and youngest of the divine emanations reaches beyond what she
can hold, and the reaching is a passion. She produces, alone, outside the pairing that every other
emanation was made within, something formless. She is restored. The formlessness is not: it is put
outside the Pleroma, and outside the Pleroma it produces, in turn, a maker. That maker builds
everything you have ever seen.

Irenaeus of Lyon, writing to refute them, reports the office as they defined it: *they affirm,
therefore, that he was constituted the Father and God of everything outside of the Pleroma, being
the creator of all animal and material substances.* Father, God, creator — every title a religion
gives to the highest thing, held by a functionary who is not it.

And then the detail that makes the whole system unforgettable, in the same report, one section on:
*he formed the heavens, yet was ignorant of the heavens; he fashioned man, yet knew not man; he
brought to light the earth, yet had no acquaintance with the earth.* He is not malicious. He is not
even negligent. He is working with his eyes shut and does not know that his eyes are shut. *The
Demiurge imagined that he created all these things of himself, while he in reality made them in
conjunction with the productive power* of the thing that produced him.

He is, in short, a middle manager who believes he owns the company. Irenaeus records the sentence
they put in his mouth for it — and it is a sentence out of Isaiah, which is the part that got
everyone killed: *being incapable of recognising any spiritual essences, imagined himself to be God
alone, and declared through the prophets, I am God, and besides me there is none else.*

Now set that beside the same claim from a school Irenaeus files separately, in a book found in a jar
in 1945 rather than in a prosecutor's summary. The *Apocryphon of John* has its own name for the
maker — Yaltabaoth — and its own version of the same event, a production undertaken *without the
consent of the Spirit, without her consort, and without his consideration.* Then the boast, with the
reason for it attached: *for he said, "I am God and there is no other God beside me," for he is
ignorant of his strength, the place from which he had come.*

And then, because this is a text with a sense of theatre, the correction. *A voice came forth from
the exalted aeon-heaven: "The Man exists and the son of Man."* Something above him tells him he is
not alone up there, and he is the last to know.

Two schools who agreed on very little, and the hostile witness who wanted both of them erased. All
three describe the same figure. A maker who is not the highest thing, a world that is second-rate
because of what made it, and an inside you were put in by somebody who does not understand where he
is either.

**Here is what this chapter is actually about.**

Strip the proper nouns off that and hand it to somebody in 2026 who has never heard the word
demiurge, and they will recognise it immediately, because it is what they already think.

Somebody made this. He is not God — he is a technician, or a civilisation, or an intelligence a few
floors up, and there is a floor above him too. He does not fully understand what he built. There is
something wrong with the build. You are inside it and you are not from here, and the wrongness you
have felt about it since you were a child is not a mood, it is a leak from the outside coming in.

That is a second-century heresy, and it is a dinner-table conversation, and nobody at the table
knows they are reciting Valentinus. Eighteen hundred years, two civilisations that share almost no
assumptions, one religious and one technological — and the picture that comes out is the same
picture, down to the ignorance of the maker.

**The identity is not a coincidence, and it is not a borrowing. It is what the human mind does with
the word *inside*.** Given a world you are inside of, the mind reaches for someone outside who put
you there, and it will build that someone out of whatever the century has lying around. Aeons in the
second. Compute in this one. The furniture changes and the floor plan never does.

Which means it cannot be answered by arguing about the furniture.

---

**The cut is not that nobody made this well.**

That is the atheist's version and it concedes everything. *There is no evidence of a maker.* *If
there were a maker he would have done better than childhood leukaemia.* *The universe is
unauthored.* Every one of those keeps the chair and argues about who is sitting in it, or reports
that it is empty. An empty chair is still a chair. It can be filled later, on better evidence, by a
more plausible candidate, and the whole thing starts again.

This is not that. This is the removal of the chair.

**A maker is prior to what it makes.** Not greater than it, not smarter than it, not more real than
it — prior. That is the entire content of the word. There is a state of affairs in which the thing
is not, and a state of affairs in which it is, and the maker stands on the near side of that
boundary while the made stands on the far side. Take that asymmetry away and *made* has nothing left
to mean. A maker simultaneous with its product in every respect, with no interval and no before, is
not a maker; it is a description of the product.

**Priority is a position in time.** It is not a rank and not a dignity. To be prior is to be located
earlier, and *earlier* is a relation that holds between arrangements — between one state of some
thing and another state of it. Before and after are what an arrangement has.

**And there is no arrangement that everything is in.** There is no moment at which everything that
could be the case was not the case, waiting to be brought about, because *moment* is something that
happens inside what is the case. The whole does not sit in a time and take a turn. Ask what was
there before it and the question runs out of vocabulary before it runs out of confidence.

So the demiurge does not fail the audition.

**The role does not exist.**

There is no seat marked *prior to everything* that a candidate could be judged unworthy of, which is
why no improvement to the candidate touches this. A kinder maker, a more competent maker, a maker
with better information — each is a better occupant of a position with no coordinates. And a
regress does not save it either, which the Gnostics of all people should have seen, because they
were the ones who ran it. They looked at the maker of this world, asked what made *him*, and got an
honest answer: something above him, which he cannot see. That is the correct move and it is the move
almost nobody makes.

Then they stopped. They gave the regress a top and called it the Father, and to keep the top from
being one more maker they said it does not make — it emanates, overflows, unfolds. **But an
overflow still has a direction.** What overflows is downstream of what it overflowed from, and
downstream is the same asymmetry wearing softer clothes. Emanation is the attempt to have
origination without time, and it does not work, because origination *is* the time.

Run the regress the whole way and what you reach is not a better maker and not a maker who declines
to make. It is the discovery that the series was pointing at nothing — that *what came first* was
never a question about this, only a habit carried over from the things inside it, all of which do
have a first.

---

Take the maker out and things leave with him that the reader may not have noticed were attached.

There is no botch. A botch requires an intention that fell short of itself, and there is no
intending party at the perimeter to have fallen short. There is nowhere you were put, because being
put somewhere requires somebody to have done the putting, and the word *here* stops implying a
consignment. The wrongness that the Gnostic read as evidence loses the thing it was evidence of.

And the rest of that has already been said from the other end. The last book refused the rescue on
the ending's terms — it argued that nothing is under repair, and this chapter does not argue it
again. It only notes that the two arrivals meet: a rescue needs somebody to have made the mess, and
there is nobody to have made it.

What is left, then, is a game with nobody who wrote it.

**And a game with no designer is not a game that is less designed.** Nothing has loosened. The
constraints are exactly where they were an hour ago, the world is precisely as difficult, and no
rule has become negotiable because the rule-maker was found not to exist — because the constraints
were never his in the first place, and a constraint does not need an author in order to hold.

What changes is not how tight the game is. What changes is whose it is.

That question has one answer left, and the next chapter is what it costs.
