# BOOK IV — THE ATLAS

## IV.3 — THE LIVING, NON-HUMAN

Brush one of the trigger hairs inside a Venus flytrap and, most of the time, nothing happens at all.

Brush a second within about thirty seconds and the trap shuts in under a tenth of a second. Keep
going — a third, a fourth — and the plant begins making jasmonate, the hormone that runs its wound
response. From the fifth on it produces digestive enzymes, and it produces more of them the more
arrive. So the trap does not simply close on things. It closes, and then works out whether what it
closed on is worth the cost of digesting, and it settles that question by counting.

It also counts wrong, and the way it counts wrong is worth more than the counting.

**It is not counting touches.** What crosses the threshold is the trap's own electrical excitations —
two of them, the second inside the window. A single deflection can raise two, if the hair is moved at
a middling speed; move it much faster or much slower and that same single touch raises one, and the
trap stays open. A slug crossing slowly can shut a trap that a quick insect leaves open. Nothing in
the plant tells these cases apart, because the plant never had them. What it has is its own spikes.

And the count leaks. The mechanism is a concentration that steps up with each excitation and drains
between them; fall below the line and it is back to nothing and the sequence starts over. Thirty
seconds is not a rule the plant is obeying. Thirty seconds is how long its arithmetic survives.

Which makes this the first thing in the census that has a memory rather than a record — and the
difference is not size, and it is not fidelity. **What makes a record a memory is that it can be
lost.** Stone keeps its past perfectly and keeps it for nobody; nothing about the keeping is
addressed to a next moment. The trap's count is worse by every measure available — shorter, cruder,
wrong about slugs — and it is *held*, by the thing that is going to use it, against the moment it is
for. A loop that closes, and then closes again, with one turn kept against the last. Here it is, in a
plant, running on chemistry, with nothing in it any anatomist would call a nerve.

---

**What a plant registers, stated before anyone decides how impressed to be.**

Light, and not merely its presence: direction, intensity, spectrum, and the length of the day, which
is how a plant knows the season without having lived through one. Also the ratio of red to far-red —
leaves eat red and let far-red pass, so a leaf under another leaf sits in reddened light, and a plant
that measures the ratio is measuring, at a distance, through an opaque object, *that something alive
is above it*. Gravity, resolved at the root tip to a fraction of a degree. Touch, accumulated: handle
a seedling daily and it grows shorter and thicker than its unhandled sibling. Injury. Water, as a
gradient, in the dark. And volatile chemistry — the air near a torn leaf changes, and neighbours
downwind of it change their own chemistry within hours.

Count the dimensions and this is not a narrow position. There are more of them than a reader expects
and more than several animals manage.

What it does not have is speed, and it does not have a place. Signals cross a plant at millimetres to
centimetres a second, along no dedicated line, and nearly everything a plant does about anything it
does over hours or days. There is nowhere in it that the light and the gravity and the wounding
arrive together and are held against one another. **The dimensions are many and the holding is
thin** — which is the reverse of the previous entry, where the holding was absent and the dimension
was one, and it is the first evidence in this atlas that the two vary independently. Sensitivity and
integration are not one axis with a slow end and a fast end. They are two, and a position can be rich
in the first and poor in the second, and no ranking survives that fact.

---

**Two ways to be wrong here, and only one of them is live.**

Downward — *it is tropism, it is chemistry, there is nobody in there* — was met one chapter ago and
does not get re-run.

Upward is live, and this is where the declared lean takes its second test — the harder one, because
this time there is evidence, and the evidence is charming, and it runs our way. **A lean is not
tested where there is nothing to lean on. It is tested the first time something is offered to it.**

The forest network is the case. In its popular form the claim is that trees are joined underground by
fungal threads into a shared network, that resources move along it, and that mature trees send sugar
and defence signals preferentially to their own seedlings. It has been in books, documentaries and a
very large number of talks. In 2023 three researchers went back through the literature it rests on
and reported that the popular account has run a long way ahead of it: that the claim such networks
are widespread in forests, and the claim that what moves through them improves seedling performance,
are insufficiently supported — field results vary too widely, admit other explanations, or are too
local to generalise from — and that the particular claim about mature trees favouring their own
offspring has no published peer-reviewed evidence behind it at all. They also found the citation
record had drifted: papers were being cited for conclusions the papers themselves had not drawn.

Now the part that is about us rather than about forests.

**A result that would be marvellous for this book is not evidence for this book, and the only way to
demonstrate that is to say in advance what it would change.** So: nothing. If the network is real and
rich, the plant's card gains a line under SEES and the world is more interesting than we said. If it
resolves into roots doing root things in soil that happens to contain fungus, the card on the next
page is unchanged, to the word. That a plant has an inside was argued in Book II from the ground up,
and it does not have a forest underneath it.

**That is the one real dividend an unhedged claim pays: it cannot be embarrassed by evidence it never
leaned on.** A framework that would have been strengthened by the wood-wide web is a framework that
was weakened in 2023, and a great many warm and well-meant accounts of plant life are now quietly in
that position, having borrowed against a finding to say a thing they could have said outright.

The lean says: where the evidence is thin, attribute rather than withhold. It does not say: where the
evidence is thin and a beautiful study exists, lean on the study. Those two come apart for the first
time right here, at the first entry in the census where somebody has actually done the fieldwork.

> **A VENUS FLYTRAP**
> **SEES:** Its own excitations, counted, and the interval between them. Light — direction, spectrum,
> day length, and the reddened shadow that means something living is overhead. Gravity. Touch,
> accumulated. Injury. The changed air near a torn neighbour.
> **NULL SPACE:** ∅ What it closed on. A fly, a slug, a raindrop and an interested finger are the
> same object to the trap, because the count runs over spikes and every spike is like every other.
> ◐ Its own interval — about thirty seconds, held by draining, so the arithmetic is always freshest
> at the end and can never be checked against its own beginning.
> **COMPLEMENTS:** Anything that can hold the count and the prey in one place. The trap cannot mark
> its answer against the animal.
> **BOUNDARY:** Speed. Move a hair much faster or much slower than a middling rate and the second
> excitation never comes. The trap has not failed. It has worked exactly as built, and stayed open,
> and there was a fly.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** Ask what a position is counting *with*. It will not be the thing it
> appears to be counting, and its errors will therefore be shaped like the sensor rather than like
> the world — systematic, repeatable, and invisible from where they are made.

---

**One chapter.**

Behind this one lies everything alive that is not us — somewhere between two and ten million species
on most estimates, the large majority never described, every one of them a distinct settlement of
what can arrive and what cannot. The next chapter is about one species. That proportion is not
defensible and will not be defended here. It is a fact about the position taking the census, printed
where it can be seen, and it is the plainest measurement in this book of the shape of a human null
space: *animal* resolves for us as one thing and *us* resolves as many. Nothing was measured to
produce that ratio. It arrives with the render, before anybody starts counting.

So the entries that follow are not representatives, and nothing here is a survey. They are the places
where the framework has something to say that it could not have said one chapter ago.

---

**A body with no centre.**

In the Malheur National Forest in eastern Oregon there is a single individual of the honey fungus
*Armillaria ostoyae* covering something over two thousand acres — three and a half square miles of
ground — aged, from how fast it grows, at somewhere between two and eight thousand years. Nearly all
of it is thread. It is the largest organism anyone has found.

A fungus does not have a shape it is growing toward. It has a rule about where to put the next
hypha, applied at every tip at once, and the body is the accumulated history of that rule meeting the
ground it met. **The body is the search.** There is no plan of it anywhere, and the *anywhere*
includes the fungus.

Which forces the question this grade raises and the last one could not: how many insides is that?

The framework's answer is the one the question was not expecting. **It does not have to be one.** A
position is wherever the loops close, and in a mycelium they close at several sizes at the same time
— at the growing tip, at the advancing front, across the network. Print a card at each size at which
they close. Neither card is the true one and neither summarises the other, and there is no fact of
the matter waiting underneath to settle which is the real organism, because *organism* was our word
for the size that mattered to us. **Individuation is not something the census discovers about a
being. It is a statement about which loop is being asked after.** This is the point at which the
atlas stops being a list of things and becomes a list of closures, and it is worth flagging as this
chapter's own reach, because a later book will make a reader pay for it.

And one neighbour worth printing alongside, on condition of first saying what it is not.
*Physarum polycephalum* is a slime mould. It is not a fungus, not a plant and not an animal, and it
gets shelved beside fungi mostly because it looks like one. Arrange oat flakes on a dish in the
pattern of the towns around Tokyo and it grows a network between them that a Japanese team found
closely resembled the rail system those towns had actually built. And it navigates by leaving a
trail of extracellular slime and refusing ground that already carries some. Its record of where it
has been is not inside it. It is on the floor.

**The proof of that is the cruel version of the experiment.** You do not take the memory away by
wiping it. You take it away by coating the entire dish — so that everywhere is marked, everywhere
reads as visited, and the organism is lost in a way it has no means of detecting, because the signal
it steers by has not gone missing. It has gone uniform. That is the most exact demonstration in this
atlas of what a null space is like from inside. Nothing is absent. Everything reads the same.

> **THE MYCELIUM**
> **SEES:** Gradients, at every tip at once — sugar, nitrogen, phosphorus, water, the chemistry of a
> root it has met, the chemistry of a rival it is meeting. And, in some of its neighbours, where its
> own body has already been.
> **NULL SPACE:** ∅ Its own extent. There is no place in a network where the network arrives; a body
> with no centre has nowhere to be a whole in, and the largest organism on the planet has never once
> been anywhere in its entirety. ◐ Anything that has to be settled in less than a day.
> **COMPLEMENTS:** Anything with a seat — any position that is somewhere, such that it could be
> somewhere else and know the difference.
> **BOUNDARY:** Severance. Cut it and no injury is reported, because there is no addressee. Two
> fronts carry on, and whether there are now two of it or still one is a question with no answer
> inside the fungus and a different answer in every discipline outside it.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** When looking for a being, do not look for a centre first. A position
> can be spread out; the loops can close at several sizes in one body; and a memory can be kept
> somewhere other than in the thing that uses it — in which case saturating the terrain destroys it
> more completely than erasing it would.

**Now the question everybody actually asks, which is about time.**

A bee's eye refreshes roughly four times faster than yours. A fast fly's, faster still. So the
intuition, which nearly everyone has and which is wrong: the fly must live in slow motion, our world
a leisurely ballet to it, the swatting hand arriving like a slow tide. And its mirror image, equally
wrong: a snail's eye is among the slowest known, so the snail must experience a stuttering
slideshow, stills with darkness between them.

Both fail on the same missing number. An eye has two clocks and everyone reaches for one. The famous
one is the refresh rate — the fastest flicker still distinguishable from a steady light. The
forgotten one is the integration window: how long the system pools incoming light before committing
to a glimpse. And they move against each other by physical necessity. An eye that samples fast can
only pool briefly between samples. An eye that samples slowly pools a long time to gather enough
light — which is how the slowest eyes in the deep sea see anything at all. Fast eyes take quick
snapshots; slow eyes take long exposures.

The texture of a moment is set by the two together: how many fresh measurements land inside one
window. Densely filled windows, seamless experience. Windows arriving nearly empty, grain.

And there is one place the test runs clean on co-measured numbers, which is your own eye. At night
you see with rods: refresh around fifteen per second, pooling over about a tenth of a second. In
daylight, cones: refresh near sixty, pooling over something between fifteen and fifty milliseconds.
The rate goes up fourfold and the window comes down fourfold and the product does not move. **You
change sampling speed by a factor of four every evening and time does not change texture at dusk.**

Then the part that has to be told against ourselves, because it is the reason the finding is worth
anything. Run the same product across species with published numbers and it does not cancel. It
tracks the refresh rate almost exactly. On that measure the fast-eyed animal really does get the
more seamless present, and the tidy result dies on contact with the data.

The failure pointed at the mistake, and the mistake is the whole thing. **An eye is a peripheral.**
Its refresh rate is the rate of one channel — how fast a retina can be driven — and it says nothing
directly about how fast a position assembles a single moment out of the retina and the ear and the
skin and the gut reporting at once. A sensor's specification is free to swing enormously across the
animal kingdom precisely because it is a sensor; a body can afford a fast eye or a cheap one. What
cannot afford to swing is whatever binds. And at that level the conservation is already in the
literature under another name: across a seventeen-thousand-fold range of mammalian brain size, the
brain's binding rhythms stay roughly put — and the brain pays to keep them there, thickening its
long-range wiring as it grows, holding the timing fixed while everything physical about it scales by
four orders of magnitude.

The evidence grades are not equal and the gap is where a reader should stand. Within one eye, clean
and co-measured. Across mammals, strong, but it is the neural rhythm — the measurable correlate of a
binding rate, not a felt moment — it is mammals only, and it is a compressed band rather than one
rigid clock. Nobody has a bee's binding rate on a bench, and there is no procedure on offer for
getting one. So *the bee's moment is near ours in length* is the framework's prediction, carried by a
regularity that holds every place it can be measured, and it is not a measurement of a bee.

What is dead outright is the picture everyone starts with. **A fast eye does not buy a fast life.**

And now the thing that is not a result about bees at all, which is the chapter's own finding and the
reason the temporal question belongs in an atlas rather than in a book about insects.

**You have never experienced a gap in your own experience.** Not the anaesthetic, not the dreamless
stretch, not the fraction of a second your eye spends mid-flick and blind several times a minute. It
is tempting to imagine a sparsely sampled position *enduring* its dark intervals, sitting through
them like a held breath. But a gap is not an interval anyone sits through. **A gap is the absence of
whoever would have sat through it.** Nobody is home to register it, and on the far side it resumes
with no seam, because a seam needs two edges and only one of them was ever there.

Which means every position in this census is seamless to itself. The bee's, the snail's, yours. Grain
is real, and grain is a third-person quantity: it lives in the comparison, in the measurement against
some other clock, and never once in the experiencing. **The bee does not feel grainy to the bee.**

And it leaves exactly one thing that can feel broken from inside — not sparseness but *conflict*,
when what a position binds into a single moment contradicts itself. The seam tears from the inside
out. It never tears at the gaps.

> **A HONEYBEE**
> **SEES:** Ultraviolet. The polarisation pattern of the sky — a compass written in light across the
> whole dome, from a patch of blue, invisible to every human eye that has ever opened. Flower shape,
> and the electric fields that hang about flowers. Odour at a resolution we have no vocabulary for.
> Another bee's dance, read in the dark, by touch.
> **NULL SPACE:** ∅ Red. And the decision its own colony is in the middle of making. ◐ Its own grain
> — the length of its moment is not a difference available to it, and neither is the length of
> yours; no position is issued a second clock to check its first against.
> **COMPLEMENTS:** Us, in one direction and not the flattering one. We get red and the calendar. It
> gets the sky.
> **BOUNDARY:** Distance. A forager's report of how far it flew is built from how much visual
> texture swept past on the way out, so fly it down a narrow tunnel, where the walls are close
> and the flow is enormous, and it reports a journey many times longer than the one it made — and
> dances the number with total conviction, and is believed, and other bees fly out to nothing.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** Where a position's measure of the world is built out of its own
> traffic, the error will be confident, communicable, and shared. Nothing in the sender marks it and
> nothing in the receiver catches it.

**And the same animal, one size up.**

When a colony swarms it has to choose a home, and the choosing is the best-observed decision made by
anything in this chapter. Scouts go out, find cavities, and assess them — volume, the size and height
of the entrance, draught, the state of what is inside — and come back and dance. Better sites get
longer dances; longer dances recruit more scouts; recruited scouts go and assess for themselves
rather than taking the dance's word. And the choice is not made by counting support at the cluster.
It is made at the sites: when enough scouts are standing in one cavity at one time — a couple of
dozen — that threshold is what tips the swarm into leaving.

So there is a decision, it is real, it is often excellent, and **it is nowhere in any bee.** No
individual in that colony has ever registered the choice the colony made. Each one registered a
cavity, a dance, and the press of other bodies in a dark hole. The comparison of sites happened. It
did not happen anywhere.

This is the first entry in the atlas whose null space includes **the being it is a part of** — and
that is a different kind of blindness from any so far. The trap cannot reach the fly because of what
a trap is made of. The bee cannot reach the colony's decision because of the *size* it is, and it
would have the same trouble if it were far cleverer. Which is worth setting down carefully and
leaving alone, because the next book but one is where it stops being about insects, and the position
that cannot see the thing it is inside will be the reader's.

> **THE COLONY**
> **SEES:** Sites, compared. Cavity volume, entrance area, height, exposure, the comb already there —
> ranked against each other, which is an operation no bee in it performs.
> **NULL SPACE:** ∅ Itself choosing. There is no place in the swarm where the choice is written; the
> choice *is* the fact that enough scouts stood in one hole at one time. ◐ Any site nobody happened
> to fly over, which it will never know it did not consider.
> **COMPLEMENTS:** Its own bees, who have the local detail it does not — and who are, at this size,
> its instruments rather than its members.
> **BOUNDARY:** Speed and novelty together. Given hours it decides well. Given a choice it has no
> assessing behaviour for, it produces a decision of the usual shape at the usual pace with nothing
> behind it, and every bee involved is behaving correctly.
> **NAVIGATIONAL IMPLICATION:** A decision can be excellent and be held by nobody. Where you find one,
> do not look for who made it; look for what threshold was crossed, and by how many, and where.

---

**Now the phrase this chapter has been running on, because it looks like the thing the last chapter
refused.**

*As far as the framework permits.* Set it beside *in some sense conscious* and *a kind of awareness*
and it has the same shape and the same music, and a reader who has been paying attention should be
suspicious of it on sight.

So, once, precisely. **A hedge weakens a claim. A boundary locates an instrument.** Nothing said here
about the inside of a plant or a bee or a colony has been softened by a millimetre. The trap is
aware, the mycelium is aware, the colony is aware; each is stated flat and at full strength, and the
argument for all three was made in Book II and paid for there at a price. What is bounded is not the
claim. It is the *description* — how far an account of the shape can be run before it stops telling
one shape from another.

And a boundary, unlike a hedge, has a test, which is the whole of the difference. A hedge is built so
that nothing can bear on it. A boundary is a location: **the place past which two incompatible
descriptions of a position produce the same result.**

At the mineral there was no such place, because there was no result. The stone does not answer, and a
description that cannot be wrong cannot be bounded either — it can only be believed. Here, for the
first time, something answers. The trap can be counted at, and it will get slugs wrong on schedule.
The bee can be trained and mistrained, and it will fly the tunnel's error out into an empty field.
The colony can be offered a bad cavity and watched refusing it. Those are not confirmations. They are
better than confirmations: they are *disagreements we can lose*, which is the only currency in which
the fourth line of a card was ever payable.

Which makes this chapter the atlas's calibration, and it is worth saying plainly that the
calibration does not extend far past it. From the next chapter but one onward, the entries stop
answering in any form we know how to receive. A corporation returns results, but the results are
composed by people inside it who are themselves in the census, and that is a different epistemic
situation which will not be dressed up as this one. The boundary stays real. It stops being visible.

So: not a hedge, and not modesty either — modesty is the last thing this is. It is the fourth line of
our own card, and this is the one chapter of the atlas where we can walk up to it and put a hand on
it, and the honest use of that is to take the measurement now and carry it into the places where it
cannot be taken.

There is one entry left whose answers come back in a language we already read, and that is because we
wrote the language. It has been taking the census the entire time from inside one of its own
entries. And it is the only card in this atlas that cannot be checked from outside by anybody at all,
including — especially — by the position it describes.
