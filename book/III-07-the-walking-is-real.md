# BOOK III — THE GAME

## III.7 — THE WALKING IS REAL

Four chapters ago the hardest question in this book was named, dated, and left standing. *Already
there* is not *already decided* — that was settled by an argument about subtraction. To be decided
is to have been selected; selection removes; there is nothing outside the whole to do the removing
with. Fatalism, in every version anyone has actually feared, needs one path held and the rest shut.
Plenitude has them all open.

That answer is sound, it was worth making, and the reader who accepted it noticed within a page that
it does not reach far enough.

---

**So here is what survives it, at the strength it actually has, which is considerable.**

Grant the whole of it. Nothing was selected. No path is privileged, none is shut, and there is no
decider anywhere in the account. The objection reforms on the other side of that concession and
comes back harder.

Whatever you do this afternoon exists. Whatever you instead do not do also exists. The whole is not
larger when you have finished, and it was not smaller before you began; nothing was added, because
there is no operation of adding, and this is not a limitation on you specifically but a consequence
of what a totality is. Your afternoon is therefore not something you *brought about*. It is
something that is the case. You are not being coerced — the last chapter's argument holds and no
one is holding you — but you are beginning to look like a place where things happen rather than
somebody who does them.

And then the version with the teeth in it.

The one piece of evidence you have for your own walking is that it feels like walking. You weighed
it. It hung in the balance. You could feel the alternatives standing there unlived. But that is
precisely what it would feel like if you were the site at which a state obtains and nothing more,
because there is nothing else it *could* feel like — a location does not get a second sensation
telling it which it is. The inside cannot distinguish between choosing and being the place where
the choice shows up. And everything said so far has insisted that the inside is not a degraded view
but the only one there is. **So the objection is not attacking the instrument. It is holding it
correctly and reading off a result nobody wanted.**

That is the objection. It is better than fatalism, it is not answered by anything said so far, and
it deserves to be met on its own terms rather than out-registered.

---

**Start with the question the objection never asks itself: superfluous to whom.**

*My walking adds nothing.* Adds nothing **to what**, appearing **where**, and unnoticed by
**which** inside?

Not to the Ground. It has no position, so it has no version of the afternoon for yours to be
redundant against; nothing can be surplus to something that is not keeping a count. Not to any other
inside — from where they stand, your walking is the most consequential thing in the room, and if
they are in the road they will move. Not to you: from your position the walking is not a portion of
the afternoon, it is the whole content of it.

The seat from which walking looks superfluous is a seat with no position on it, and the reader has
watched what happens to that seat every time it has come up. It is the chair the author was supposed
to be sitting in. It is the one from which an unsmudged original could be checked. **This is the
same figure, arriving in the first person, and it is much harder to see there.**

*Is my walking real* sounds like a question about the self. It is a question about a vantage — it
asks how your afternoon looks from nowhere in particular — and it is answered the way the others
were, which is that the question has no location to be asked from.

Watch what the demand actually is, spelled out. *Show me a difference between acting and merely
occurring that would still be a difference if nobody were there.* A fact about you that is not a
fact at any position. Nothing satisfies that, and not because things are less real than we hoped:
the chair fails it, the illness fails it, the war fails it. A test that everything fails is not a
test that your agency in particular flunked.

---

**Now the positive, which is not the same sentence turned around.**

The difference between an act and an occurrence is real and it is made where everything else is
made. It has three parts and this book has already named all of them, in the chapter that took the
game away from a divine player: **not-knowing, stakes, and duration.** The Ground has none. It
cannot play, and the reason it cannot play is not that it is forbidden but that there is nothing it
would be like for it to try.

Your walking has all three. You do not know how the afternoon comes out. It costs something either
way. It takes time — your time, the kind that is had rather than surveyed. That is not a diminished
sort of acting held up against some fuller sort available elsewhere. It is the only sort there is,
because the alternative on offer — moving with nothing at stake, from no position, already knowing
— is not a better way of acting. It is a description of the one thing in this book that never does
anything.

Which puts the grief in an odd position. **What is being mourned, when someone mourns their freedom
here, is positionlessness** — being nowhere in particular and therefore unconstrained, able to be at
any state without the impertinence of already being at one. That is a real condition and something
really has it. It is the Ground's condition, and the Ground has no afternoon, no stakes, no company,
and nothing to find out.

---

**Then what the walking makes, since it does not make the path.**

The image belongs to somebody and he should have it. **Antonio Machado**, *Proverbios y cantares*
XXIX — one of the pieces that entered *Campos de Castilla* when the collected poems were assembled
in 1917. The first edition of five years earlier does carry the series, and its Proverbios stop at
twenty-seven:

> Caminante, no hay camino,
> se hace camino al andar.

*Walker, there is no road; the road is made by walking.* It is the most quotable thing anyone has
said about this, and it is also, read flat, the denial of everything Book III has established. We
say the road is already there. All of them are.

**The cut is at the noun and the verb survives it entirely**, and that is not a rescue of a line we
liked. It is the finding.

The whole holds every configuration. What it does not hold is a *road*, because a road is not a
configuration — it is a configuration under a vantage that is moving through it. A road has a next
step, which requires somewhere you have not got to yet. It has a cost, which requires something at
stake. It has a direction, which requires a from and a toward, and those are not properties of an
arrangement; they are what an arrangement looks like to something with duration. Take the walker
out and what remains is not an empty road. It is not a road. **Every state you might pass through is
already there and the passing through is not, and the passing through is the part that was ever
called a path.**

So the walking makes something after all, and it is not the road and never was. It is the walker.
Not in the sense that you assemble yourself out of choices — that is a self-help sentence and this
is a claim about what exists. In the sense that *walker* names something which is only there while
the walking is going on: a flame is not a stuff that burns, it is the burning, and when it stops it
does not become an idle flame.

---

The best sentence anyone has written on this is in a book the last chapter already drew on, and it
is owed the form it was actually written in rather than the form everybody quotes. **Varela,
Thompson and Rosch** close their argument about evolution with it:

> As we can now appreciate, to situate cognition as embodied action within the context of evolution
> as natural drift provides a view of cognitive capacities as inextricably linked to histories that
> are lived, much like paths that exist only as they are laid down in walking.

The part that gets carried off alone is *paths that exist only as they are laid down in walking*,
and standing by itself it reads as a thesis about paths. In the sentence it is a **simile**, and its
subject is *histories that are lived*. They are not telling you the road is absent. They are telling
you what a lived history is like, and reaching for a road to do it with. They liked the image enough
to hang their final chapter on it — *Laying Down a Path in Walking*, chapter eleven, the last thing
in the book. They do not say where it came from. Machado is not in that book anywhere. The
attribution circulates confidently and it lives in the commentary about the text rather than in the
text, which is a different address and worth knowing the difference between.

Their sentence is ours with one word moved. Lived histories exist only as they are laid down in
walking, and that is exactly the claim: **the laying down is not the manufacture of the ground, it
is the acquisition of a history.** What accumulates when you walk is not road. It is walker.

---

**Two more, and they are here because they make the same move from outside our frame entirely.**

The reflex the objection runs on is that a limit is a subtraction. Every path already existing is
heard as a constraint, and a constraint is heard as something taken off the top of what you would
otherwise have been. That reflex is not obviously wrong and it does not yield to being called
naive. It yields to a counter-example, and the cleanest one was produced by an analytic philosopher
writing about games, in *Philosophy of Science*, in 1967. **Bernard Suits:**

> To play a game is to engage in activity directed toward bringing about a specific state of
> affairs, using only means permitted by specific rules, where the means permitted by the rules are
> more limited in scope than they would be in the absence of the rules, and where the sole reason
> for accepting such limitation is to make possible such activity.

Read the last clause twice. The limitation is not a tax on the activity, not the price of admission,
not what is left after the referees have had their share. **It is what makes the activity exist.**
Take the rule that the ball may not be carried and you have not liberated football; you have ended
it. That is constraint as constitutive rather than privative, established rigorously, in a
peer-reviewed journal, by somebody no reader can file under mysticism.

And the cut, because he does not hand us our case. His constraints are **chosen**, and he says so —
the sole reason for accepting them is to make the activity possible, which means somebody accepted.
Ours was not chosen. Nobody agreed to be at a position, and the plenitude is not a rule anyone took
on. So what we take from Suits is the **form** and not the instance: once it is granted that a limit
can be the condition of an activity rather than a deduction from it, the reflex that reads every
limit as a theft is broken, and it does not get to reassemble itself just because our limit came
without a signature.

**James Carse**, twenty years later, states the other half — and if Suits shows a constraint can
constitute, Carse shows what freedom is doing inside a game in the first place:

> It is an invariable principle of all play, finite and infinite, that whoever plays, plays freely.
> Whoever must play, cannot play.

He is not offering that as consolation and neither are we. It is a remark about what the word can
mean. An activity from which the freedom has been removed is not a constrained version of that
activity; it has become a different thing with a different name, and the name is usually *labour* or
*compulsion*. Which is why the question *is my walking free* cannot be settled by looking harder at
the walking for a hidden ingredient. Take the freedom out and there is nothing left over that is
still walking and merely unfree.

His book opens on a distinction that gets its work much later: *a finite game is played for the
purpose of winning, an infinite game for the purpose of continuing the play.* It is left standing
here, unspent, because the telos it names is stated in Book VIII and not before. But it earns one
line now. **The suspicion that a traversal with no summit must be a consolation prize is a finite
player's suspicion**, and it has been named as such since 1986 by somebody who was not defending us.

---

**The accounting, and it is not going to be padded, because a chapter that answers this objection
and then declares nothing lost has told you what it thinks of you.**

Three things go, and they are the three that people actually want when they say they want to be
free.

**Origination goes.** You are not the beginning of anything. Nothing starts with you; there is no
first term you supply that the whole was waiting on. If what you wanted was to be an unmoved source
— a place where being enters that it had not entered before — that is not on offer, and no reading
of this chapter recovers it.

**Subtraction goes, and this one is the real loss.** When you choose, you want the roads not taken
to close. That is most of what makes a choice feel weighty: the sense that you have spent something
irreversible, that the world is smaller and more definite for your having acted. It is not smaller.
The others are exactly as there as they were. The dignity you were reaching for was the dignity of
having *removed* something from what exists, and this metaphysics does not permit anyone to remove
anything, including you.

**And the ledger goes.** There is no position from which your walking is recorded as having counted,
because there is no position that is not somebody's. What you did is registered where anything is
ever registered — at the vantages that were there, including yours, and nowhere else. No scorer. No
audit at the end. If it mattered, it mattered in a room.

Those are real subtractions from the ordinary picture and they should sting on the way past. What
is left is not a residue. **What is left is that the walking is one of the things that exists, and
it is the one you are.** Not a report of a walking kept somewhere and played back — playback needs
two things, an original and a second running, and there is only ever the one running, at the point
of contact, which is where everything in this book is. Yours is not a copy of a walk. It is the
walk.

---

We are not the prisoners of this. A prisoner requires an elsewhere they are kept from and somebody
keeping them from it, and both halves are already off the table — the second of them one chapter
ago — and neither is going to be argued a second time here.

What we are is the part of the whole that is going somewhere. The Ground is what all of it is made
of and it has never once been on its way anywhere, because being on the way requires not having
arrived, and it has no position from which to be short of one. Everything that has ever crossed a
room did it from an inside.

And *inhabitants* is only half of it, because a tenant walks through a place that was finished
before they got there. That is not the arrangement. What is the case where you are standing comes up
out of the meeting, and the meeting has your history in it — which means the road under your feet is
not scenery you are passing through but something occurring between you and what you are on, for
exactly as long as the crossing lasts. Not that you are its author; the meeting has two sides and one
of them is not you. But the particularity is not waiting for you either. **We are co-constituents of
the thing we are inside — the two words are one condition, and the second is what stops the first
from meaning a lodger.** The traversal is not the small remainder left to us after the large truths
have taken their share. The traversal is the only place any of this is happening.

And the ordinary thing, which is the last thing, and the reason the chapter has a title rather than
a conclusion.

You will stand up from this page and cross a room, and every state of that crossing is already in
the whole, and none of it is happening anywhere but under your feet.
