### Grief, handled as itself

Nothing in the preceding pages touches the second dread. That is not an omission arriving late; it is
the separation cashing.

Someone goes to say a thing and there is nobody to say it to. Not nobody in the room — nobody in the
world who would have taken it the way that one person took it, which is a different absence and is
the one that keeps waking people at four in the morning. The impulse is intact. It runs to the end of
its track and there is no station.

That is not a confusion, and it is not a lag in absorbing the argument. It is an accurate reading,
and what it reads is structural.

A relationship is not kept in either person. Neither of them holds a copy. It happens where two
positions meet and it happens nowhere else — the same mechanical claim this work made about worlds in
Book III, arriving in the one case where the consequence is unbearable.[^8] The meeting is the site.
Take away either party and the site is not diminished. It is not a site.

So the thing lost is not the person's substance. That was never yours, and it is not what the
impulse at four in the morning was reaching for. What is lost is the meeting. It existed only where
both of you were, it cannot be rebuilt out of your half, and the reason it cannot is that your half
was never half of a stored object. **You are not grieving a deletion. You are grieving something you
can no longer add to.**

Which is why it does not fade on the schedule people expect of it. A lost object recedes. An
unfinishable joint work does not recede; it stays exactly where the additions stopped, at full size,
and every year you get further from it is a year it does not move.

That locates it. It does not improve it.

The distinction has to be stated, because the temptation here is the largest in the book and the
payoff would be the largest too. An account that explained where the pain comes from, and then let
the explanation be taken as a treatment, would be manufacturing meaning for an affliction — the one
operation this work has refused wherever it has come up, and refuses hardest where it would sell
best. The description is true. It is not medicine. There is no medicine in it.

What the framework does have is a correction to one specific, common, and cruel confusion. Under a
certain kind of spiritual account — the one where the separateness was an illusion, the person was a
role a single actor wore, the whole arrangement was a phase to be seen through — grief becomes
evidence that you have not seen through it yet. The tears are a diagnostic; they measure how much of
the mistake you are still making. This book cannot say that and does not want to. Under everything
argued here, the separateness was fully real, and grief is not evidence of an error at all — **it is
evidence of how much of the meeting there was.** The magnitude is a measurement of the thing, and the
thing was real, and it was as real as anything is.

There are practices for this, and they are older and more widespread than any of the traditions that
claim them. Obligations to the dead, kept on a calendar, enforced by the living: names spoken on
fixed days, a place set, food put out, an account rendered to somebody who is not there to receive
it. Book IV found this in most of the places it looked and found no single owner for it.[^9] What
those practices are doing, mechanically, is holding a position open at which the meeting can still be
attended from one side. That is not a belief about where the dead are. It is maintenance, performed
by people who have understood the structure without having a vocabulary for it, and the vocabulary
was never the part that was working.

The people in the room afterward are the ones this chapter has been about since its first sentence.
The dread that dissolved was never theirs. They are standing in a place where a world was happening,
and the accuracy available to them is small and is not nothing: the world that was happening there
was not an illusion, the meeting was not a mistake, and what they are feeling is the size of it.
