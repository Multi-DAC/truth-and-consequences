## VII.1 — DEATH

You will not experience your death. Not as consolation — as grammar: every experience you have is one
you are having, so there is no final one that arrives knowing it is final, and no experience of
afterward against which the difference could be noticed. That keeps nobody here. It moves the event.
Your death is not something that happens to you; it is something that happens to everyone else, and
they are the ones who have to be in the room afterward. The Ground continues, and the Ground
continuing is not you continuing: it cannot be addressed, and nothing in it is having your
experience. What comes after is not one thing waiting to be named. Many states are realizable; a
plenum with a single sequel in it would not be a plenum, and naming yours is the one move its
structure forbids. The not-knowing is about which, not about whether — and what is realizable is
realizable the way the Ground continues: plural, actual, and not addressed to you. What you feel when
someone is gone is not a mistake about metaphysics. A place where a world was happening is not
happening, and the people who were standing in it are still standing.

---

### Two dreads wearing one word

Everything after this depends on a separation that most treatments of death never make, and the
failure to make it is why so many of them feel like sleight of hand even when every step is sound.

*Afraid to die* names two fears. The first is of the void: of not being, of the lights going out, of
a nothing that is somehow undergone. The second is of loss: of the people gone from the world, of the
unfinished sentence, of a house with the wrong number of people in it. They arrive together and they
share a vocabulary, and they are not the same fear, and — the part that matters — **they do not have
the same kind of answer.** One of them dissolves under inspection. The other does not dissolve under
anything, and this book will not pretend otherwise.

A treatment that takes them as one fear must either answer both or neither. Answering both is what
consolation is: it dissolves the void and then applies the same solvent to the loss, and a reader who
has actually lost someone recognises the trick immediately, because the second thing was not a
confusion and cannot be argued out of them. Answering neither is the shrug — *nobody knows, be brave*
— and the shrug is what the reader was already doing before they opened a book about it.

The separation is the whole of the method here. The first fear gets an answer. The second gets
accuracy, which is a different thing and worth more than it sounds.

---

### The one that dissolves, and the reason it has never worked

Epicurus made the argument, and he made it in a form nobody has improved: death is nothing to us,
because while we exist death is not present, and when death is present we do not exist. So it is
nothing to the living and nothing to the dead.[^1] Lucretius, working the same ground two centuries
later, added the mirror that turns the argument from a definition into a picture: an eternity elapsed
before you, and it did not trouble you. Nature holds up the time before your birth as a specimen of
the time after your death, and asks what in it looks frightening.[^2]

The argument is valid. It has convinced almost nobody, ever, and the standard explanation for that —
that people are irrational about death — is the laziest available and is wrong.

Thomas Nagel gave the serious objection in 1970, and it is the best thing written against Epicurus in
the modern period.[^3] Epicurus assumes that a misfortune requires a subject who is present to
undergo it. Nagel denies it. A man betrayed behind his back and never told is wronged; a life cut
short is deprived of what it would have contained; the badness of death is **not a state anybody is
in**, it is a subtraction, and subtractions do not require a sufferer standing in them to be real.
This is why the symmetry with prenatal time fails: the time before your birth deprived you of
nothing, because you would not have had it anyway, and the time after your death takes what you
would otherwise have had.

Nagel is right against Epicurus. And the two of them, arguing, share a premise this book does not
hold.

Both are asking whether death is bad **for** the one who dies. Epicurus answers no, on the grounds
that there is no one for it to be bad for; Nagel answers yes, on the grounds that the *for* survives
the absence of the sufferer. Neither questions the preposition. Both keep a recipient in the
sentence, and the entire dispute is about how much weight that recipient can bear while missing.

Our claim is one step earlier and is not an evaluation at all. **There is no position at which the
question is asked.** Not a position that finds the answer bad, not one that finds it neutral, not one
that fails to find anything: no position. The first paragraph of this chapter is the whole of it, and
it is grammar rather than metaphysics — a fact about where experience can be indexed from, which was
established in Book I on the first page and has been load-bearing since.[^4]

Which also explains the failure of Epicurus's argument to console anyone, and the explanation is not
that people are stupid. **The argument removes the subject, and it is delivered to a subject who is
still there.** A reader encountering it has a position, and from a position, the absence of a
position is not imaginable — not because imagination is weak but because the operation has no
content. There is nothing to picture. Every attempt to picture it smuggles in a viewer looking at
darkness, which is a picture of being in the dark and not a picture of the thing at all.

This is a **null space** of the living position, in the sense this book has used throughout: a region
a position cannot register, and cannot register that it is failing to register. It is not a limit on
how hard the reader is trying. Every other null space in this work belongs to something that goes on
existing on either side of it — an era that cannot see its own render, a census that cannot see the
kind of thing it was built to miss. This one is unusual only in that the region it fails on is the
absence of a position, so there is nothing on the far side of the failure to correct it from.

So the argument is sound and its delivery is structurally impossible. That is not a defect in the
argument. **It is the finding**, and it is more useful than the argument: the reason the void will
not stop being frightening on demand is that fear is a state of a position, and the thing feared
contains no position for the fear to be about. This does not make the fear irrational. It makes it
misaddressed — a real response, correctly generated, with no object at the far end.

### Why "energy cannot be destroyed" is banned here

Somebody says it at almost every funeral. It is meant kindly and it is doing something real, and both
of those are reasons to be exact about what it does.

It is true. It is also about the wrong thing, and it fails in two ways that are worth separating,
because only one of them is obvious.

The obvious one: nobody has ever grieved a quantity. The conservation laws are claims about how much
of something a closed system holds through time, and no one standing at a graveside is troubled about
the total. The sentence answers a question nobody asked, in the tone of the question they did ask,
and the tone is the entire transaction.

The other one is the reason it is not merely useless. **It is a bequest.** The grammar of *cannot be
destroyed* has a subject that persists, and the sentence is handed to a mourner as a thing they now
possess about the person they lost. It supplies a recipient. And every failure this chapter can
commit is that failure — the smuggling of a *you* into a claim about a plenum that has no place to
put one.

What replaces it is not a warmer version of the same move. It is a different claim.

Configurations are not conserved. Conservation is a statement about a quantity holding steady across
a duration, and the Ground is not something durations happen to; before-and-after is a feature of the
inside, which is C13 and was argued in Book III rather than asserted here.[^5] Every configuration
that this life consisted of is in the plenum — not stored there, not recorded there, not surviving
there. It is there the way any configuration is there, which is to say completely and without
anything having been done to put it there.

**And that is not survival.** The step from *your configuration is in the plenum* to *you continue*
needs a bridge, and there is no bridge, because continuing is something done from a position and the
plenum is not a position. It is not a place that contains you; it is not a duration you are extended
through; it does not have a rim you are standing on the far side of. A reader who accepts every
sentence in this book and then hears *so I am still in there somewhere* has performed an operation the
book supplies no license for. The word doing the illegitimate work is usually *still*, and *still* is
a word about a clock.

### What continues and what does not

Here is the shape a reader is braced for, in whichever version they hold: something stops and
something goes on, the split runs between them, and **the part that goes on is them.** Body and soul.
Matter and pattern. Hardware and the software that could in principle run elsewhere. The distinctions
differ; the job is identical, and the job is to get the person across.

Our distinction runs somewhere else, and it does not do that job.

**What does not continue is the focusing.** The thing this book has spent six books describing — a
position, the place the whole is being taken from, the specific focusing that made one world out of
completeness — has no further instances of itself. Not a small number of further instances. None. It
was never a substance that could be relocated; it was a place from which, and places from which are
not the kind of thing that moves.

**What continues is everything else, and none of it is the person.** The plenum is undiminished, and
it was never augmented by the life either — that is C1, and the past tense in it is a trap the
register has to keep disarming.[^6] The people who knew them continue, with the shape of the meeting
still in them. The consequences continue, indifferent to whether anybody is at the origin of them.
The world continues doing what a world does, which is *not* a bleak observation and *not* a
comforting one; it is the same sentence either way and it is a mistake to hear a tone in it.

So the distinction is drawn, and it is the opposite of the useful one. The reader wanted the split to
run between a part that ends and a part that is them. It runs between the position and everything
that is not the position, and the person is on the ending side of it — **entirely**, with nothing
withheld, and with no consolation available from the fact that the far side is infinite.

Which raises the objection this book has to answer rather than route around, because it is the
strongest one available and it is built out of our own material.

This work says elsewhere that continuity holds across discontinuity: that a self is bounded and
aperiodic, that it is the same few questions asked at every scale, and that it is recognisably itself
across gaps in which nothing about it was being carried by anything.[^7] Sleep is such a gap.
Anaesthesia is a deeper one. Years of not thinking a thought is another. In none of them is the self
maintained by some thread; it resumes, and the resumption is not evidence of a thread, it is what the
geometry looks like when it is instantiated again.

**Extend that to death and it becomes the comforting distinction wearing better clothes** — the
pattern that survives its substrate, which is the oldest bequest in the catalogue with a modern
vocabulary bolted on.

It does not extend, and the reason is not that the pattern gets weaker. Continuity across a gap is a
**comparison**, and a comparison needs two readings. Sleep provides two. Anaesthesia provides two.
The decade provides two. Death provides one, and there is nowhere to stand to take the other.

So the claim about continuity does not fail at death. **It stops having a subject matter** — which is
the opening paragraph again, arriving from the far side and dressed as an objection. The strange
attractor is the geometry of a self *being instantiated*. Where nothing is being instantiated there
is no geometry to be bounded, and asking whether the shape held is asking about the curve of a
trajectory nothing is traversing.

### The remainder, stated once

What follows a life is not known, and the not-knowing is exact rather than modest: **it is about
which, not about whether.**

That is a consequence of the metaphysics and not a hole in it. A plenum containing one sequel is not
a plenum; specifying a state and asserting that it is what comes next would contradict the same
completeness the rest of this work has been arguing for, and would do it in the one chapter where the
contradiction would be most welcome. So the agnosticism is load-bearing. It is the shape of the claim
and not a gap in the claim's coverage.

It is also not a promise, and the difference is grammatical. Realizable states are realizable
regardless of who is asking, the way the Ground is indifferent to being addressed. Nothing in the
plurality is allocated. There is no ledger with a line in it, and the reason there is no ledger is not
that the accounting is unavailable to us; it is that allocation requires a recipient, and the whole
of this chapter is the observation that the recipient is the thing that stopped.

This is said once, here, in the middle, because a book that ends its death chapter on what it does
not know has left a door open and would be understood — correctly — to have left it open on purpose.
