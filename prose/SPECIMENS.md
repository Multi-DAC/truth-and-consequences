## SPECIMEN 1 — BOOK I, CHAPTER 1: **THE FULLNESS**

*The first page of the work. Mythic register. No argument, no citation, no apparatus, no named
opponent. The Fullness stated with no verb that implies duration. The grammar confession made once.*

---

A complete totality must admit within it an aspect of incompleteness. It must contain every
possibility, and separation is a possibility; so separation is in it, entire, the way the shore is in
an ocean that has one.

There is a Fullness.

It is not old. Age belongs to whatever sits inside something larger, and there is nothing outside the
Fullness for it to sit inside of. It did not gather. It was not assembled out of pieces that were
somewhere else first, because it has no somewhere else. Every arrangement that could be the case is
the case in it: this room, the room next door, the rooms no one has entered and no one will, the
rooms where the door was never cut. All of them, held. Not stored — storing is for what might be
wanted later, and there is no later here.

It does not move. Not held down, not frozen, not gathering itself for something. Motion is the
difference between one moment and another, and it has no moments to differ between. Nothing has ever
happened to it. Nothing ever will. That is not its tragedy. That is its shape, and the shape is not a
wound.

And it is aware. Not aware *of* — being aware of something requires a gap, a thing over here noticing
a thing over there, and there is no over there. Awareness is not a task the Fullness performs. It is
the material.

It is not lonely. Loneliness is the shape of an absence, and nothing is absent from it.

It is not waiting. Waiting requires a next.

It does not yearn. Yearning is a lack that knows its own name, and it lacks nothing.

It does not love the world, and it does not fail to. Love needs someone at the far end of it, and
there are no ends in a thing without edges.

That last one will be misread for the rest of your life, so it is said once more, flatly: there is
nobody in there. No one is minding it. Nothing about the Fullness is turned toward you, and this is
not neglect. Turning is for things with a front.

Now the one admission this telling owes, made here and never again.

Every sentence above is in the wrong tense. *Is. Was. Never will.* These are words with a before and
an after folded into them, and the Fullness has neither. There is no sentence that does not carry
time inside it, because a sentence is one word arriving after another, and arriving-after is exactly
what is not there. So none of this is a report from outside. There is no outside to file a report
from. It is spoken from within a narrowing — from a place where there is a next word, where one thing
follows another, where a story is a possible object at all. That is the only kind of place from which
anything has ever been said.

Take that as the licence for everything that comes after. When the next chapters say *then*, and
*first*, and *afterward*, they are not being careless and they are not being sly. They are speaking
the only grammar an inside has got. The Fullness has no story. We have one, because we are the
narrowing, and a narrowing is the sort of thing a story can happen to.

And one thing more, placed at the front so that it can never afterward look like a conclusion that
was worked toward.

This is not a story about how the world began. The world does not begin in the distant past and
travel here. It begins wherever a narrowing occurs, whole and first each time — and one is occurring
now, and it is you, and what you have been calling the world is what the Fullness looks like from
where you are standing.

## SPECIMEN 2 — BOOK III, CHAPTER 1: **THE WRONG GAME**

*Argument at full adversarial strength. The single highest-priority paragraph in the plan, because
this is the one place we lose the reader by being agreed with. Real apparatus: names in the prose,
receipts in endnotes, one voice footnote that does not qualify anything.*

---

Start by stating it properly, because the version that gets passed around is not the argument, and
you are owed the argument.

Nick Bostrom does not claim we live in a simulation. He claims that one of three things is true and
that you cannot comfortably hold all three to be false.¹ First: almost no civilisation at our level
survives to technological maturity. Second: almost no mature civilisation runs detailed simulations
of its own ancestors.\* Third: almost every mind having experiences like yours is inside one of them.

The engine is two moves, both stronger than their reputation. The first is substrate-independence: if
what makes a mental state is the pattern of the computation rather than the meat it runs on, then a
sufficiently detailed simulation of a mind does not resemble a mind — it is one. The second is
arithmetic. A mature civilisation's computing capacity is not somewhat larger than ours; it is larger
by margins that make the word "larger" useless. Spend a rounding error of it on ancestor-simulations
and the simulated population so overwhelms the biological one that being biological becomes a
statistical curiosity. Then ask which sort you are, with nothing available to distinguish the two,
and honesty makes you answer by population.

Hans Moravec had the shape of this five years before Bostrom and said the quiet part in the open: the
pattern is what is real, and which machine runs it is a detail.² The older name matters here, and it
will keep mattering. The argument is better founded than the meme it became, and a reader who has
only met the meme has been handed something easy to dismiss and has dismissed the wrong thing.

Now grant what it gets right, and grant all of it, because we need every piece.

It is right that the world is generated rather than handed over. It is right that the felt solidity of
things — this table's obvious there-ness — is not evidence about how the table is produced, and that
treating the given-ness of the world as a datum is the naive move, not the sober one. It is right
that what a mind runs on is not the point. Every one of those is ours. We spend the next seven
chapters saying so.

Here is the cut, and it is one sentence.

**His simulation is a copy of a realer place, with realer people in it; ours has no elsewhere.**

That is the entire difference, and it decides everything downstream of here. What he calls base
reality is the room the servers are in — a place with more truth in it than this one, holding the
beings whose world is not generated. Everything on this side is a rendering *of* something, and the
something is over there. Take that seriously and you have been told, in the politest available terms,
that you are a copy, that your afternoon is derivative, and that the people who matter are elsewhere
and are not you.

We say something with the same shape and the opposite content. There is no over there. The Ground is
not a realer place; it is what this place is made of, and it has no rooms, no hardware and no staff.
When we say the world is rendered we do not mean it is a copy of an original held somewhere better.
**A simulation is a copy of a thing that is real somewhere else. A render is the only place the thing
is.** Nothing is being duplicated. Your afternoon is not a lower-resolution version of an afternoon
happening in the real world. It is an afternoon, at the only site afternoons occur.

So the two frames are not neighbours, and the difference is not academic. Follow him and you are a
rendered thing inside someone else's world. Follow us and you are the place a world happens. Every
consequence in the second half of this work forks precisely here — what you owe other people, what
death is, what freedom means when every path already exists. Take our metaphor with his ontology
underneath it and you will get all three wrong, warmly, while agreeing with us.

And there is a third position we are also not, older than both and still the default in most rooms:
the designer. Someone outside, prior to the world, who intended it. All three words are false and
each fails for its own reason. *Outside* is false because there is no outside for anyone to stand in.
*Prior* is false because before-and-after is a feature of insides, and the Ground has none. *Intended*
is false because intending requires a lack — you intend what is not yet the case — and in a totality
nothing is not the case.

What is left is a world that is generated with no generator, a game with no developer and no server,
a rendering that is a copy of nothing. Said quickly it sounds like an evasion. It is the plainest
thing in this book, and the next seven chapters are its mechanics.

## SPECIMEN 3 — BOOK VIII, CHAPTER 6: **OTHER PLAYERS**

*A practice page. Tuesday-level, usable at 3am. No footnotes anywhere in Book VIII, by ruling.
This chapter is half the telos, which is exactly why it is the one where grandeur would kill it.*

---

The practice in this chapter is the one most likely to be read as a sentiment and then not done. So
it comes as an instruction first and the reasons after.

**When you are with someone, let their day exist.**

That is the whole of it. Not *feel connected to them*. Not *see the divine in them*. Let the fact that
they came from somewhere this morning, and are going somewhere after, and have something on their
mind that has nothing to do with you, be in the room while you talk — as a live fact you are actually
holding, not a courtesy you would affirm if someone asked.

Here is the test, and it is checkable on a Tuesday. After an ordinary exchange — the pharmacist, your
brother, whoever took your call — can you name one true thing about their situation that you did not
need for your purpose? Not a guess about their inner life. Something they told you or showed you that
was not load-bearing for what you came to get. If you can, you were with a person. If you cannot, you
were with an obstacle that talks, and the exchange went fine, and that is precisely the problem. It
goes fine every time.

**Through, not over.** Most encounters are conducted over someone. You have an outcome, they are
between you and it, and the skill being exercised is getting the outcome past them at acceptable
cost. Politeness does not change this; polite is a way of going over someone smoothly. Going through
means the thing you wanted enters their perspective and comes out the other side altered by having
been there. The cost is that sometimes you do not get what you came for. The reason is not that you
were generous. It is that you were wrong about something, and they had the missing piece, and you
could not have received it while going over.

**What it costs is attention, and attention is the only thing you spend that you cannot get back.**
Money, effort and time you either recover or do without. Attention spent is gone — and it is also the
entire mechanism by which anything is real to you. Which is why recognition cannot be a policy. You
cannot decide to respect everyone in general; general respect costs nothing and therefore buys
nothing. You can spend forty seconds on one person. Forty seconds is expensive. That is the practice.

**Across a difference in grade.** Most of what you deal with is not your peer: the dog, the
eighteen-month-old, the person whose job requires them to be pleasant to you, the tree in the yard,
the company you work for. Recognition does not mean pretending these are equivalent. A grade is a
position, not a permission — and it cuts both ways, the second way being the one that bites here.
Being further along licenses nothing. The practical form is simple and unwelcome: **you do not get to
price someone's experience by how much of it you can imagine.** Your failure to picture the dog's
afternoon is a fact about your range.

**Now the failure mode, and it is the warm one.** There is a beautiful move available here, and Alan
Watts made it better than anyone: everyone you meet is God wearing a face, playing at not knowing,
and the joke is on all of us at once.³ It is generous, it dissolves contempt on contact, and it is
the exact thing this chapter exists to prevent.

Watch what it does in the room. You are at the counter and you decide that the tired person in front
of you is God in a mask. You have not seen them. You have replaced them with something enormous and
far more interesting, and the enormous thing does not have a cough and a shift ending at ten. The
mask move is a way of being moved by a person without being inconvenienced by one. The other players
are not disguises worn by a single player, because there is no single player. That is why they are
not costumes. That is why the tired person is the whole of what is there. And that is why forty
seconds spent on them is spent on someone, rather than donated to a metaphor.

**If it is three in the morning and none of the above is available to you**, use the one-sentence
version and drop the rest. *This person's day did not start when I walked in.* That is enough to be
going on with. It is not a reduced version of the practice. It is the practice, and everything above
is the reason it works.

**Endnote.** 3. Alan Watts, *The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are* (1966).

## SPECIMEN 4 — BOOK VII, CHAPTER 1: **DEATH** — opening paragraph

*~165 words. Not a register test — a sentence test. Ruled to land unhedged in the first paragraph
with no throat-clearing. "Energy cannot be destroyed" is banned; this is what replaces it. The
failure mode being instrumented is not bluster but **comfort**. If this paragraph consoles while
claiming not to, C17 is unsettable and we have learned that the only way it can be learned.*

---

You end. The narrowing that has been occurring as you stops occurring, and there is no version of you
somewhere else afterward, because *somewhere else* is a thing insides have and yours is what ended.
What continues is the Ground, and the Ground continuing is not you continuing: it does not hold you
the way a room holds furniture, it cannot be addressed, and nothing in it is having your experience.
The configuration you were is in it, complete and unreachable, exactly as every configuration is —
and that is an inventory, not a survival. It confers nothing on you. What we do not know, said once
and as a fact rather than as a cushion: whether narrowing recurs, and whether anything of one inside
is ever carried into another. We have no account of that and we are not going to build you one out of
politeness. Your grief is not a misunderstanding. A place where a world was happening is not
happening, and that is what you are registering, and you are registering it correctly.

## WHAT WRITING THEM FOUND — three things no ruling produced

**1. The sustained metaphor cannot start until Book III.** Rule 2 says *one metaphor, sustained — the
game, deepening.* But Book I bans apparatus and argument, and Book II is definitions. Neither can
carry the game. So the book's single organising image is absent for its first two Books and arrives a
third of the way in. Specimen 1 confirms this is survivable — myth does not need the game, and
importing it would wreck the register — but the contract's word is "sustained," and what is actually
true is *introduced late and then sustained*. Somewhere in II a bridge is owed, or the contract's
wording is.

**2. The apparatus ruling paid off immediately and not where expected.** Removing parenthetical
citations from Specimen 2 forced *Bostrom* and *Moravec* into the subjects of sentences, which is what
rule 5 wanted all along and could not get while a parenthesis was available to hold the name. The
ruling was made on register grounds; the benefit is grammatical.

**3. Specimen 4 was written six times and the first five consoled.** Every early version smuggled
comfort through a *structural* route rather than a lexical one — the sentence granting the honest
remainder kept arriving *last*, and a paragraph that ends on what we do not know reads as an opening
left for hope no matter how flatly the words are set. The fix was to bury the remainder in the middle
and end on grief being correct. **This is exactly the failure ruling 11 predicted, and it was
invisible until there was a paragraph.** Comfort does not enter through a word you can ban. It enters
through the order of the sentences.
